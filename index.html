Bitget Says a Flaw in a Third-Party Security Product Handed Over the Credentials, as Withdrawals Reopen

The exchange's chief executive says private keys and cold wallets were never touched. High-level internal credentials were — and they were enough to issue fraudulent withdrawal commands. Bitcoin withdrawals returned on Monday, Ether is scheduled for Tuesday and USDT for Wednesday. The attacker is still moving the money.

By The Daily Cryptograph Staff | September 28, 2026

Bitget chief executive Gracy Chen said the exchange's $388 million exploit stemmed from a vulnerability in a third-party security product, which allowed the attacker to obtain what she called "high-level internal credentials." Those credentials, she told Cointelegraph, were then used to issue fraudulent withdrawal commands. Bitget's private keys were not compromised and its cold wallets were not affected — a distinction the exchange has now stated twice, and one that separates this incident from the key-compromise failures of previous cycles.

The exchange says it has addressed the flaw and tightened its withdrawal controls: internal access has been restricted, independent verification has been added for withdrawals, and monitoring for unusual activity increased. The attack itself occurred on Sept. 24, when Bitget detected unauthorised transfers from several of its hot wallets and suspended withdrawals; the exchange initially put the affected assets at about $352 million and revised the figure to $387.5 million after accounting for further transfers on Zcash and TRON. Part of its hot and warm wallet infrastructure was compromised; the cold side was not.

Service is returning in stages. Bitget resumed Bitcoin withdrawals on Monday — on the Bitcoin network and on BNB Smart Chain first, Chen said during a Monday ask-me-anything session — with further assets and networks to follow over the coming days; Ether withdrawals are scheduled to return Tuesday and USDT on Wednesday. The attacker, meanwhile, continues moving stolen crypto through THORChain. Bitget has not disclosed how much of the money has been recovered or frozen: Chen said some assets have been frozen with help from other industry participants, but that a total would be released only once the amounts are verified.

Fund Shares That Keep Paying While They Secure the Loan

Tokenization September 28, 2026

Franklin Templeton has partnered with Bybit on a programme that lets institutional investors use tokenized shares of its money market funds as trading collateral — taking the tokens beyond buy-and-hold and into the plumbing of margin. Eligible clients can pledge fund shares issued through Franklin Templeton's Benji platform while the assets stay in off-exchange custody, and in return draw credit lines denominated in USDT or USDC to trade on Bybit. The shares need neither be sold nor moved onto the exchange.

The point is yield that does not stop. Institutions keep earning on the money market fund holdings while those same holdings finance a crypto position — the classic collateral-efficiency argument for tokenization, applied to a product that has been tokenized for years and a use for it that has not existed before. The two firms also say they are planning a tokenized investment product for wallet users on Bybit and the Mantle network, though details have not been disclosed. Demand for tokenized money market funds has grown quickly: the Bank for International Settlements valued the market at more than $9 billion as of September 2025.

Eleven Million Down, Thirty Pledged, and One Ohio Senate Seat

Politics September 28, 2026

A political action committee aligned with and largely funded by the crypto industry reported spending more than $11 million on advertising supporting Republican Jon Husted and opposing Democrat Sherrod Brown in the race for one of Ohio's seats in the US Senate. In a Thursday filing with the Federal Election Commission, Defend American Jobs — affiliated with the Coinbase- and Ripple Labs-backed group Fairshake — reported $11.4 million of media spending on the contest.

The buys appear to be the opening tranche of Fairshake's pledge to spend $30 million in Ohio, aimed at blocking the return of Brown, who previously chaired the Senate Banking Committee and was among the more outspoken voices in Congress against the industry. The precedent is not encouraging for anyone hoping for a cheaper race: in 2024 the Ohio Senate contest between Brown and Republican Bernie Moreno drew more than $300 million of outside spending, with FEC records showing the crypto-aligned PAC and its affiliates alone put up about $41 million to oppose Brown and support Moreno. Moreno won with 50.2% to Brown's 46.4%.

A Thousand Six Hundred Sixty-Five Coins, Bought Above the Company's Own Average

Corporate September 28, 2026

Strategy bought 1,665 Bitcoin for $142.7 million last week, lifting its holdings to 847,666 BTC. The company paid an average of $85,681 per coin between Monday and Sunday, according to a Sept. 28 filing with the SEC. Across everything it has ever bought, it has now spent $63.95 billion at an average price of $75,437 per Bitcoin, including fees and expenses — so last week's purchases were made well above the lifetime average that the company's disclosures have turned into a fixed point of the treasury-company story.

The funding was the usual circular arrangement, executed with slightly different arithmetic. Strategy sold 1.47 million common shares for $246.2 million in net proceeds, allocating $142.7 million to Bitcoin and $103.5 million to repurchasing its STRC preferred stock. It bought back 1.53 million STRC shares for $151.7 million, with a further $48.1 million coming from dollar cash holdings, which fell to $1 billion from $1.05 billion. A separate dollar reserve kept for preferred dividends and debt interest slipped to $5.02 billion from $5.04 billion after $22.1 million of dividend payments. The week before, after a two-week pause, the company bought 950 BTC for $75.7 million.

Liquidity Appears at $85,700, and the Bid Leaves With It

Markets September 28, 2026

Bitcoin fell to one-week lows on Monday, dropping below $82,700 for the first time since Sept. 21, as upward momentum gave way to order-book liquidity moves. After posting its highest weekly close since late January — about $84,450 — the pair failed to mount another test of the eight-month highs above $87,000 reached last week. Instead, more than $30 million of ask liquidity appeared clustered around $85,700; conspicuous liquidity at a single price level often signals a calculated attempt by large traders to influence direction, and spot accelerated its decline as the cluster appeared. CoinGlass data showed the drop liquidating nearby longs — roughly $70 million over 24 hours at the time of writing.

The larger frame is a quarter that is nearly over and still generous. Bitcoin is coiling below its 2026 opening level days before the Q3 candle closes, with quarter-to-date gains still above 40% — on track to be its best third quarter in nine years. The week brings US inflation data and a geopolitical bid for volatility, after crypto joined US stock futures in falling on the potential for fresh US strikes on Iran. A market that has risen this far into a data week is a market with something to lose.

✦ The Last Fork Anyone Would Recognise: Ethereum's Turn Toward a Cryptographic World Computer ✦

Vitalik Buterin says Ethereum's Hegotá upgrade could be the network's last "normal" fork, after which development turns toward recursive STARKs, automated formal verification, optimised consensus and quantum-safe cryptography. Hegotá, planned for 2027, is likely to be the final upgrade whose features and technology would still be recognisable to somebody who knew Ethereum in 2015, Buterin said on Sunday. The remark is less a roadmap than a boundary marker: everything before Hegotá belongs to the first era, and everything after belongs to a different kind of system.

The change began, in his account, with PeerDAS — the mechanism that lets nodes verify data availability by sampling portions of the data rather than downloading all of it. That marked Ethereum's transition "from being just a blockchain to being something much more powerful," he said. The destination he describes is a "cryptographic world computer," an architecture that combines blockchains with cryptographic verification, privacy and decentralised offchain components, rather than one in which every node downloads and re-executes every transaction.

It is a useful frame for reading the next two years of Ethereum argument. If upgrades stop being recognisable, then the debates that have organised the network's politics — block times, gas limits, the shape of the consensus — stop being the debates that matter, and the interesting questions become cryptographic ones that most holders have never had to price. Whether that makes Ethereum stronger or simply harder to govern is the question Hegotá will leave behind.

After the Rules, the Watching: MiCA Enters Its Supervision Phase

Regulation

The European Securities and Markets Authority will focus its coordination of national crypto supervision in 2027 on operational resilience, outsourcing and firms keeping sufficient operations inside the bloc, as implementation of the Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation deepens. Chair Verena Ross said Monday that the regulator's emphasis under MiCA had shifted "from rulemaking towards supervision and convergence." "We want innovation to flourish within a framework that provides clarity for firms, safeguards for investors and confidence in the markets," she told the European Parliament's economic affairs committee. ESMA's 2027 work programme, also published Monday, sets out closer coordination among national regulators supervising crypto asset service providers, with liquidity, reverse solicitation and asset classification among the areas in scope. The transition is the moment European crypto firms discover what their licences actually require.

Hong Kong Extends Audit and Reporting Oversight to Licensed Crypto Firms

Regulation

Hong Kong's Securities and Futures Commission and the Accounting and Financial Reporting Council signed a memorandum of understanding on Monday that extends their cooperation on financial reporting and auditing to licensed crypto businesses. The agreement covers financial and compliance reporting by SFC-licensed virtual asset service providers, licensed corporations, registered open-ended fund companies and authorised funds, along with the related audit and assurance work. It also establishes a framework for information sharing, case referrals, mutual assistance and coordinated inspections and investigations, replacing a 2021 agreement signed with the body then known as the Financial Reporting Council. SFC chair Kelvin Wong said the expanded cooperation would deliver "more comprehensive oversight" across a wider range of entities and activities. For licensed platforms, the practical effect is that the numbers they publish now have a second regulator reading them.

Four Times Its Peg, and a Regulator Rethinking Market Makers

Asia

South Korea's Financial Services Commission says it is considering a market-making regime for digital assets, after a Japanese yen-backed stablecoin traded at as much as four times its peg on a major domestic exchange. Upbit opened trading in JPYC on Sept. 17, with the market opening at 12 Korean won and reaching 37.6 won an hour later — a function, observers said, of thin liquidity rather than of the asset. "There were also criticisms that user losses occurred from the price surge after the JPYC listing, so demands for discipline in this area are expanding," said Yoo Young-joon, director of digital finance policy at the FSC. The obstacle is legal as much as practical: the country's Virtual Asset User Protection Act contains no carve-out for market making from its market-manipulation provisions, which is precisely why market makers have stayed away and why thin books have become the norm. Researchers had warned of the liquidity gap long before JPYC arrived.

A Fake Mainnet, a Real Two Million Dollars

Security

Scammers stole about $2 million in Ether through a bridge for a fake GIWA blockchain that a decentralised exchange initially mistook for the project's mainnet. In a reconstruction published Monday, DYORSWAP said the fraudulent bridge received about 767.65 ETH from 1,335 addresses, of which the scammers drained 766.25 ETH. GIWA — an Ethereum layer-2 developed by Dunamu, the operator of Upbit — said on Sunday that its mainnet had not launched and warned that purported mainnet connection details circulating online were false. "We do not have our mainnet running currently," the project wrote. The episode is a reminder that in the gap between a project's announcement and its deployment, the only thing a user has to check against is the project's own denial.

A Month of Blocks Undone, and the Payments the Rollback Cannot Reach

Ledger

Zano has rolled back approximately a month of blockchain history after a vulnerability involving Gateway Addresses allowed unauthorised ZANO and Freedom Dollar into circulation. The chain restarted at block 3,833,000 — immediately before Hard Fork 6, the upgrade that introduced the affected feature — and recovery requires nodes, miners, stakers, exchanges and other services to adopt the update. A month of legitimate transactions is invalidated along with the unauthorised tokens, and payments already settled on other blockchains cannot be unwound. "Doing nothing meant unauthorized ZANO and fUSD in circulation without limit, diluting every holder and breaking the most basic promise a currency makes: a fixed supply," said Zano's head of marketing and growth, Quinten van Welzen. "It would also tell every future attacker that exploited coins get to keep their value." The team has promised a reimbursement and claims process.

Two Point Four Billion, the Best Week for the Funds Since 2025

Markets

US spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds recorded their strongest week of inflows this year, drawing $2.39 billion after adding $134.5 million on Friday. The weekly total was the largest since the week ending Oct. 10, 2025, when net inflows reached $2.71 billion, according to SoSoValue, and it surpassed the previous 2026 high of $1.92 billion set in August. Year-to-date flows rose to about $926 million on Friday, recovering from a deficit that had reached roughly $5.55 billion in early July. Daily figures tell the more cautious story: inflows slowed after approaching $1 billion on Monday, while Bitcoin retreated from above $87,100. The funds bought the dip for a month and then, in the last several sessions, watched it keep dipping — which is roughly the position of everyone else who is long.

A Governor's Pen Barring Officials From Issuing Memecoins

Policy

California Governor Gavin Newsom signed legislation on Sunday barring state and local public officials from issuing memecoins, while criticising President Donald Trump's crypto ventures. Assembly Bill 2409, introduced by Assembly Member Avelino Valencia in February, also prohibits digital asset service providers from offering certain official-linked memecoins to California residents, and applies to tokens issued on or after Jan. 1, 2027. "No official should profit off their office — and we're putting stronger protections in place to ensure it doesn't happen in our state," Newsom said. Enforcement falls to the attorney general, a district attorney, a city attorney or county counsel by civil action. The governor also signed Senate Bill 1208, extending the state's money-laundering statutes to illicit digital asset transactions and authorising police to freeze, seize and forfeit assets tied to crimes.

Privacy for Bitcoin Without Asking Miners to Change a Rule

Privacy

Researchers at the cryptography firm Alloc Init have proposed Shielded Bitcoin, a system for Zcash-style private transfers on Bitcoin that requires no soft fork. It would conceal amounts, senders, receivers and the links to previously spent funds using encrypted notes and zero-knowledge proofs, treating Bitcoin as "a neutral publication and ordering layer" while separate indexers verify the proofs, check for double-spends and rebuild the shielded state. The paper was published by Clara Shikhelman, Mikhail Komarov and Aleksei Moskvin. Developer Vadim Zavodil argued on X that much of the privacy stack already exists in Zcash and questioned how much privacy a new pool could offer without that network's accumulated anonymity set — a fair objection, and a temporary one.

Lightning Is Added to the Protocol Built for Paying Machines

Infrastructure

Block has joined the x402 Foundation and added Bitcoin Lightning support to the payment protocol, extending its push into payments for artificial-intelligence agents. The company said last week that Lightning suits the low-cost, high-volume traffic agentic commerce will depend on — the same conclusion that has so far made stablecoins the default unit of account for machine payments. "Bringing Lightning to x402 is a concrete step toward making Bitcoin everyday money for people and the agents acting on their behalf, and we're excited to help the industry build on it," said Steve Lee, who heads Block's Bitcoin development initiative, Spiral. Whether agents settle in bitcoin or in dollars will be decided by fee curves rather than by sentiment.
