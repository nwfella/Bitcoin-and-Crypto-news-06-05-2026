Regulation United States — Minnesota

Minnesota Crypto ATM Ban Goes Into Effect After Reported $1M Losses

Minnesota's statewide prohibition on virtual-currency kiosks took effect Saturday, two weeks after Governor Tim Walz signed the law in May — making the state the second to enforce a total ban after Tennessee's July 1 crackdown. Under SF 3868, operators are barred from installing, operating or maintaining crypto ATMs, and companies with machines already in the field must deactivate them by today, with physical removal required by Dec. 31.

The state commerce department reported that Minnesota residents — largely senior citizens — lost about $1 million to crypto-kiosk scams from 2023 to 2025, while the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center logged more than $151 million in 2025 losses tied to digital assets or wallets across the state. Officials said the schemes "disproportionately target seniors," often pressuring victims to send money quickly amid fake emergencies. CoinATMRadar counted 201 crypto ATMs operating in Minnesota before the ban. Georgia's transaction-limit law took effect the same day as Tennessee's, and Delaware and New Jersey lawmakers have advanced similar bills.

Research Stablecoins

Bank of Italy Finds No Consistent Cost Advantage for Stablecoin Remittances

A Bank of Italy study of 200 USDC remittances across 10 payment corridors linking Italy with Brazil, Argentina, Japan, the UAE and South Africa found stablecoin transfers offered no systematic cost or speed advantage over traditional rails — because fiat on- and off-ramp frictions absorb most of the expense and delay. End-to-end costs ranged from 0.3% to nearly 9% depending on corridor, with settlement under 20 minutes where instant payment systems exist and one to two business days elsewhere.

Against the World Bank's global average remittance cost of 6.65%, stablecoins were cheaper in most corridors examined, though less expensive than Wise in only three of seven comparable routes. The authors argue the advantage would grow substantially if stablecoins could be spent directly in the real economy without reconversion to fiat.

Regulation Mining — Russia

Russia Expands Crypto Mining Ban to Moscow Through 2032

Russia has added Moscow, the Moscow Region and parts of Kursk Region to its cryptocurrency mining ban list, with restrictions taking effect Aug. 15, 2026 and running through Dec. 31, 2032. Resolution No. 936, signed by Prime Minister Mikhail Mishustin on July 25, amends the December 2024 order and also covers eight municipal districts and the city of Lgov in Kursk — all areas facing electricity-supply strain.

The Moscow Region's energy ministry cited growing power demand, noting 65 data centers connected to the grid with combined capacity of 734 megawatts, including 19 regionally with 233 MW. Earlier restrictions remain in parts of Buryatia and Zabaykalsky Krai through March 2031.

✦ Analysis: TIPS Say the Bond Sell-Off Is Real — and That's Bad for Bitcoin ✦

The mainstream story blames rising Treasury yields on inflation from the Iran war and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz — WTI briefly topped $85 a barrel Thursday, and 30-year yields hit their highest since 2007 after the FOMC. But Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities tell a different tale: the five-year breakeven rate has fallen to ~2.2% and has been trending down since May, moving in the opposite direction of nominal yields. While the five-year nominal yield rose 33 basis points, TIPS data suggest an 84 bps rise in real yields, partially offset by a 51 bps decline in expected inflation.

The cause is likely a mix of reserve liquidation — Asian central banks selling Treasuries to defend currencies (the yen, peso and rupee have all required intervention) — plus demand destruction risk from a persistent oil shock and the AI sector's voracious demand for capital. Goldman projects roughly $755 billion of AI capex in 2026 and $920 billion in 2027; UBS raised its 2026 investment-grade issuance forecast to $1.8 trillion. With rates this high, government bonds are, for the first time since 2019, more profitable than crypto cash-and-carry trades, per Glassnode. Rising real returns make non-yielding assets like Bitcoin relatively less attractive — and a September rate hike is now priced at 63%.
