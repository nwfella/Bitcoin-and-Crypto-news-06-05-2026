Institutions Banking

BNY to Bring Transfer Agency Records Onchain in Blockchain Push

BNY, one of the world's largest custodian banks overseeing more than $59 trillion in assets, is launching a blockchain-based transfer agency platform that will move fund ownership records onchain. The New York-based institution's digital transfer agency covers approximately $8.6 trillion in assets across 7.6 million accounts. Early adopters include Baillie Gifford, which plans to use the platform for what it describes as the first "fully native" UK-regulated tokenized fund, alongside BlackRock and BNY Dreyfus. "We think of BNY as modernizing a function that sits behind every single fund transaction," said Carolyn Weinberg, BNY's chief product and innovation officer. The move follows BNY's European regulatory progress under the MiCA framework.

Robinhood Posts Record Quarter, But Crypto Revenue Slides 38%

Online brokerage Robinhood posted record second-quarter revenue of $1.31 billion, up 32% year-over-year, with net income rising 48% to $573 million. However, cryptocurrency transaction revenue fell 38% to $100 million from about $160 million a year earlier. The company recorded $40 billion in crypto trading volume during the quarter — $18 billion on the Robinhood app and $22 billion from Bitstamp, the crypto exchange it acquired in June 2025. Robinhood continued expanding its digital asset business, launching the Robinhood Chain mainnet, introducing tokenized US stocks in over 120 countries, and debuting its first decentralized lending product, Robinhood Earn. Nearly 100,000 customer accounts are now enrolled in Agentic Trading.

Exchanges Regulation

Binance.US to Attempt Prediction Markets Entry as CFTC-Licensed Entity

Cryptocurrency exchange Binance.US will apply for a Designated Contract Market license with the US Commodity Futures Trading Commission to offer prediction markets services, CEO Steve Gregory announced at the Rare Evo blockchain conference. The DCM license would allow Binance.US to compete with platforms like Kalshi and Polymarket. The company says it will file its application in August. Under CFTC guidelines, DCMs can legally trade futures or option contracts based on any underlying commodity, index or instrument. The application comes more than a year after the SEC dismissed its lawsuit against Binance, its US entity and former CEO Changpeng Zhao.

Regulation MiCA

Hungary Repeals Crypto Checks as First MiCA License is Granted

Hungary has rolled back its mandatory third-party approval requirement for certain crypto transactions, reversing a policy that disrupted the country's digital asset market. The Hungarian Parliament voted to repeal the crypto validator requirement, with Finance Minister Kármán András stating that previous rules prompted many service providers to halt operations. CoinCash is now set to resume services after receiving authorization under the European Union's Markets in Crypto-Assets (MiCA) regulation, making it one of the first firms to be licensed under the landmark framework. "Due to the negative and market-shaking regulations so far, many players have terminated their services related to cryptocurrencies in Hungary, but the market is now showing signs of recovery," András wrote on Facebook.

Regulation Asia

South Korea Plans Stablecoin Rules as Opposition Pushes Crypto Tax Repeal

South Korea's Financial Services Commission plans to draft a consolidated Digital Asset Basic Act with the ruling Democratic Party, giving lawmakers a government-backed proposal covering stablecoins and the broader cryptocurrency market. The bill would cover stablecoin issuance and circulation, digital asset business rules, exchange entry requirements, disclosures, internal controls and system-resilience standards. Ten separate digital asset and stablecoin bills are already pending in Parliament, with key disputes remaining over whether won-denominated stablecoin issuers should be majority bank-owned and whether ownership limits should apply to major exchanges. The FSC has not finalized when the consolidated bill will be introduced.

Policy Stablecoins

UK Policy Sprint Finds Cross-Border Payments are Stablecoins' Top Use Case

The UK Financial Conduct Authority's Stablecoin Sprint has identified cross-border payments as the clearest near-term use case for stablecoins, particularly in emerging markets with limited access to US dollars. Industry participants said domestic UK consumers have little incentive to switch because existing payment methods are already fast and inexpensive, though merchants could benefit from lower costs and faster settlement. The findings informed the FCA's June 30 final rules requiring UK-issued stablecoins to be fully backed by reserve assets and redeemable at par. The regulator said feedback from the policy sprint — which brought together banks, payment firms, stablecoin issuers and other industry participants — will also shape its future policy on stablecoin payments.
