Markets The Institutional Deal

BitGo Acquires NYDIG Institutional Trading Business

BitGo has completed its acquisition of NYDIG's institutional trading business, absorbing the Bitcoin infrastructure firm's institutional client relationships and about 30 employees — financial terms were not disclosed. The acquired business provides derivatives, structured products, financing and capital markets services to asset managers, hedge funds and companies.

BitGo CEO Mike Belshe said the deal will "meaningfully scale" the company's trading and infrastructure capabilities, while NYDIG's head of financial infrastructure, Pete Janney, framed the sale as joining "an even deeper set of resources." For NYDIG, the divestiture clears the way to focus on power generation, Bitcoin mining and high-performance computing data centers, where its development pipeline exceeds 3 gigawatts, including more than 1 GW expected to come online in 2027 and 2028.

Treasury The Paris Accumulator

Capital B Raises $24.5M to Buy 270 Bitcoin

French Bitcoin treasury company Capital B raised €21.0 million ($24.5 million) through a private share placement at €0.58 per ABSA — one share bundled with four share-subscription warrants — with legendary cypherpunk and Blockstream CEO Adam Back and asset manager TOBAM participating.

The proceeds will fund the purchase of 270 Bitcoin, raising Capital B's total holdings to 3,415 BTC and ranking it the 29th-largest publicly traded Bitcoin treasury, behind Strategy's 843,775 BTC. If all issued warrants were exercised, the company would see a further €135.8 million ($158 million) capital injection — following a June shareholder vote, passed with 99.34% support, to allow up to €5 billion in capital increases.

Regulation The Golden State Ban

California Advances Bill to Ban Memecoin Issuance by Public Officials

California lawmakers passed Assembly Bill 2409 restricting public officials' involvement with memecoins, citing conflicts of interest and "pay-to-play arrangements." The Senate approved it 40-0 and the Assembly concurred 78-0; the bill now sits in the enrolled stage awaiting the governor's signature.

Under the measure, digital asset service providers could not offer California residents memecoins issued on or after Jan. 1, 2027 by federal public officials or state or local officers, with memecoins defined as assets whose value derives primarily from public interest, speculation or community engagement. The bill lands as investors in the Official Trump (TRUMP) memecoin sit an estimated $3.2 billion underwater, according to a Thursday Public Citizen report.

Politics The $4.7B Ledger

Trump Cost Investors $4.7B Through Crypto 'Schemes': Public Citizen

Nonprofit consumer advocacy group Public Citizen reported that President Donald Trump "left investors at least an estimated $4.7 billion underwater" since 2022 through the World Liberty Financial governance token, his NFT trading cards, the TRUMP memecoin and Trump Media's digital asset treasury.

The bulk of the losses — $3.2 billion — came from TRUMP memecoin buyers, which the group described as "wealth transferred to a small group of early buyers rather than money that simply vanished." Trump meanwhile earned $7.2 million in NFT licensing fees, more than $600 million from World Liberty token sales and an equity stake, and $635 million in memecoin licensing fees. Public Citizen renewed its call for ethics provisions in the CLARITY Act ahead of its Sept. 15 cloture vote.

Bitcoin The Node Alert

Core Lightning Confirms Vulnerabilities, Urges Node Upgrade

Core Lightning, an open-source implementation of Bitcoin's Lightning Network, confirmed that several of the high volume of AI-generated CVE reports it received are real vulnerabilities and urged node operators to install a forthcoming security update. As an interim measure, operators should restart with --offline — which blocks payments from entering, leaving or routing through the node — rather than shutting down entirely, so the daemon can still respond if a counterparty force-closes a channel. The flaws are separate from the remote denial-of-service vulnerabilities patched in May and July.

Security The Bridge Promise

The Sandbox Plans 1:1 Repayment After $700K Bridge Exploit

Blockchain gaming platform The Sandbox pledged to repay eligible SAND holders 1:1 after an Aug. 21 bridge exploit drained about 14.7 million SAND — worth roughly $700,000 — from an Ethereum vault. The attacker exploited a configuration flaw in SAND's Base and BNB Chain contracts to become the sole verifier of incoming bridge messages, though the more than 339 trillion unbacked tokens minted were isolated and cannot be bridged or redeemed. Claims open within two weeks, with two centralized exchanges holding over 72% of eligible balances distributing directly to customers.

GnosisDAO Approves Gnosis Chain for Ethereum Economic Zone

Digital Yuan Operator Count Hits 30 as China Adds 8 Banks

China's central bank added eight lenders — including Ping An Bank, Bank of Shanghai and China Bohai Bank — to its digital yuan network, bringing the number of e-CNY operating institutions to 30. Regulation The 86% Gap

CARF May Miss Most Onchain Crypto Tax Activity: Chainalysis

Potentially taxable onchain crypto activity reached at least $457 billion globally in 2025 — $112.6 billion in the US alone — yet transactions covered by the OECD's Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework (CARF) account for just 14% of it, per a new Chainalysis report. North America led all regions with $134.6 billion in estimated taxable activity, followed by the European Union at $125.1 billion, with the remaining 86% including activity CARF's reporting requirements don't fully capture.
