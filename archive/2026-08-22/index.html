Markets The $126,000 Question

Standard Chartered Says $100K Bitcoin Year-End Target May Be 'Too Low'

Bitcoin may move toward its all-time high of $126,000 before the end of the year, with the recovery potentially accelerating after Oct. 6, according to Geoff Kendrick, global head of digital asset research at Standard Chartered. In a Friday note, Kendrick said the latest rally has been driven largely by short liquidations while inflows into spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds have also started to recover. "For the first time this year there is now a risk my end year forecast (of USD100k) is too low," he wrote.

The note reverses a gloomy February, when Kendrick cut the year-end BTC target to $100,000 from $150,000 and the Ether target to $4,000 from $7,500, expecting Bitcoin to fall to around $50,000 first. Other watchers echo the turn: Swan Bitcoin CEO Cory Klippsten said Bitcoin may bottom in October, while 10x Research founder Markus Thielen said an August close above $63,000 could confirm the bear market is ending. Bitcoin traded near $76,844 at press time, up 24% over the past week.

Security The Seed Hardening

Coldcard Firmware Update Hardens Seed Generation After $112M Exploit

Coinkite released a security upgrade for its Coldcard hardware wallets that strengthens seed phrase generation by mixing user-supplied entropy with improved device randomness. Firmware 5.6.1 for the Coldcard Mk4 and Mk5 and 1.5.1Q for the Coldcard Q requires newly generated seeds to include input from at least 65 keypresses with unpredictable timing, 50 rolls of a six-sided die or 128 coin flips — combined with randomness from multiple device sources so private keys stay unpredictable even if one entropy source fails.

Coinkite urged users to upgrade immediately, stressing that existing seed phrases remain vulnerable even after upgrading and must be replaced with new seeds before migrating funds. Confirmed losses from the Coldcard exploit reached 1,778 BTC — about $112 million — per an Aug. 14 Galaxy Research report, making it the third-largest crypto exploit of 2026. The release also re-verifies transactions immediately before signing against a compromised USB port attack and adds hardware RNG checks plus a boot-time verification test.

Infrastructure The 350 Millisecond Era

Solana Cuts Blockchain Slot Time to 350 Milliseconds

Solana reduced its slot time to 350 milliseconds — the first such reduction since the network's genesis — according to Jacob Creech, vice president of technology at the Solana Foundation. "We're in a new era of 350ms. Next stop, 300ms," Creech said in a Friday post. Average slot times stood at 360ms at press time, down from the network's original 400ms target, per the Solana slot time explorer.

In June, the Foundation outlined plans to cut slot times from 400ms to 200ms to improve latency and accelerate confirmations, with three further 50ms reductions planned. All four stages are targeted for mainnet activation in Agave v4.2, the validator client developed by Anza, though the schedule remains tentative. The enabling proposal, SIMD-0525, was approved and merged on May 14.

Ethereum The Gas Warning

Ethereum Foundation Warns Tools May Break With Glamsterdam Upgrade

The Ethereum Foundation has warned that some wallets, indexers and gas estimators may break because of gas-model changes in the upcoming Glamsterdam upgrade. The Protocol DevOps team said any tool relying on a hardcoded maximum gas limit "will break" and must be updated, urging developers to test their systems on Plataberget, the public testnet launched Aug. 13 ahead of Glamsterdam's activation on Sepolia and Hoodi.

Under EIP-8037, a separate state-gas dimension is introduced for operations that create new state: a plain ETH transfer to an existing account still costs 21,000 gas, but sending ETH to a new account costs more. Developers should revisit software that assumes 21,000 gas covers every transfer or uses only one gas dimension when estimating costs. Glamsterdam also includes enshrined proposer-builder separation, block-level access lists and larger contract and initialization-code limits.

✦ Feature: MANTRA Chain Halted After Token Crashes to Record Low ✦

MANTRA's native token sank 18.5% from its 24-hour high to a record low shortly before MANTRA Chain stopped producing blocks, prompting the project to halt the network as a precaution. According to CoinGecko, OM fell from $0.005060 to an all-time low of $0.004126 around 11:00 pm UTC Thursday before recovering to about $0.0044 — still down roughly 10% over 24 hours, with trading volume climbing.

"We don't have a root cause or timeline to share yet," MANTRA said Friday, adding that the network had been paused over an "unexplained incident" while it investigates. The halt prevents assets from moving on MANTRA Chain and has prompted affected exchanges to pause deposits and withdrawals, with no timeline given for either service to resume.
