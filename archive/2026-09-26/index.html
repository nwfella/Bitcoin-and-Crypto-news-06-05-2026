★ Crypto Mom Takes Her Leave ★

Hester Peirce Quits the Securities and Exchange Commission on October 2, and Her Chair May Sit Empty a Long While

The commissioner who spent eight years arguing for rules rather than raids has posted her resignation letter. She will teach law in Virginia come November. Her seat has been vacant-in-waiting since June 2025 — and the last commissioner to surrender one waited eighteen months for a successor who never arrived.

By The Daily Cryptograph Staff | September 26, 2026

Hester Peirce has submitted her formal resignation from the US Securities and Exchange Commission, effective Oct. 2. She posted a copy of the letter to her X account on Friday, thanking the president for the "honor of her professional lifetime" and saying she leaves the agency under the leadership of Chairman Paul Atkins and Commissioner Mark Uyeda — the two remaining members, both Republicans. Peirce served on the commission for about eight years, and since Feb. 4, 2025 was also director of the agency's Crypto Task Force. She acquired the name "Crypto Mom" for her long advocacy of clear, rules-based regulation of the industry — a position she held through administrations that did not share it, and through one that now does.

The arithmetic of the departure is the part worth recording. Her term at the SEC officially expired in June 2025, but commissioners "may continue to serve up to approximately 18 months after terms expire if they are not replaced before then," according to the agency — which is why she was still sitting when she resigned. Her seat may not be filled promptly. Caroline Crenshaw, the agency's previous Democratic commissioner, departed in January, eighteen months after her own term ended, and no nomination to fill that seat has been made by President Donald Trump. An agency designed for five commissioners is now functioning with two, and will shortly be functioning with two for at least as long as the Senate takes to act.

Peirce is expected to join the law school of Regent University in Virginia as an associate professor in November, a move Cointelegraph reported in May. The university said she would help bolster its academic focus in federal litigation, securities regulation and digital assets. She leaves behind an agency that has radically changed its approach to crypto enforcement since Trump took office in January 2025: the SEC has dropped several enforcement actions and investigations into crypto companies, including those tied to the president and his family. Peirce's exit, in other words, is not a protest against that turn — it is a departure from an agency already travelling in the direction she long argued for, and the open question is who arrives next, and when.

The Sixth Circuit Says a State's Gambling Law Beats the Swaps Argument

Regulation September 26, 2026

Kalshi lost on appeal on Friday when the 6th US Circuit Court of Appeals ruled that Ohio and Tennessee may regulate the prediction market's sports-event contracts under their own state gambling laws. A three-judge panel sided unanimously with the two states, finding that Kalshi had failed to demonstrate its sports-event contracts are "swaps" falling within the jurisdiction of the Commodity Futures Trading Commission. The ruling follows a similar finding from the 9th Circuit Court of Appeals last month, which broke from an April decision by the 3rd Circuit that permitted the company to do business in New Jersey while its appeal proceeds.

That April ruling held Kalshi was likely to succeed with its argument that federal law preempts New Jersey's regulations — and it is the split between the circuits, rather than any single holding, that now matters most. Cointelegraph reported on Wednesday that a group of state lawmakers had filed an amicus brief with the Supreme Court, urging it to weigh in on the case between Kalshi and state gaming authorities. The question the justices would be asked to settle is a broad one: whether state authorities or federal agencies hold jurisdiction over prediction market companies. Until something settles it, the answer will differ by federal circuit, and by state line.

Fifteen Per Cent a Week, Algorithms, and Very Little Trading

Enforcement September 26, 2026

The Commodity Futures Trading Commission said it is suing Cash FX Group and three individuals in a $950 million foreign-exchange investment case involving cryptocurrency. The complaint, filed Friday in the US District Court for the Middle District of Florida, names Cash FX and its chief executive Huascar Jose Lopez Castillo of Brazil, The Conversion Pros and its chief executive Ronald Pope of Oregon, and Justin Halladay of Florida. The agency alleges the defendants operated a multilevel-marketing Ponzi scheme, soliciting and accepting more than $950 million for the purported purpose of trading retail foreign-currency contracts in a commodity pool.

The marketing, per the CFTC, promised returns of up to 15% weekly and claimed the pool's funds were handled by expert traders, proprietary algorithms and artificial intelligence. In fact, the agency alleges, Cash FX engaged in minimal forex trading and misappropriated most of the participants' money — using new contributions to pay fictitious trading profits while directing millions of dollars to each defendant. Participants also received false accounting statements, the CFTC says, and lost at least $406 million.

Bitget Raises the Tally to Three Hundred Eighty-Eight Million

Exchanges September 26, 2026

Bitget released an updated incident report on Thursday's security breach, revising the value of affected assets from $352 million to about $388 million. The exchange said onchain tracing places $387.5 million in assets transferred to attacker-controlled addresses — roughly $35 million more than it disclosed a day earlier — after identifying additional affected assets on the Zcash and TRON networks that were omitted from the initial estimate. Bitget said the revision reflects a more complete accounting of the original incident rather than fresh unauthorised transfers.

Withdrawals will remain paused while the exchange works to recover funds, and a bounty programme has been launched to incentivise the freezing or return of the assets. "The incident remains contained and no further unauthorized transfers are possible," Bitget said. The follow-up report did not address comments from chief executive Gracy Chen, who on Thursday said preliminary evidence pointed to possible North Korean involvement. The breach spans addresses on EVM networks, the XRP Ledger, Zcash and TRON; the assets taken include XRP, Ether, USDT, Zcash, USDC, USDT0, XAut, BNB, AVAX and TRX. Even revised upward, it stands behind only the roughly $1.5 billion of Ether taken from Bybit in February 2025.

The Perpetual Contract Crosses Into the Stock Market

Derivatives September 26, 2026

OG.com Markets has filed with the Commodity Futures Trading Commission seeking approval to offer perpetual futures tied to individual stocks. In a Thursday filing, the company proposed rules allowing it to list cash-settled single-stock futures that never expire and that trade 24 hours a day, five days a week. OG.com was recently spun out of Crypto.com as an independent prediction-markets and derivatives platform valued at $5 billion; chief executive Kris Marszalek said at the time that it intended to expand beyond prediction markets into futures and perpetual contracts, and Robinhood took an equity stake shortly afterwards as part of a multi-year deal to route its prediction markets through OG.com's CFTC-regulated exchange and clearinghouse.

The filing places OG.com alongside Coinbase, Kalshi and Kraken parent Payward in a widening American push to bring the perpetual onshore. The product was pioneered in crypto by BitMEX in 2016 and has never had an obvious home in US equity markets, precisely because its defining feature is what traditional futures lack: no expiration date, and therefore no need to roll. Whether the American equity market wants that property is now a question for the regulator rather than the exchange.

✦ The Last Resort Gets Cheaper: A Quantum Hedge for Under $67 ✦

StarkWare reports that the estimated computational cost of preparing a quantum-resistant Bitcoin transaction has fallen below $67, down from the roughly $320 spent on the first such mainnet transaction in August. The improvement came from participants in the firm's Quantum-Safe Bitcoin Optimization Challenge, who found ways to reduce the GPU computation required to build the transaction — and the defence requires no change to the network's consensus rules, which is rather the point of it. The first QSB transaction was mined and confirmed on Aug. 26, with engineering work from Tomer Giladi and direct submission through MARA's Slipstream service. Preparing it consumed approximately 3,100 GPU-hours across roughly 100 GPUs, at a compute cost of about $320, excluding network fees.

Avihu Levy, a StarkWare researcher, published the design in April, outlining hash-based protection against quantum attack without touching consensus rules. He described it at the time as a "last resort measure" because of its cost, complexity and limited applicability — and continued to argue for protocol-level change instead. The economics are the whole argument. "A construction that costs a few hundred dollars per transaction is a demo," the firm wrote in its Sept. 23 update. "One that costs $67 is closer to something a holder with a large unexposed balance might reach for in an emergency." The dashboard now shows an estimate nearer $66. The caveat stands, however: the optimisations have been demonstrated in benchmarks, not on mainnet.

The Tokenization Lane Is Narrow, and Most Tokens Stand Outside It

Policy

"Tokenization is coming to America," said Robinhood chief executive Vlad Tenev after the SEC announced its Innovation Exemption last week, and the market agreed with him: Bitcoin and Ether rose more than 10%, and Uniswap's UNI gained more than 30% in the days that followed. The five-year exemption creates a path for certain venues to trade tokenized National Market System stocks onchain without registering as a securities exchange, and for third parties to tokenize stocks — but only on a specific set of conditions. The tokens must confer the same "rights and privileges" as the underlying shares, and trading venues must permission both their users and their pools. Most stock tokens already in circulation fall outside the new rules: a token that merely tracks a share's price without granting shareholder rights is classified as a synthetic stock, and is not compliant.

Strategy Proposes Paying Its Preferred Holders Every Single Day

Corporate

Strategy is asking shareholders to approve moving its four preferred stocks, including STRC, to daily dividend payments — without changing the dividend rates or the total amount paid. The board approved the proposal on Thursday, according to a Friday filing with the SEC, and shareholders will vote at a virtual special meeting on Oct. 28. If it passes, every calendar day becomes a dividend record date and the corresponding payment follows on the next business day. STRC moves first, with its initial daily dividend expected Nov. 2; STRF, STRK and STRD follow in January, with their first payments under the schedule expected Jan. 4. Strategy is not the pioneer here. Strive moved its SATA preferred stock to daily payments first, announcing in May that it would pay every business day from June 16 at a 13% annual rate.

Wiring Tokenized Deposits Into the Format Banks Already Speak

Infrastructure

IBM has added beta connectivity between its Digital Asset Haven platform and Swift's blockchain-based ledger for tokenized deposits, alongside a beta option for banks to run the platform inside their own data centres. The addition is a messaging adapter that lets institutions instruct tokenized deposit transactions in ISO 20022, the global standard for financial messaging — so banks can reuse existing payment formats and compliance processes instead of building blockchain-specific workflows. Institutions in Swift's programme have already tested tokenized deposits on the ledger through the platform. Swift built that ledger with more than 40 financial institutions and launched it in July with 17 banks, among them HSBC, Citi, BNP Paribas, UBS and Standard Chartered; HSBC and Standard Chartered completed its first live cross-border transaction in August. The on-premises variant runs on IBM Z and LinuxONE without public cloud services, keeping key management behind the bank's own walls.

The Last Three Hundred Fourteen Coins, and the End of a Treasury

Corporate

Sequans Communications has sold its remaining 314 Bitcoin, closing out a treasury strategy that once saw the French semiconductor company hold more than 3,200 BTC. The exit follows the redemption of its convertible debt in May and lets it refocus on its cellular internet-of-things and software-defined radio businesses. Chief executive Georges Karam said the sales eliminated the convertible debt and strengthened the balance sheet, leaving Sequans with no cryptocurrency holdings and no outstanding debt beyond government-financed research and development obligations. The company launched the strategy in June 2025 on the back of a $384 million sale of equity securities and convertible secured debentures, when Karam called Bitcoin "a premier asset and a compelling long-term investment." It began unwinding less than six months later, selling 970 BTC in November to redeem half the convertible debt, and by May 2026 said it was "no longer pursuing" the strategy at all. It joins a growing list of digital asset treasury companies that have abandoned or scaled back accumulation during the 2026 bear market.

"No One Really Uses Ethereum," and a Flippening That Needs Five Times

Opinion

Kyle Samani, co-founder of Multicoin Capital, predicts that more crypto companies will choose to build on Solana over Ethereum, citing its ease of use and greater functionality, and that SOL will flip ETH during "this market cycle." He told Cointelegraph as much on an episode of Trade Secrets. The arithmetic is not small: the prediction requires roughly a fivefold increase in Solana's $58 billion market capitalisation to surpass Ether's $293 billion. Samani argues that "today, no one really uses Ethereum," and that it remains a leading network chiefly because of stablecoins — and stablecoins borrowed against Ether as collateral. In percentage terms the two have moved in near-lockstep through the recent upturn: over the past month Ether rose 30% and Solana 34%, though Solana's gain comes off a smaller base and followed a 59% decline over the past year, against Ether's 45%, according to TradingView. Samani stepped down as Multicoin's managing partner in February after ten years in the industry.
