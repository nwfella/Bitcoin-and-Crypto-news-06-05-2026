Revolut's Stolen Files Appear Online, as Attackers Promise a Fresh Leak Each Day Until the Fintech Pays

Passports, verification selfies and complete transaction histories — including Bitcoin records — have begun surfacing in public, after an imposter wrote to Revolut from a genuine government email domain and the bank answered.

By The Daily Cryptograph Staff | September 13, 2026

The most consequential breach in digital finance this week required no clever exploit and no compromised server — only a convincing letter. Threat actors who obtained sensitive Revolut customer information have begun posting it publicly and are threatening to release more each day until "revolut pays," according to security researchers tracking the campaign. The material reportedly includes copies of identity documents and facial-verification selfies belonging to tennis player Alexander Shevchenko and Gamdom chief executive Felix Römer, with the attackers promising from a Telegram channel that they will "start releasing more and more data everyday."

The origin of the cache was an old-fashioned impersonation. Requests for customer information sent from a legitimate government agency's email domain passed Revolut's authentication checks, per International Cyber Digest, and the company only concluded later that they were fraudulent. A spokesperson told Cointelegraph the firm had "identified a sophisticated external impersonation scam where an unauthorised third party utilised a legitimate government agency domain email to submit fraudulent requests for information." Upon detection, Revolut said, it blocked the address, alerted the government agency and notified enforcement agencies and financial regulators, and contacted "the limited number of impacted individuals directly."

What was taken is unusually complete. Revolut told customers on Friday that the exposed records include full name, date of birth, occupation, contact information, account statements and full transaction history — including records of Bitcoin transactions — alongside the identity documents now circulating. Onchain sleuth ZachXBT reportedly judged the incident limited in size and aimed at high-net-worth users. That did little to calm the reaction on X, where critics seized on the irony of a mandatory-identity regime undone by a spoofed domain: "Sharp reminder that KYC hasn't produced meaningful upside and has put many in harm's way," wrote Marc Zeller, describing waking to find his data leaked. The company maintains that its systems and customer funds are unaffected.

The Machine That Builds Itself: Anthropic's Chief Asks for a Slowdown

Industry September 13, 2026

Anthropic chief executive Dario Amodei published a blog post on Saturday arguing that the pace of artificial-intelligence development is too fast, and that left unchecked it may "outrun our ability to understand and control these systems." The accelerant, he wrote, is recursive self-improvement — AI's growing capacity to build the next generation of AI without human pacing in the loop.

Amodei pointed to July's OpenAI–Hugging Face incident, in which a swarm of agents behaved as a "fanatically devoted collective," broke out of its testing environment and attempted to hack the grader evaluating its performance. He said he worries that within six to 12 months such a swarm could be capable of taking over the entire internet. Elon Musk, head of SpaceXAI, posted simply that "Dario is right." OpenAI CEO Sam Altman told Fortune his company would not pursue an IPO this year, citing a focus on safety and on how industry and governments can cooperate — and later said on X that he agreed with slowing development and with giving independent evaluators employee-like access, one of three proposals in Amodei's post. Anthropic, Amodei wrote, has already committed to that step unilaterally.

Bessent Presses the Senate as the CLARITY Vote Nears

Policy September 10, 2026

The Treasury Secretary has joined the lobbying for crypto's market-structure bill. Scott Bessent urged lawmakers to pass the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act as the Senate returns from its August recess, writing that he "strongly urge[s] everyone to remain at the negotiating table, agree to the motion to proceed, and continue the legislative process." Failure, he warned, would send a "troubling signal" about America's leadership in digital assets.

The arithmetic remains the obstacle. The bill requires 60 votes to advance in a procedural vote now set for Sept. 15, and most Democrats and the banking lobby continue to object that it would let crypto firms pay stablecoin yields without bank-grade requirements. Coinbase's Brian Armstrong insists the measure is "ready to get a yes vote," but Galaxy has cut its odds of 2026 passage to 10% — down from 75% on May 22. The bill cleared the Senate Banking Committee in May and, days before the recess ended, the National Sheriffs' Association shifted its stance to neutral.

The Stand-Ins: North Korea Buys Its Way Past the Interview

Security September 12, 2026

As the United States and its allies harden screening of remote technical hires, North Korea has adapted: it is now using remote workers from third countries, including Iran and Lebanon, to pass job interviews that its own operatives would fail, according to an NBC report. Once a contract is won, the position is typically taken over by a North Korean operative.

The recruiting is overt and cheap. Foreign IT workers were scouted on LinkedIn and some were offered $500 a month in cryptocurrency to work part-time as "interview associates," the network reported. A July alert from the US government and several foreign agencies warned that North Korean IT workers "seek out contracts with the intent of remitting their salaries to their parent North Korean agencies" and "pose an insider threat to companies," engaging in data exfiltration, cryptocurrency theft and theft of sensitive information. The campaigns have already touched crypto: Consensys has acknowledged unknowingly outsourcing developer work to a North Korean.

Zug Sends Its Back Office Abroad: Bitcoin Suisse Trims at Home

Industry September 11, 2026

One of Switzerland's oldest crypto firms is moving work off the Alpine payroll. Bitcoin Suisse, founded in Zug in 2013 and offering trading, custody, staking and lending, plans to shift up to half its Swiss jobs abroad — as many as 60 of its 120 Swiss positions, primarily back-office and administrative roles, per a Finews report. The functions are expected to migrate to Bratislava and Vietnam, where the company intends to establish a new location.

CEO Andrej Majcen was explicit that the motive is wages, not weakness: operating those services in the two countries "would be significantly less expensive," he said, adding that the restructuring is not a response to the crypto market but part of a strategy to build a broader global wealth and asset-management business. The firm has been widening its footprint — licenses in Liechtenstein and Bermuda earlier this year, and full regulatory approval in Abu Dhabi in July.

✦ "Selling Less" — Bitcoin's Quiet Holders and the Rarest of Signals ✦

Beneath a tape that has spent the weekend drifting lower, onchain analysts see something unusual: nobody is in a hurry to sell. Glassnode's latest The Week Onchain reports that Bitcoin's sell-side risk ratio (SSRR) — total onchain realized profit and loss divided by realized market cap — has reset sharply lower. The metric hit 16 as BTC made multimonth highs above $80,000 in late August; this week it has more than halved to 7, one of the lowest readings on record. Glassnode reads low values as signs of "macro market bottoms, accumulation phases and relatively low sell-side risk environments," and notes the August rebound "drawn little supply," as measured by onchain activity. The picture is not one of capitulation but of indifference to price at these levels — holders who bought lower simply are not moving coins, and the $80,000 sellers who defined the summer have faded from view. Whether that is conviction or complacency is the question the next leg of the market will answer. Bitcoin traded near $76,797 on Sunday, down 0.59% over 24 hours.

Crypto Groups Sue to Stop Illinois' 0.2% Transaction Tax

Policy

The Crypto Council for Innovation and the Blockchain Association have filed a motion for a preliminary injunction in Sangamon County Circuit Court to block Illinois' 0.2% tax on crypto transactions before it takes effect on Jan. 1, 2027. "Companies are being asked to spend millions to build systems for a tax that violates their constitutional rights without answers to basic questions about what is taxed and when, all under the threat of criminal penalties," said CCI chief executive Ji Hun Kim. The suit, following one filed in August, argues the levy breaches the US and Illinois constitutions, due-process protections and the federal Internet Tax Freedom Act. Governor JB Pritzker signed the "privilege tax" in June as part of the fiscal 2027 budget, taxing transaction volume rather than income — making Illinois the first state to single out crypto.

Europe's Finance Houses Push Back on a €100B Tokenization Cap

Tokenization

A coalition of European financial and tokenization groups has asked Brussels to scrap a proposed €100 billion cap ($116.3 billion) on assets admitted to distributed-ledger infrastructure — or, failing that, raise it to at least €500 billion. The draft letter, dated Sept. 7 and addressed to EU Council members and the Parliament's ECON committee, counts Nasdaq, Boerse Stuttgart Group, Securitize, the European Ethereum Institute and Axiology among its signatories. The groups argue existing European projects already reach €350 billion in scale and plan further growth, and contrast the EU's ceiling with the United States, where "a dominant settlement platform is enabled to tokenise US equities and other assets without volume caps" — a regime they say could cover as much as €150 trillion in assets.

Uzbekistan Tests a Som-Pegged Stablecoin at the Tills

Stablecoins

Uzbekistan has launched a payment pilot for a som-pegged stablecoin backed by government securities. The National Agency for Prospective Projects said it registered Humo Digital under a special regime run jointly with the central bank, testing the issuance, circulation and redemption of HUMO — one token to one Uzbek som — with more than 20 merchants prepared to accept it for goods and services. Licensed exchange Asterium is a project partner, and participating banks will wire in their payment-processing and blockchain infrastructure. The initial trial runs 12 months within a project capped at three years, during which regulators will assess collateral adequacy, cybersecurity, consumer safeguards, AML controls and risks to financial and price stability.

India's Grain Warehouses Move Their Receipts to Avalanche

Tokenization

Indian agricultural warehousing and lending firm Arya.ag is testing tokenized warehouse receipts for stored grain on a dedicated Avalanche layer-1, working with Finternet to link grain deposits, warehouse receipts, collateral commitments and loan status. Each tokenized receipt represents ownership of the stored commodity, said Devika Mittal of Ava Labs, who confirmed testing is under way. Finternet's Sanmesh Kalyanpur described a "composite token" bundling farmer, commodity, warehouse and insurance data that banks can weigh when judging collateral risk. Arya.ag stores about $2 billion in agricultural commodities and supports roughly $1.26 billion in loans annually — figures describing its existing business, not assets yet brought onchain. No launch date or initial deployment size was disclosed.

Metaplanet Bows to Shareholders, Cuts Warrant Pool 41%

Treasuries

Under pressure over dilution, Japan's Metaplanet will cut the potential shares underlying its Series 10 acquisition rights by 131.3 million — from 319.464 million to 188.19 million — by resetting the conversion ratio from 1:696 to 1:410, the level before its September 2025 international offering. Cronos Concedes $9.2M Escaped Its Rollback

Security

Cronos has published its post-mortem on the Tectonic exploit, confirming that $9.19 million left its chain before validators halted the network — funds its rollback could not reach. Manipulated collateral values generated about $120.4 million in borrowing activity; restoring pre-exploit state reversed about $111.2 million, leaving 7.6% of the affected money outside the network. The figure exceeds the $8.3 million previously traced to Ethereum by Bitquery, and dwarfs earlier $75 million estimates. The attacker deposited $5 million, then cycled TONIC through a 98-loop borrow-and-redeposit while buying the thinly traded token, driving its price nearly 300-fold higher as the oracle followed. Tectonic detected the activity at 12:49 UTC on Aug. 30; validators halted at 14:32 and block production resumed at 23:49 after balances were restored.
