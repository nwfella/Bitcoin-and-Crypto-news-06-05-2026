★ The Stablecoin Land-Grab Reaches Main Street ★

Coinbase and Moov to Put Stablecoin Rails Behind 1,000 Community Banks

The exchange's regulated infrastructure will ride Moov's payments platform into more than a thousand community banks and credit unions — the same week Nasdaq took a $100 million stake in Kraken's parent and 21 global banks pressed ahead with a dollar stablecoin of their own.

By The Daily Cryptograph Staff | September 10, 2026

The stablecoin business moved one step closer to the corner bank on Thursday. Cryptocurrency exchange Coinbase partnered with financial platform Moov to bring stablecoin infrastructure to more than 1,000 community banks and credit unions that are part of Moov's customer base. The arrangement will combine Coinbase's regulated digital asset infrastructure with Moov's payments platform to offer stablecoin payment acceptance, settlement and real-time funding, the companies announced Thursday.

The infrastructure will support consumer stablecoin payments, merchant settlement and payouts, and will give businesses and merchants access to Coinbase custodial accounts. Community banks in the United States typically hold less than $10 billion in total assets and include state chartered institutions as well as savings and loan holding companies — a franchise large enough to matter and small enough that none of them will build this plumbing alone. That is the opening Coinbase and Moov are aiming at.

The announcement lands in the middle of a broadening rush by the largest incumbents. U.S. Bank, the fifth-largest commercial bank in the country, completed a live cross-border payment on Wednesday using its proprietary USBDC stablecoin on the Stellar blockchain. Earlier this month 21 financial institutions — among them Bank of America, Citi, Goldman Sachs, Deutsche Bank and UBS — announced plans to form a company that will issue a dollar-denominated stablecoin in the first half of 2027. Non-banks are crowding in too: in August, Western Union partnered with infrastructure provider Rain to launch a digital wallet and Visa-branded card that lets users hold and spend a dollar-backed stablecoin. The rails are being laid quickly, and this week the community bank became a customer rather than a bystander.

Tokenization Nasdaq Buys Into Kraken

Nasdaq Invests $100M in Kraken's Parent at a $21 Billion Valuation

Nasdaq has invested $100 million in Payward, the parent company of cryptocurrency exchange Kraken, as part of the New York electronic bourse's expansion into tokenized assets. Nasdaq's venture arm made the investment, Payward revealed in a Thursday announcement, and as part of the deal Kraken will offer tokenized versions of Nasdaq-listed stocks on its own platform. The investment builds on a partnership between the two announced in March.

Payward will also adopt Nasdaq's surveillance technology across its crypto, equities, tokenized equities, futures and options venues, folding an exchange-grade monitoring layer over a business that has grown well past spot crypto. The investment valued Kraken's parent at $21 billion, people familiar with the matter told Bloomberg on Thursday. Cointelegraph approached Nasdaq for comment but did not receive a reply before publishing time.

The deal lands a month after Nasdaq shared plans to acquire Level Markets as part of its push into "always-on" markets — and a year after it filed a tokenization proposal with the SEC. In April, Deutsche Börse invested $200 million in Payward as part of its own plans to widen access to blockchain-based securities; earlier this month Kraken partnered with the London Stock Exchange on 24/5 trading of tokenized UK equities from 2027. Per RWA.xyz, the distributed value of tokenized stocks is now more than $2.9 billion, up 7.4% over the past month — small, but compounding.

Industry One House Becomes Two

Consensys to Split Into MetaMask and a Separate Institutional Blockchain Company

Consensys Software Inc., the Ethereum software company behind MetaMask, plans to split into two independent companies, separating its consumer business from its institutional blockchain infrastructure operations. According to Wednesday's announcement, the separation is expected to be completed by the end of 2026, with Joe Lubin serving as chairman and CEO of MetaMask and executive chairman of the new Consensys.

The new Consensys will house the company's protocols and institutional infrastructure businesses — including Linea, Besu and Teku — and will be led by CEO Mike Kriak and President David Cunningham. Its focus: Ethereum infrastructure and helping financial institutions deploy blockchain technology for tokenization, stablecoins and other onchain financial services. MetaMask, meanwhile, will remain anchored in consumer self-custody while expanding beyond crypto into payments, savings, investing and traditional financial products.

MetaMask has recorded more than 100 million downloads across roughly 190 countries and has facilitated trillions of dollars in transaction volume, according to the company, which said the restructuring reflects increasingly divergent priorities for its consumer and institutional arms. Launched in 2016 as an Ethereum browser extension for reaching decentralized applications and managing crypto assets, MetaMask has spent the past year broadening into payments, yield and tokenized traditional assets — and in June launched Money Account, which pays up to 4% variable APY on eligible mUSD balances, funded by DeFi lending strategies rather than issuer interest.

✦ Feature: Post-Mortem — The 7.6% That Got Away ✦

Cronos has given its official accounting of the Tectonic exploit, and the number that matters is the one the rollback could not reach: $9.19 million left the blockchain before validators halted the network. In Tuesday's post-mortem report, the layer-1 said manipulated collateral values generated about $120.4 million in borrowing activity. Restoring the network to its pre-exploit state reversed roughly $111.2 million, leaving 7.6% of the affected funds outside the chain — a revision upward from earlier estimates that had placed the damage at about $75 million.

The mechanics were familiar and brutal. Blockchain data provider Bitquery described an attacker who deposited $5 million, then repeatedly borrowed and redeposited TONIC through a 98-cycle loop while buying the thinly traded token — activity that drove TONIC's price nearly 300-fold higher as Tectonic's price feed followed it upward. One transaction emptied nine Tectonic lending markets through 11 transfers involving stablecoins, Bitcoin, Ether and other assets. Bitquery had previously traced $8.3 million to Ethereum; Cronos now puts the total off-chain figure at $9.19 million.

The timeline closes a window of less than eleven hours: Tectonic detected the activity at 12:49 UTC on Aug. 30, validators halted the network at 14:32:47 UTC, and block production resumed at 23:49:01 UTC after balances were restored. The residual makes the case study uncomfortable for every chain that advertises a rollback button — halted ledgers can be rewound, but keys in a stranger's wallet cannot.

Treasuries Skipping a Week

Strategy Skips Its Bitcoin Buy to Repurchase $176M of STRC Preferred

Michael Saylor's Strategy, the largest corporate Bitcoin treasury, skipped its weekly Bitcoin acquisition to repurchase $176 million of its preferred STRC stock. The company bought back 1.8 million STRC shares for an aggregate $176.3 million between Aug. 31 and Sept. 7, according to a Tuesday filing with the US Securities and Exchange Commission, and doubled the size of its Digital Credit Securities Repurchase Program to $2 billion.

With no new purchases, Strategy's holdings sit at 845,050 Bitcoin, acquired for a total of $63.6 billion at an average purchase price of $75,412 apiece — a cost basis that leaves the position roughly break-even at Thursday's price near $77,194. The pause follows last week's $370 million buy, its first since mid-June.

The capital-allocation math explains the shift. STRC traded at $97.70 premarket, a 2.3% discount to its intended $100 par value, while the Nasdaq-traded MSTR common stock fell more than 3%. Trading below par limits Strategy's ability to raise funds through STRC sales and may force it to raise the dividend rate further — already lifted to 12% annually after the company's June 29 capital framework, which for the first time permitted Bitcoin sales to fund dividends. Buying back the discount is, for now, the cheaper lever.

Regulation Bessent to the Table

Treasury Secretary Urges CLARITY Passage as the Senate Returns

US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent urged lawmakers to pass the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act when the Senate returns from its August recess next week. "I strongly urge everyone to remain at the negotiating table, agree to the motion to proceed, and continue the legislative process," Bessent wrote in a Wednesday post on X, warning that failing to pass the bill would send a "troubling signal" about America's leadership in the digital asset industry.

The Senate is scheduled to return next Monday. Majority Leader John Thune has filed for a cloture vote on Sept. 15, where Republicans will need a handful of Democrats to reach the 60-vote threshold. The remarks come a week after the National Sheriffs' Association dropped its opposition on Sept. 3, shifting to "neutral" and removing one source of law-enforcement resistance to the market structure bill.

The odds have not followed. Galaxy has cut its probability of the CLARITY Act passing in 2026 to 10%, down from 75% on May 22. The bill cleared the Senate Banking Committee in May, but most Democrats and the banking industry pushed back, arguing it would let crypto firms offer yields on stablecoins without facing the same requirements as banks. With fewer than 36 legislative days before a new Congress is seated, the window is narrowing to a slit.

Taxation The Twelve-Month Reprieve Ends

Germany Proposes a Flat 25% Crypto Tax Starting in 2028

The German Federal Ministry of Finance has reportedly issued a draft proposal to shift cryptocurrency trading profits to the standard 25% flat-rate tax starting in 2028, applying to all crypto assets acquired after Jan. 1, 2027, per a draft seen by Die Welt. Grandfathering protections would let assets bought before the deadline stay under the old rules — which today make profits entirely tax-free after twelve months of holding, a perk that has made Germany a favored jurisdiction for long-term holders. Finance Minister Lars Klingbeil first revealed the overhaul plans in April and has said Berlin expects an additional €2 billion (~$2.3B) in revenue from crypto taxation.

Protocols Seven Years, Then Ethereum

Harmony Proposes Shutting Down Its Layer 1 and Migrating ONE to Ethereum

Seven years after launching its mainnet, Ethereum-compatible layer-1 Harmony has proposed sunsetting its blockchain and migrating its native ONE token to Ethereum. The plan calls for a final network snapshot, ERC-20 ONE tokens issued on Ethereum and migrated exchange listings, with an airdrop to the same addresses and no claims required. Multisig safes, liquidity pools and onchain applications cannot be migrated, and users were urged to exit all smart contracts before Sept. 10. Validators may begin shutting down that day, with $1.372 million set aside to compensate those who stop on time, retain stakes and serve as governors. It comes less than four weeks after an exploit minted forged ONE tokens.

Italy's Central Bank Orders Sanctions Screening for Crypto Transfers

Banca d'Italia has ordered mandatory sanctions screening for cryptocurrency transfers, telling crypto asset service providers they must put policies and internal controls in place to enforce EU financial sanctions and to identify customers and transactions linked to sanctioned parties. Fake Security Alerts Target Trezor and BitBox Users

Hardware wallet makers Trezor and BitBox warned users about phishing emails disguised as urgent security notices after suspected compromises involving third-party email services. Protocols Two Must-Ship Proposals

Ethereum Names Two 'Must Ship' Proposals for the Hegotá Upgrade

The Ethereum Foundation has identified two "must ship" proposals for the Hegotá upgrade, prioritizing censorship resistance and more flexible account authentication as it narrows the fork's scope. The ranking evaluated 62 proposed EIPs with input from roughly 60 researchers and engineers across the Foundation's Protocol cluster — the first unified view the group has published. The headliners are EIP-7805 (FOCIL), designed to give users a path to transaction inclusion without relying on centralized block builders, and EIP-8141 (Frame Transactions), which would introduce native account abstraction and a route toward post-quantum authentication. The designation means Hegotá's schedule should adjust before either proposal is dropped.

Europe A Cap Set Too Low

EU Finance Groups Push to Scrap the Tokenized Securities Cap

A coalition of European financial and tokenization groups — among them Nasdaq, Boerse Stuttgart Group, Securitize, the European Ethereum Institute and Axiology — has urged EU lawmakers to remove a proposed €100 billion ($116.3 billion) cap on tokenized financial instruments, or raise it to at least €500 billion as a baseline. Their Sept. 7 letter to EU Council members and Parliament's Economic and Monetary Affairs Committee argues that some existing European projects already reach €350 billion in scale and plan further growth. They contrast the ceiling with the United States, where they say a dominant settlement platform can tokenize US equities without volume caps, covering assets they estimate at as much as €150 trillion.
