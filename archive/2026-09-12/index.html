Reform UK Takes £36 Million From a Crypto Billionaire — the Largest Donation in British Political History

BitMEX co-founder Ben Delo paid the whole sum up front rather than in instalments, The Telegraph reports, so that it could not be blocked — a gift Farage calls confidence, and Labour calls the price of a party.

By The Daily Cryptograph Staff | September 12, 2026

The entanglement of British politics and digital-asset fortunes reached a new pitch on Saturday, as Ben Delo, the British crypto billionaire who co-founded the BitMEX exchange, gave £36 million — roughly $49 million — to Nigel Farage's Reform UK. By the accounting of the DPA and other outlets, it is the largest single donation ever made to a political party in Britain, dwarfing anything the country's campaign-finance system has previously absorbed. Delo said he had given the money to secure a "fair fight" at the polls.

Farage received the gift with gratitude and a flourish. "I am honored and humbled that Ben Delo has shown such confidence in Reform," he said, adding that Delo "knows that we are the only party that can turn the country around and reverse Britain's decline." The donation makes Delo the party's foremost patron, displacing billionaire Christopher Harborne, who gave £9 million last August.

The structure of the gift has drawn as much attention as its size. The Telegraph reported that £36 million represents £1 million a month until a general election in 2029 — but that Delo paid the entire amount in advance, the better to prevent any future attempt to block or unwind it. The donation lands amid a broader reckoning: Labour MPs have reportedly weighed making a moratorium on crypto donations permanent, following earlier gifts from Harborne and George Cottrell, and Farage himself remains under investigation over crypto-linked contributions. Delo's own history is no small part of the story — he was one of three BitMEX co-founders who pleaded guilty to US federal charges tied to the Bank Secrecy Act, paid a $10 million fine in 2022, served no prison time, and received a presidential pardon in March 2025 alongside Arthur Hayes and Samuel Reed.

Bitcoin Charges $79,000, Then Loses Its Footing as Bond Yields Snap

Markets September 11, 2026

Bitcoin returned to $79,000 on Friday after August's Consumer Price Index landed at 3.4% year-on-year — broadly the number the market expected, and enough to send risk assets briefly green. BTC had first dropped to $76,000, then reversed upward, gaining more than 3% on the day. The S&P 500 added 1% and the Nasdaq 1.1%, a rally catalyzed by CPI conforming to forecasts only a day after the Producer Price Index overshot.

The calm did not hold in the bond market. The 30-year Treasury yield whipsawed to its highest since June 2004 before falling to 5.309%, and traders doubled down on a 0.25% Fed hike on Sept. 16 — CME FedWatch put the odds at 85%, up from 60% a week earlier. "This is a nervous market," The Kobeissi Letter wrote. Core CPI rose 0.3%, a tenth more than anticipated, with gasoline up 3.9% as WTI crude circled $100 a barrel amid the expanding US–Iran war. Official rates sit at 3.50–3.75%, and Governor Christopher Waller has said he would lean toward holding if inflation showed at least "some signs of disinflation."

The CLARITY Act Turns Its Eye on "Non-Decentralized" DeFi

Policy September 11, 2026

A revised draft of the CLARITY Act would direct US regulators to decide whether the people behind "non-decentralized finance trading protocols" must comply with securities, commodities and anti-money-laundering rules. The text — posted on Senator Cynthia Lummis' website — defines such a protocol as one whose functionality, operation or rules can be materially altered by a person or coordinated group, including those whose controllers can restrict users or whose transactions are not governed solely by transparent, pre-established code.

The SEC and CFTC would write activity-based rules covering registration, conduct, disclosure, recordkeeping and supervision, while Treasury would set how Bank Secrecy Act duties apply to affected controllers. Software and distributed ledger systems would not be required to register in their own capacity, and participation in a security or incident-response council would not by itself establish control. The draft arrives before a procedural Senate vote on Sept. 15 that needs 60 votes — and its ethics section remains largely unchanged, the very point that has stalled negotiations. Coinbase's Brian Armstrong says the bill is "ready to get a yes vote"; Galaxy's odds of passage in 2026 sit at 10%.

Blockstream Will Not Pay: "It Is Theft, Not White-Hat Work"

Security September 11, 2026

Bitcoin infrastructure firm Blockstream declared it will pay no ransom to recover funds still held by the actors who raided the Liquid Network. "Taking assets without authorization and withholding their return is a crime, not responsible disclosure," the company said. "It is not white-hat activity. It is theft." Blockstream said it had engaged in good faith but would not accept the hackers' terms — a 10% bounty paid from Blockstream's own funds — as relayed onchain by Jan3 chief executive and former Blockstream strategy chief Samson Mow.

The accounting has narrowed to a hard remainder. Liquid paused operations on Sept. 6 after self-described white hats withdrew roughly 4,000 BTC — about $320 million — from its federation wallet; 3,400 BTC came back once affected bridge nodes were patched, leaving 598 BTC outstanding. Liquid resumed block production on Thursday, minting empty blocks after emergency updates, with transfers still suspended. Blockstream said it will work with law enforcement, exchanges, service providers and forensic specialists to trace the assets if the coin does not return.

✦ "You Can't Copy a Network" — Hyperliquid's One Real Risk ✦

Crypto Banter founder Ran Neuner says the greatest threat to Hyperliquid is not a rival exchange but a regulator's pen. Speaking on Cointelegraph's Chain Reaction podcast, Neuner argued that authorities have already begun writing rules for centralized venues and predicted decentralized platforms would be next: "The biggest issue is that we don't know how regulators are going to treat the decentralized exchanges." Hyperliquid's layer-1 and its perpetuals DEX lead the sector with about $223 billion in 30-day volume, per DeFiLlama — and Neuner is bullish that the moat holds. "You can't copy a network," he said. "There can be a thousand competitors to Uber. How many of them are going to succeed? Hardly any." Traders, he noted, gravitate to the busiest node because liquidity is what lets them enter and exit at all. Relief may come from Washington: President Donald Trump said in August that CFTC Chair Michael Selig was working to bring Hyperliquid into the US in a "fully compliant and legal fashion," a remark that lifted HYPE about 20% in a day. The token traded near $82 on Friday, up more than 220% year-to-date, with a market capitalization around $18.2 billion — against a fully diluted valuation of roughly $78.4 billion. No formal proposal, application or launch timeline has been published.

India Issues $107M of Bonds Natively Onchain

Tokenization

India's SEBI and central bank launched a tokenized corporate bond pilot, with REC, Larsen & Toubro and IIFL issuing a combined 10.25 billion rupees (about $107 million). Under Demat 2.0, bonds are issued and held as digital tokens on a distributed ledger owned by the statutory depositories and settled against the RBI's wholesale CBDC. SEBI says issuers now receive funds the day of bidding rather than two to three days later, and calls it the first such blend of native onchain issuance, depository records and CBDC settlement in regulated infrastructure.

Ethereum Names Two Must-Ship Proposals for Hegotá

Protocol

The Ethereum Foundation ranked 62 proposed EIPs for the Hegotá upgrade — its Protocol cluster's first unified view — and designated two as "must ship": EIP-7805 (FOCIL) for censorship resistance and EIP-8141 (Frame Transactions) for native account abstraction and a path to post-quantum authentication. Fifteen more sit in the A-tier, eight remain candidates, seven are below the line and 28 were declined. Client teams could begin implementation in late 2026, after Glamsterdam. The "must ship" label effectively defines the fork: if either is at risk, the schedule moves before the proposal drops.

Lords Vote 194–138 to Demand a Digital Asset Strategy

Policy

The UK House of Lords backed Amendment 88 to the Financial Services and Markets Bill by 194 to 138, requiring the Treasury to prepare, publish and consult on a digital asset strategy within 12 months of the bill becoming law. The strategy would cover cryptoassets, stablecoins and tokenized securities alongside innovation, consumer protection, and firms' access to banking, payment and settlement services. The Labour government opposed the measure, insisting it already has a strategy in execution. The bill must still return to the Commons, which may accept, amend or reject the change.
