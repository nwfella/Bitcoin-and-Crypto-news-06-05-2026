Bitget Restores Every Withdrawal as the $388 Million Is Put to THORChain

The exchange says all tokens return on Friday — and that its protection fund, set up with 5,500 BTC for exactly this kind of week, now stands at $309 million. The permissionless swap protocol that carried the money away is being asked publicly to bar the addresses that carried it. It says it cannot. NEAR Intents, which could, did.

By The Daily Cryptograph Staff | September 30, 2026

Bitget says it will resume withdrawals for all tokens on Friday, closing a week in which the exchange has been restoring access asset by asset after a breach that cost it $387.5 million and has since become a referendum on what a permissionless bridge owes a hacked counterparty. Chief executive Gracy Chen said in a Wednesday post on X that Bitcoin, Ether and USDt access had already been returned, that the remaining tokens would follow two days later, and that the company's Protection Fund — established in January 2022 with 5,500 BTC for precisely this class of loss — had been replenished to $309 million. The fund, she said, "was created for moments like this and absorbed the financial impact of the incident."

The incident itself landed on Sept. 24, when the exchange detected unauthorised transfers from part of its hot and warm wallet infrastructure and suspended withdrawals. It first put the loss at about $351.6 million and revised the figure upward to $387.5 million after accounting for further transfers on Zcash and TRON. Cold wallets were not touched. Chen has declined to narrow the field of suspects — an inside job has not been ruled out, nor have North Korean actors — and the company has opened a bounty offering 5% of any frozen funds and 5% of any recovered ones.

On Wednesday the security firm SlowMist traced the earliest logged hostile activity back to Aug. 31, three weeks before the theft, when an attacker exploited a zero-day vulnerability in a third-party security product. SlowMist describes a password retrieved from an environment variable, a second security product's management platform entered on Sept. 25 under an internal employee's identity, and a deleted, highly customised tool recovered afterwards that forged risk-control parameters and invoked the withdrawal process directly. Two fabricated Bitcoin withdrawal orders entered processing and returned errors. The earliest transfer SlowMist could verify onchain occurred at 2:31 a.m. UTC+8 on Sept. 25 — 93 TRX to an attacker-controlled address, then 0.84 Ether eleven seconds later — inside a compiled record spanning roughly two hours and fifty-two minutes across several chains.

The recovery attempt has produced the week's sharpest disagreement. Chen publicly urged THORChain, the cross-chain swapping protocol through which the stolen assets are being moved, to refuse service to the flagged addresses. THORChain's answer was a question of its own: "THORChain is decentralized and permissionless like Bitcoin, Ethereum, and BNB Chain. What responsibility should Bitcoin, Ethereum, and BNB Chain bear when handling known stolen funds?" NEAR Intents, which performs a comparable function, took the opposite view. Its SHIELD system blocked more than $50 million in attempted transfers, froze $503,000 in the attempt, and watched about $166,000 pass through regardless — and the protocol says it will forgo the 5% freeze bounty and the 5% recovery bounty so that more of the money can return to Bitget. Blockchain investigator ZachXBT reported Wednesday that wallets tied to the hack moved roughly $3.8 million in ZEC into the network's Ironwood pool, about 14% of the 18,917 ZEC taken.

The awkward fact sitting under all of it is that THORChain is not, in practice, as ungovernable as its defence implies. It halted its own network within hours in May when $10.7 million of its funds were exploited, and it retired its admin key in February 2025 — the very mechanism it would now need in order to filter an address at all. Whether the retirement is a principle or a reprieve is the question the next hack will settle.

A Ceiling at Eighty-Five Thousand as the Long Bond Refuses to Cool

Markets September 29, 2026

Bitcoin gave back its latest gains after Tuesday's Wall Street open, a local rally to $84,540 stalling as the US trading session began and the pair retreating below its daily opening level near $83,600. The obstacle overhead was not crypto's own: the 30-year Treasury yield pushed to new 24-year highs above 5.60%, and the 10-year reached 5.26%, on the cusp of passing its June 2007 high for levels last seen in April 2002.

The pressure is broad rather than specific to digital assets. Geopolitical uncertainty around the US–Iran war, high oil prices and persistent inflation kept investors cautious, and surging yields dragged precious metals down with everything else — gold fell 3.6% on Monday to $4,115 an ounce before rebounding to $4,166. Market commentator The Kobeissi Letter called the move "highly unusual." On the crypto side, analysis points to long-term holder supply reinforcing the resistance at $85,000, with the recovery case resting on support nearer $82,500. Q3 gains remain above 40%, which is either the number that matters or the one that hides everything else.

One Thousand Six Hundred Sixty-Five Coins, Bought Above the Company's Own Average

Corporate September 28, 2026

Strategy bought 1,665 Bitcoin for $142.7 million last week, lifting its holdings to 847,666 BTC. Its USD cash balance fell to $1 billion from $1.05 billion, and the separate reserve kept for preferred dividends and debt interest slipped to $5.02 billion from $5.04 billion after $22.1 million of payouts. The preferred dividends are now the second obligation this machine must feed.

Glamsterdam Goes to Sepolia on the Sixth, and the Gas Rules Change With It

Protocol September 29, 2026

Ethereum developers have scheduled the network's next major upgrade, Glamsterdam, to activate on the Sepolia testnet on Oct. 6. Node operators must update both execution-layer and consensus-layer clients before activation, the Ethereum Foundation said. Among the changes is enshrined proposer-builder separation, which moves the handoff between specialised block builders and validators into the protocol itself, reducing the network's reliance on outside middleware.

The upgrade also introduces block-level access lists, which record the accounts and storage locations used during each block, and rewrites gas pricing to better reflect the cost of creating and accessing data on Ethereum. That last item carries a migration warning: contracts relying on fixed gas stipends or hardcoded gas limits may need changes. Developers have not set dates for the validator-focused Hoodi testnet or for mainnet, and are targeting 2027 for Hegotá, the upgrade expected to follow — the one Vitalik Buterin says may be Ethereum's last recognisable fork.

Five Hundred Fifty Million Frozen, and a Senate Report Saying It Was Not Enough

Stablecoins September 29, 2026

Tether says it helped authorities freeze nearly $550 million in Iran-linked USDt during 2026 — more than $130 million across four wallets this year, plus $344 million in April tied to the Central Bank of Iran — and that its cooperation has produced more than $4.9 billion in cumulative freezes, $2.4 billion of it with US authorities. "USDt is not a haven for sanctioned actors, terrorist organizations or criminal networks," said chief executive Paolo Ardoino.

Both men can be right: an issuer that freezes $550 million is also an issuer whose token is, by volume, the one worth using.

Three Commissioners Left to Write the Rules

Regulation September 29, 2026

After Friday, the two US agencies that police digital assets will be down to three commissioners between them. Hester Peirce — known across the industry as "Crypto Mom" — leaves the SEC after eight years, roughly two months before the 18-month extension of her second term would have run out. Chair Paul Atkins and Mark Uyeda remain, a bipartisan panel meant to hold five reduced to two.

The CFTC has been led by chair Michael Selig as its sole commissioner. Under federal law only the President can nominate replacements, and the White House has announced no nominations, though an official said Trump intends to do so "in the near future." A CFTC spokesperson said Selig "welcomes new Commissioners upon their nomination and confirmation" and that the agency was "more than equipped" to oversee its part of the crypto market. The vacancies land after the failed vote on the CLARITY Act, which leaves guidance rather than statute as the operative instrument — and fewer hands to issue it.

Base Adds a Compliance Layer to Its Token Standard

Tokenization September 30, 2026

Base activated its Cobalt upgrade on Wednesday, its third major release, expanding the B20 token standard so that issuers can stack several compliance checks onto a single asset — identity verification, accredited-investor status and sanctions screening could all gate the same transfer. Issuers can also schedule corporate actions such as stock splits, changing the share count displayed in wallets and apps without minting, burning or altering the underlying balance.

The upgrade grants authorized administrators the power to move tokens out of a holder's wallet without the holder's approval and attach a public note to the transfer; Base says issuers decide whether to enable that and who may use it, and that Base itself cannot initiate such transfers. Cobalt also introduces Validity Transactions, which become eligible for inclusion only once specified onchain conditions are met — a swap that executes only if an asset reaches a set price before a block deadline — and can stay private until included. The building blocks of a regulated security are being assembled on a chain that spent its first years as a place to trade memecoins.

✦ Who Is Liable When the Bridge Will Not Look Away ✦

The week's most consequential legal question was posed, in public, by a chief executive to a protocol that has no office to answer it. Bitget asked THORChain to refuse service to addresses holding $387.5 million of its stolen assets. THORChain replied that it is "decentralized and permissionless like Bitcoin, Ethereum, and BNB Chain," and asked what responsibility those chains would bear when handling known stolen funds. Crypto lawyer Yuriy Brisov, speaking to Cointelegraph, says the answer turns on something the protocol has already given away.

"It depends on the level of decentralization," Brisov said. "When they do this — when they block some addresses — they show that their nodes aren't truly decentralized." Blocking is good for the community when the power is used to stop malicious activity, he argues, but it is also what "opens themselves to any other legal claim" — because a system that can filter fraud can, in principle, be asked why it does not filter something else. The strongest defence for any decentralised protocol is the appearance that it cannot intervene; an intervention made in good faith is still an admission of capability.

NEAR Intents has chosen the other side of that trade, and said so plainly. Its general manager, Alex Shevchenko, allowed that permissionless systems need not be neutral: "The people who build these systems make choices about what those protocols enable. Refusing to help launder stolen assets is one of ours." He went further: "A financial system where stealing an asset gives you an unrestricted right to monetize it isn't a freer system. It is simply a system that protects the thief." NEAR will hand back the frozen funds through legal process rather than collect the bounty.

The precedent is not theoretical. THORChain processed roughly $1.2 billion of the funds from the $1.46 billion Bybit hack — a coincidence made possible because its admin key had been retired just 11 days earlier. In May it paused its own chain within hours when $10.7 million of its own funds were exploited. Whatever the protocol's stated position, the market's will be measured in what happens the next time the choice is not close.

London Opens Its Window, and the Old Registrations Do Not Come With It

Regulation

The UK's Financial Conduct Authority opened applications on Wednesday for crypto businesses seeking authorization under a regime taking effect on Oct. 25, 2027. Firms intending to keep operating in Britain should file by the end of February 2027, and the regulator expects to decide those applications before the regime begins. The framework extends the FCA's remit well beyond anti-money-laundering and financial-promotion rules, covering stablecoin issuance, trading platforms and market abuse — rules finalized in June. "The UK's new crypto regime will give consumers greater protections and firms a clear framework to operate in," said Dominic Cashman, the FCA's director of authorization. The trade body The Payments Association welcomed the opening with a warning: existing MLR registration "will not carry over, and firms should be realistic about the standards they will need to meet."

A Fifth of a Percent, and the Draft Rules That Explain Who Pays It

Policy

Illinois tax officials published draft rules detailing how the state's already-enacted 0.2% digital asset transaction tax would apply to stablecoins, DeFi and wallet transfers. Under the proposal, stablecoins count as taxable digital assets while nonfungible tokens are excluded. DeFi transactions are generally exempt unless users pay fees that constitute "valuable consideration" — protocol fees for operating or maintaining a platform — while network fees and swap fees paid solely to liquidity providers would not trigger the tax. Bridging is treated as taxable exchange activity when performed through a digital asset broker for consideration, and exchange-to-self-custody transfers can be taxed when the exchange charges a fee. The Digital Asset Tax Act passed in June over industry objections and takes effect Jan. 1, 2027; the Department of Revenue is taking comments through Oct. 30.

Greece Joins the Register, and a Regulator Widens Its Denial

Regulation

Greece entered the European Union's crypto register for the first time, with four providers — BCash, Xenios Blockchain Group, Capital Wallet Greece and Piraeus Bank — added to ESMA's MiCA list. Six further providers from Germany, France and Slovenia brought the register to 359 unique firms. The honour of being Greece's first could have gone to Binance, which withdrew its application on June 24; the Wall Street Journal later reported that ECB President Christine Lagarde intervened to block it. Greece's HCMC denied the attributed remarks and has now expanded that denial in comments to Cointelegraph. The country splits oversight between the HCMC and the Bank of Greece — a structure MiCA permits, and one that makes the licence a matter of which regulator is reading.

Payments

Binance Pay began letting eligible overseas visitors to Japan spend more than 100 cryptocurrencies at the vast majority of PayPay-supported merchants on Wednesday. The service runs through HIVEX, a framework connecting overseas QR payment services to PayPay's network, with USDt as the backend settlement layer: HIVEX settles with PayPay, and PayPay settles merchants in yen. Fuel That Cannot Claim Its Own Credit Twice

Enterprise

Petrobras has built two applications on Cardano to track sustainability data for sustainable aviation fuel and its Diesel R renewable fuel. The first, developed with PUC-Rio's Ledger Labs, tokenizes the environmental attributes of aviation fuel into digital tokens — CS-SAF — that carry metadata aligned with the CORSIA offsetting scheme, so that the same claim cannot be used more than once. It uses a Book-and-Claim model, which separates the environmental benefit from the physical fuel and assigns it to an airline, company or passenger even if the fuel is burned elsewhere; passengers can claim a certificate tied to their journey. The second application creates digital checkpoints tracking production, transport and use of Diesel R for Scope 3 reporting. The work extends a collaboration begun in 2023 with blockchain training for employees.

Analysis

Veteran trader Peter Brandt had called Oct. 4 as the day Bitcoin's bear market would end; he now thinks the market beat him to it. "There's a good possibility we have seen the low and now are entering a new bull market cycle," Brandt tells Cointelegraph, arguing that the fall to around $58,000 in late June may already have marked the cycle bottom — while leaving open a pullback toward $65,000 or $66,000 in early October to shake out late buyers: "We all know Bitcoin doesn't go straight up."
