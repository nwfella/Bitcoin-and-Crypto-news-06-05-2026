Markets The $1.92 Billion Week

Bitcoin ETFs Post Strongest Weekly Inflows Since October 2025

US spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds recorded $1.92 billion in net inflows last week — their strongest weekly performance in nearly 10 months — after months of uneven flows, according to SoSoValue data. ETF analyst Nate Geraci added that spot Ether ETFs attracted roughly $700 million, with Bitcoin and Ether funds each posting their best week since October 2025.

The renewed demand came as Bitcoin jumped more than 20% on the week, surging past $79,000 on Friday after starting near $63,000. Despite the burst, US spot Bitcoin ETFs remain down about $2.91 billion net for 2026; August's $2.38 billion inflow makes it the strongest month so far this year. The last major wave — $3.42 billion in October 2025 — immediately preceded the Oct. 10 crash that triggered the industry's largest liquidation event on record.

Markets The Compliant Pathway

HYPE Jumps 20% on Trump Signal for Hyperliquid's US Return

Hyperliquid's native token surged more than 20% in 24 hours after President Donald Trump said regulators were working on a compliant pathway to make the decentralized trading platform available to American users. HYPE traded near $62 before the remarks and jumped as much as 16% to a $72.28 high before settling around $70, per CoinGecko.

Trump referred to CFTC efforts during a White House event — "I understand that Mike is also working to bring Hyperliquid into the United States in a fully compliant and legal fashion" — though neither the CFTC nor Hyperliquid has released a formal proposal. Meanwhile, shares of Nasdaq-listed HYPE treasury firm Hyperliquid Strategies (PURR) closed up 30.4% at $9.39, and someone paid about $65,000 for 719 PURR call options roughly four hours before Trump spoke.

Security The Governance Heist

Term Finance Vault Governance Exploit Drains Estimated $8.5M

Decentralized lending protocol Term Finance lost an estimated $8.5 million after an attacker exploited governance control of its strategy vaults, according to blockchain security firms. PeckShield said the attacker drained about 2,843 Ether (ETH) — worth $6.87 million at the time — and 1.68 million USDC, roughly 68% of the $12.45 million held in Term's vault product, including nearly all of its ~$8.8 million in Ethereum deposits.

Term Labs said it had irreversibly shut down all Term Meta Vaults and revoked their DAO governance roles, keeping withdrawals open. Onchain monitor Defimon said the attacker cheaply acquired a majority of a sparsely held governance token and passed proposals to seize control. The vaults use Yearn V3 infrastructure, but Yearn stressed the attack hit a custom governance wrapper and does not apply to standard Yearn setups.

Markets The Consolidation Phase

Bitcoin Consolidates Near $77K as $90K Odds Hit 48%

Bitcoin consolidated above $77,000 after Friday's Wall Street open — its highest levels since May 15 — as gold joined the rally to 14-week highs of $4,632 per ounce, up 2.2%. On a monthly basis, BTC/USD and gold were up 13% and 16%, respectively, with prediction markets now pricing roughly a 48% chance of $90,000 Bitcoin by year-end.

Market commentary The Kobeissi Letter attributed the synchronized surge in precious metals and crypto to inflation, record deficit spending and US Treasury policy — including the department's pledge to at least double long-dated bond buybacks. Trading firm QCP Capital flagged that financial stress signals extend beyond the US, pointing to surging Japanese government bond yields after a rare joint currency intervention, as the macro bid for hard assets spreads worldwide.

✦ Feature: The $100,000 Question ✦

Standard Chartered wavers on its Bitcoin year-end target — and the miss may be on the low side. Geoff Kendrick, the bank's global head of digital asset research, told clients in a Friday note that "for the first time this year there is now a risk my end year forecast (of USD100k) is too low." He now sees Bitcoin moving toward its $126,000 all-time high before year-end, with the recovery potentially accelerating after Oct. 6, driven largely by short liquidations while spot ETF inflows recover.

The turn reverses a gloomy February, when Kendrick cut the year-end BTC target to $100,000 from $150,000 and the Ether target to $4,000 from $7,500 while expecting a dip toward $50,000. Industry watchers echo the shift: Swan Bitcoin CEO Cory Klippsten said Bitcoin may bottom in October, while 10x Research founder Markus Thielen said an August close above $63,000 — already far surpassed — could confirm the bear market is ending. Bitcoin traded at $79,402 at press time, up 24% over the past week.

Regulation The Sept. 5 Deadline

Pakistan Opens Crypto Licensing Portal, Sets Sept. 5 Deadline

Pakistan's Virtual Assets Regulatory Authority (PVARA) opened its licensing portal, moving the country's crypto framework from legislation into enforcement. Firms providing virtual asset services on or before March 5 must file for a no-objection certificate by Sept. 5 or cease operations — a requirement facing both domestic and overseas VASPs serving the market. "The licensing window is officially open, creating a clear pathway for businesses to enter Pakistan's regulated virtual asset market," PVARA said.

Regulation The MiCA Leaderboard

Germany Widens MiCA Lead as 6 Cooperative Banks Added

Germany extended its lead in EU MiCA licensing, with six cooperative banks — Raiffeisenbank Aidlingen, Ihre Volksbank, VR-Bank Mittelfranken Mitte and others — added in the latest ESMA register update, bringing the EU's authorized CASP count to 331. Germany now accounts for 79 licensed firms, ahead of France (35) and the Netherlands (29), and up from 57 in late June. Traditional lenders continue to enter the crypto market as the bloc's regulatory framework matures.

Macro The $40 Trillion Milestone

US Debt Tops $40T as Analysts Weigh Impact on Bitcoin

US federal debt crossed $40 trillion for the first time, renewing debate over whether mounting government borrowing strengthens Bitcoin's case as a scarce, non-sovereign asset. Interest costs have overtaken Medicare to become the federal government's second-largest budget expense, and the milestone coincided with Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's pledge to double buybacks of 10- to 30-year bonds to calm a selloff that pushed long yields to 2007 highs.
