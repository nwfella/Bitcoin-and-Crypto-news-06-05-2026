Markets Macro — The $65.3K Tag

Payrolls Miss Flipped Fed Bets to a Pause — and Bitcoin Tagged a New August High

Bitcoin hit $65,340 on Bitstamp, up 1.3% on the day and a fresh month-to-date high, as markets digested July nonfarm payrolls showing the US economy lost 23,000 jobs — with May and June revised down by a combined 103,000. CME's FedWatch Tool now shows markets expecting the Fed to hold rates at September's meeting, where just yesterday majority odds had favored a 0.25% hike.

The S&P 500 opened 0.5% higher and the Nasdaq added just over 1%. Sygnum Bank CIO Fabian Dori cautioned the print cuts both ways: "An orderly slowdown supports the liquidity relief case, while a print weak enough to raise growth concerns can still pressure risk assets even as rate odds move."

QCP Capital called the macro picture "uncertain" for Bitcoin but praised the tape: "For crypto, the week's price action points to resilience rather than clear directional confirmation." The Coldcard exploit fallout and corporate BTC sales, QCP said, sparked only "limited demand for panic protection" on options markets.

ETF The Silent IPO

Spot Bitcoin ETFs Post Best Week Since April With ~$1B Inflows

Demand for US spot Bitcoin ETFs rebounded sharply this week, with Bloomberg analyst Eric Balchunas putting net inflows at roughly $1 billion — the strongest showing since April and the third-best week since October, a period he called Bitcoin's "silent IPO." The term, popularized by investor Jordi Visser, describes early investors selling into institutional demand, keeping BTC subdued despite heavy new capital.

The rebound follows the Coldcard exploit, which saw roughly $116 million in Bitcoin stolen via a flaw in how affected devices generated wallet keys. Balchunas floated — without proof — that the hack may push uneasy self-custodians toward ETFs: "Long-term I can't imagine there aren't some who migrate over."

Regulation Cloture Filed

Senate Majority Leader Thune Files Cloture, Setting Up September CLARITY Act Vote

Senate Majority Leader John Thune filed cloture on a motion to take up the CLARITY Act, setting up a key procedural vote on the crypto market-structure bill after lawmakers return in September. Invoking cloture requires 60 votes, meaning Republicans will need Democratic support just to clear the procedural hurdle — the vote is not on passage and does not guarantee a final vote.

The move revives momentum for one of the industry's most consequential bills after negotiations stalled over ethics provisions and rules governing stablecoin rewards. Lawmakers are reportedly working on a bipartisan ethics addendum aimed at Democratic concerns over President Trump's crypto-related financial interests, including a proposal requiring the president to divest from certain crypto businesses.

Law Tracing Lazarus

US Court Backs Bybit's Bid to Trace Funds From $1.5B North Korea Hack

Court records unsealed Thursday show a federal judge backed Bybit's effort to trace assets stolen in the $1.5 billion North Korea-linked hack by granting the exchange expedited discovery. Bybit filed the lawsuit under seal on June 18 against North Korea, its Reconnaissance General Bureau, the Lazarus Group and 20 unidentified defendants.

The discovery authority gives Bybit a practical route to identify alleged intermediaries and pursue the small portion of stolen assets that remains traceable — rather than relying solely on a judgment against North Korea. In its complaint, Bybit said some traceable assets reached exchanges operating in the US and sought account-holder identities, balances and transaction histories.

✦ Interview: Nansen Founder — "Bitcoin Will Never Fall Below $60K Again" ✦

Nansen founder and CEO Alex Svanevik says crypto is finally leaving the "toy world" era behind. "Crypto assets have kind of been like the 'toy world' era of blockchains. And now we're moving into the real-world era, where you see tokenized stocks, you see people trading indices like the S&P 500 and Hyperliquid," he told Magazine on the Trade Secrets show, arguing blockchains are now "giving a lot of room for non-crypto assets."

On Bitcoin, Svanevik sees the market near a bottom: "I don't think Bitcoin's gonna go back below $60,000. I think that's the past… I think forever," citing Bitcoin's role as a hedge against central bank money creation. He called the view that Solana is only for meme coins "completely ridiculous," praised its "incredible team," and backed Robinhood chain as a rising Base contender — while doubting it will ever launch a token: "They clearly don't need to." Veteran investor Michael Terpin offers the bear case, telling Cointelegraph Bitcoin will fall roughly 66% from its October all-time high of $126,100: "I think that brings us down into the 40s."
