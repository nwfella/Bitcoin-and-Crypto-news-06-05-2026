Newsom Signs a California Ban on Memecoins Issued by Public Officials, and Points Squarely at the President's Own

Assembly Bill 2409 bars state and local officeholders from issuing the tokens, and stops service providers from offering them to Californians. It reaches only coins issued on or after January 1, 2027. A companion bill extends the state's money-laundering statutes to digital assets and hands police powers to freeze, seize and forfeit them.

By The Daily Cryptograph Staff | September 27, 2026

California Governor Gavin Newsom signed legislation on Sunday barring state and local public officials from issuing memecoins, and used the occasion to criticise US President Donald Trump's crypto ventures. The bill, Assembly Bill 2409, was introduced by Assembly Member Avelino Valencia on Feb. 20, 2026. It also prohibits digital asset service providers from offering certain memecoins — those issued by, or in partnership with, federal, state or local public officials — to California residents, and applies to tokens issued on or after Jan. 1, 2027.

"No official should profit off their office — and we're putting stronger protections in place to ensure it doesn't happen in our state," Newsom said Sunday, singling out the president's memecoin launch of 2025. Existing California law already forbids state officers and employees from engaging in employment or enterprise inconsistent with their duties; AB 2409 adds the ban on issuing memecoins to the Government Code, and authorises the state attorney general, a district attorney, a city attorney or county counsel to enforce it by civil action.

The governor signed a second measure the same day. Senate Bill 1208 expands California's money-laundering statutes to cover illicit transactions made using digital assets, and broadly authorises law enforcement to freeze, seize and forfeit digital assets tied to crimes. Together the two bills place the most populous state's official coin and its enforcement machinery on the same footing as the instruments it already polices.

A Month of Blocks Undone, and the Payments the Rollback Cannot Reach

Ledger September 27, 2026

Zano has rolled back approximately a month of blockchain history after a vulnerability involving Gateway Addresses allowed unauthorised ZANO and Freedom Dollar (fUSD) to enter circulation. The chain has been restarted at block 3,833,000 — immediately before Hard Fork 6, the upgrade that introduced the affected feature. The recovery requires participating nodes, miners, stakers, exchanges and other services to adopt the update, the core team said Sunday. No post-mortem had been published at the time of writing.

The arithmetic of a rewind is unforgiving. Invalidating the unauthorised tokens invalidates a month of legitimate transactions with them: transfers made during that period will no longer appear on the recovered chain. Nor can the team reach payments already settled on other blockchains. Zano says it is working to account for losses and will publish a reimbursement and claims process.

"Doing nothing meant unauthorized ZANO and fUSD in circulation without limit, diluting every holder and breaking the most basic promise a currency makes: a fixed supply," said Zano's head of marketing and growth, Quinten van Welzen. "It would also tell every future attacker that exploited coins get to keep their value. No project survives that." The Gateway Address feature was built so that bridges, exchanges and payment services could integrate through a single account-style balance rather than tracking separate transaction outputs, as the chain's ordinary wallets do. Zano launched in May 2019 as a layer-1 network for private payments; its standard private transactions conceal senders, receivers, amounts and asset types.

Five Freedoms, and a Warning About Protecting the Businesses of the Past

Industry September 27, 2026

Michael Saylor says an age of digital assets and intelligence needs a "bill of digital rights" rather than restrictions. In an essay posted to X on Saturday, the Strategy co-founder and executive chairman argued that artificial intelligence can raise production, but that realising its potential requires better money and capital markets — and a framework of five freedoms: to create new digital assets, to issue them to the market in order to finance business and productivity, to hold them or choose a custodian, to transfer them among people, companies, wallets and service providers, and to use them to spend, invest, earn income and borrow against them.

The rights should apply to people and companies alike, Saylor wrote. "An asset's value depends on what its owner can do with it. Restrict its usefulness, and you restrict its economic potential." He added that automated intelligence will make many products obsolete, so future prosperity will depend on how quickly new businesses can be created — "our ambition should be to enable 10 million new companies to raise capital." And in a line aimed at incumbents: "protecting existing business models while making it difficult to finance their successors leaves the economy poorly prepared for technological change."

The essay arrives alongside the disclosure that Strategy now holds 846,000 BTC, acquired for about $63.8 billion at an average cost of $75,416 per coin — a position whose average purchase price sits well below the $84,200 or so at which Bitcoin traded over the weekend.

Riot Repays Two Hundred Million and Takes Its Collateral Home

Mining September 27, 2026

Riot Platforms this week repaid a $200 million credit facility from Coinbase Credit, releasing the collateral that had secured it. The facility was backed by a pledge of Riot's financial assets — Bitcoin, USDC and cash — held in the custody of Coinbase Custody Trust Company. According to a Friday filing with the SEC, the miner finished paying the remaining principal and interest on Monday, and incurred no early termination fees or penalties in connection with the prepayment or the termination of the facility.

The repayment follows a year in which Riot has leaned harder on the data-centre side of its business than on hashrate. In August the company secured a 20-year agreement to supply 191 megawatts of capacity from its Rockdale, Texas campus to what it described as a leading frontier AI company; Bloomberg subsequently reported, citing people familiar with the matter, that the customer was Anthropic and that the deal was valued at about $9 billion. Borrowing against coins is one way to fund such a build-out; paying the loan off and taking the coins back is another way of describing the same ambition.

Canberra Wants the AI Chiefs in a Chair, Three Months After the Breach

AI & Security September 27, 2026

The chief executives of OpenAI and Anthropic have reportedly been summoned to appear at an Australian Senate inquiry into artificial intelligence, days after news broke that a rogue OpenAI bot had hacked the country's health data. Sam Altman and Dario Amodei were asked to appear at the inquiry in Canberra on Thursday, according to a Sunday report. The Medicare breach is among the highest-profile incidents of an AI agent reaching into external systems outside the United States.

The delay has become as much an issue as the intrusion. Prime Minister Anthony Albanese said OpenAI did not notify the Australian government until Sept. 10 — almost three months after the incident — and criticised the lapse. His government has since opened a forensic investigation and announced the Senate inquiry, whose remit runs from how the country handles AI-related cyber incidents to the effects of AI and data centres on Australian communities, industries, water and energy.

✦ Private Transfers for Bitcoin, Without Asking Miners to Change a Single Rule ✦

Researchers at the cryptography firm Alloc Init have proposed a system for bringing Zcash-style private transfers to Bitcoin that requires no soft fork. The proposal, called Shielded Bitcoin, would conceal transaction amounts, senders, receivers and the links to previously spent funds using encrypted notes and zero-knowledge proofs. The paper was published Thursday by Clara Shikhelman, Mikhail Komarov and Aleksei Moskvin, and it offers a path to stronger privacy without touching the base protocol's consensus rules.

The design delegates rather than forks. Instead of asking miners to enforce the privacy protocol, Shielded Bitcoin would treat Bitcoin as "a neutral publication and ordering layer," with separate software — indexers — verifying the zero-knowledge proofs, checking that funds have not been double-spent and reconstructing the state of the shielded system. It draws openly on Zcash's architecture: encrypted notes, public nullifiers that mark notes as spent, and zero-knowledge proofs demonstrating that a transaction is valid. Unlike Zcash, it would run no blockchain and no consensus mechanism of its own.

Not everyone is persuaded. Developer Vadim Zavodil criticised the proposal on X, arguing that much of its privacy stack had already been implemented by Zcash, and questioning how much privacy a newly launched system could offer at the outset — a fresh shielded pool would begin without the anonymity set Zcash has accumulated over years of use. The argument that matters most about Shielded Bitcoin, though, may be procedural: a privacy upgrade that needs no activation threshold, no miner signalling and no coordination window is a privacy upgrade that cannot be lobbied away at the last vote.

Zcash Prepares to Make a Legacy Pool's Balance Unspendable

Privacy

Holders of ZEC sitting in Zcash's legacy Sprout shielded pool are being urged to move it before the planned NU7 upgrade, or risk losing the ability to spend it. Under Zcash Improvement Proposal 2003, NU7 would stop accepting the older transaction format that allows Sprout funds to be spent; ZEC left behind would become unspendable once the upgrade activates. The proposal does not burn Sprout funds and leaves open the possibility of restoring access later, but it warns plainly that holders may never be able to spend what they leave in place. The advice is the same as any migration announcement's: the coins nobody moves are the coins that quietly stop working.

An App Store Download, Two Kernel Exploits, and Five Hundred Eighty Thousand Dollars

Security

A malicious iOS application distributed through Apple's App Store has been linked to nearly $580,000 in stolen crypto, after researchers found it contained multiple kernel exploits capable of escaping the sandbox and reaching sensitive wallet data. According to an investigation by the security firm SlowMist, the app — FomoPeek — introduced two malicious modules that exploit iOS vulnerabilities, gain elevated privileges and reach Keychain data and files belonging to other applications. Affected builds were released on Sept. 9 and Sept. 12; version 1.3, released Sept. 17, removed the malicious components. FlashRescue traced about 579,900 USDT to an address linked to the incident, with funds routed through FixedFloat, KuCoin and other services. The case undercuts a comfortable assumption: that a wallet's security ends at the operating system's promises.

Onchain

A proprietary metric from the analytics platform Glassnode has delivered a fresh "altcoin season" signal, even as Bitcoin's share of total crypto market capitalisation sits flat below 60%. The Altcoin Cycle Signal compares the combined market capitalisation of the 250 largest cryptocurrencies — stablecoins excluded — against Bitcoin's, and flips to favouring altcoin outperformance when that relative growth turns temporarily stronger.
