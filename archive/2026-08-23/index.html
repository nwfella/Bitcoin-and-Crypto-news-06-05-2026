Markets The $126,000 Question

Standard Chartered Says $100K Bitcoin Year-End Target May Be 'Too Low'

Bitcoin may move toward its all-time high of $126,000 before the end of the year, with the recovery potentially accelerating after Oct. 6, according to Geoff Kendrick, global head of digital asset research at Standard Chartered. In a Friday note, Kendrick said the latest rally has been driven largely by short liquidations while inflows into spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds have also started to recover. "For the first time this year there is now a risk my end year forecast (of USD100k) is too low," he wrote.

The note reverses a gloomy February, when Kendrick cut the year-end BTC target to $100,000 from $150,000 and the Ether target to $4,000 from $7,500, expecting Bitcoin to fall to around $50,000 first. Other watchers echo the turn: Swan Bitcoin CEO Cory Klippsten said Bitcoin may bottom in October, while 10x Research founder Markus Thielen said an August close above $63,000 could confirm the bear market is ending.

Research The Belief Premium

Fed Study: Crypto Investors Are Driven by Beliefs, Easily Swayed by Returns

A new Federal Reserve Bank of Cleveland working paper offers a provocative explanation for why cryptocurrency behaves so differently from traditional assets: Americans who buy crypto don't simply have different demographics or risk appetites — they hold sharply different beliefs about future returns. Using repeated surveys of up to 25,000 US households, researchers found that expectations about crypto returns explain more of the variation in ownership than standard economic factors.

The paper, titled "Do You Even Crypto, Bro? Cryptocurrencies in Household Finance," also runs a randomized information experiment: simply giving people information about Bitcoin's recent performance increases both their desired crypto holdings and their return expectations. That could create a feedback loop in which rising prices reinforce bullish expectations and pull more investors into the market — a mechanism that may help explain crypto's persistent volatility.

Business The Prime Broker Push

Ripple Raises $275M for US Prime Brokerage Expansion

Ripple closed a $275 million senior note offering on Tuesday to support its expansion into US financial services, issued through its non-bank prime brokerage, Ripple Prime. The private placement attracted "a diverse base of institutional investors," and Ripple said proceeds will fund growth in prime brokerage, financing and multi-asset clearing.

Ripple Prime President Noel Kimmel said the response signals "confidence in our long-term vision for the growing intersection of traditional and digital asset financial infrastructure." The company acquired Hidden Road last year in a roughly $1.25 billion deal, launching its institutional prime brokerage business, which was later rebranded as Ripple Prime.

Infrastructure The 350 Millisecond Era

Solana Cuts Blockchain Slot Time to 350 Milliseconds

Solana reduced its slot time to 350 milliseconds — the first such reduction since the network's genesis — according to Jacob Creech, vice president of technology at the Solana Foundation. "We're in a new era of 350ms. Next stop, 300ms," Creech said in a Friday post. Average slot times stood at 360ms at press time, down from the network's original 400ms target, per the Solana slot time explorer.

In June, the Foundation outlined plans to cut slot times from 400ms to 200ms to improve latency and accelerate confirmations, with three further 50ms reductions planned. All four stages are targeted for mainnet activation in Agave v4.2, the validator client developed by Anza, though the schedule remains tentative. The enabling proposal, SIMD-0525, was approved and merged on May 14.

✦ Feature: MANTRA Chain Halted After Token Crashes to Record Low ✦

MANTRA's native token sank 18.5% from its 24-hour high to a record low shortly before MANTRA Chain stopped producing blocks, prompting the project to halt the network as a precaution. According to CoinGecko, OM fell from $0.005060 to an all-time low of $0.004126 around 11:00 pm UTC Thursday before recovering to about $0.0044 — still down roughly 10% over 24 hours, with trading volume climbing.

"We don't have a root cause or timeline to share yet," MANTRA said Friday, adding that the network had been paused over an "unexplained incident" while it investigates. The halt prevents assets from moving on MANTRA Chain and has prompted affected exchanges to pause deposits and withdrawals, with no timeline given for either service to resume.

Bitcoin Miners Pour Billions Into AI as Capex Outpaces Revenue

Accounting The Cash Equivalent Test

FASB Proposes Conditions for Stablecoins as Cash Equivalents

The Financial Accounting Standards Board has proposed guidance outlining when companies may classify stablecoins as cash equivalents under US GAAP. A qualifying token would need an on-demand contractual redemption right, direct redemption with its issuer for a known cash amount and at least one-to-one segregated reserves in short-term, highly liquid assets. The clarification could give companies a consistent way to report stablecoins used in payments or treasury operations — but not every dollar-pegged token would qualify.
