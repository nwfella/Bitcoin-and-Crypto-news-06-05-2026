Crypto's Market Cap Reclaims $3 Trillion, and Bitcoin Holds Its Ground at $86,000

Bitcoin printed $87,350 on Monday — its best level since January 29 — then held $86,000 through Tuesday's session. Spot Bitcoin ETFs took in $999 million, their largest single day of 2026, while perpetual open interest climbed to nearly $160 billion, the highest since October 2025.

By The Daily Cryptograph Staff | September 22, 2026

The total cryptocurrency market capitalization briefly climbed back above $3 trillion on Tuesday as Bitcoin and the largest altcoins extended a broad rally, ending the day up roughly 4.3% over the previous session. Bitcoin traded near $86,000, up about 4.5% in twenty-four hours; Ether added 2.3% to $2,745, XRP rose 5.7% to $1.53 and Solana advanced 3.6% to $117. Dogecoin was among the strongest large-cap performers, up about 11%. Data from TradingView showed BTC/USD volatility cooling after Monday's spike to $87,350 — the coin's highest level since Jan. 29 — and Sunday's weekly close of $81,120 remains the highest since the start of May.

The bid arrived from outside crypto, as it has all month. President Donald Trump, addressing the UN General Assembly in New York, pledged to reach a deal ending the US–Iran war but suggested it would come only after November's midterm elections — "I believe we'll make a deal right after the election because it doesn't make sense for them not to." WTI crude fell as low as $89.16 a barrel, its lowest since Sept. 4, aided by reports that Saudi Arabia had reopened the East-West Pipeline, the key supply route; three anonymous sources cited by Reuters said flows would need six to eight weeks to reach full capacity. US equities tracked sideways through the speech. On the charts, Glassnode noted that a classic momentum indicator had returned above a key long-term trend line, and CryptoQuant's Julio Moreno said Monday that Bitcoin's move above its 365-day moving average was the final signal needed to confirm a new bull market.

Beneath the price the leverage has rebuilt quickly. Bloomberg reported that open interest in perpetual futures across crypto climbed to nearly $160 billion, its highest level since late October 2025, and that more than $920 million in bearish positions were liquidated on Monday as prices surged. That cuts both ways: the report noted leverage could amplify moves in either direction. Spot ETFs, by contrast, brought stickier money — US spot Bitcoin funds drew $998.9 million in net inflows on Monday, their biggest daily haul of 2026 and the largest since Oct. 6, 2025, while US spot Ether ETFs took about $270 million, also a 2026 record. Despite the surge, the Bitcoin cohort still shows roughly $464 million in net outflows for the year to date.

The desk's own caveat, carried forward from yesterday: a durable advance would need coin-denominated open interest to expand — "indicating fresh positioning rather than a move driven primarily by short covering" — and a daily close beneath $77,100 invalidates the structure to the downside, exposing the True Market Mean at $76,677. Thirteen coins on our tape tonight, ten of them green: the argument has moved from whether the low is in to how much of the rally is funded by borrowed conviction.

A Billion Dollars in a Day, and Still Down for the Year

Funds September 22, 2026

US spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds drew $998.9 million in net inflows on Monday — their largest daily haul of 2026, and the biggest since Oct. 6, 2025, when the cohort took in more than $1.2 billion. The day eclipsed this year's previous high of $844 million on Jan. 14, according to SoSoValue. BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust led with $381 million, followed by the ARK 21Shares fund at $289 million and Fidelity's Wise Origin Bitcoin Fund at roughly $239 million, per Farside Investors.

The nuance is in the cumulative line. Despite the surge, US spot Bitcoin ETFs have posted about $464 million in net outflows so far in 2026 — a year that spent months bleeding the January launch enthusiasm back out. Bitcoin traded at $85,430 at the time of publication, up 4.7% in twenty-four hours and 12.3% over the past month, having briefly cleared $87,200 on Monday. US spot Ether ETFs drew around $270 million, their own 2026 record, while US spot XRP ETFs recorded no net flows, leaving cumulative net inflows at about $1.71 billion. CryptoQuant's Julio Moreno called the move above Bitcoin's 365-day moving average the last confirmation the desk needed.

NEAR Runs 80% as Its Private Rail Picks Up Volume

Markets September 22, 2026

Near Protocol's native token surged nearly 80% over the week, outpacing the wider market as the network extended its privacy-focused trading services. NEAR traded around $4.29 on Monday, up about 78.2% over seven days and 22% in twenty-four hours, per CoinGecko, against a total crypto market cap that rose about 6% over the same span. On Thursday, Near said deposits and withdrawals for perpetual futures trading through near.com were confidential by default, obscuring the link between a trader's funding wallet and a dedicated Hyperliquid trading account.

The volume numbers are doing the talking. NEAR Intents, which lets users request cross-chain swaps and has market makers compete to execute them, showed about $29.3 billion in cumulative volume and $842 million over the past seven days. The privacy wallet ZODL was its third-largest referral source by volume in the preceding day, generating about $3.8 million across 458 transactions, and a single swap of roughly $613,000 in ZEC ranked among the largest displayed. Near also said confidential total value locked had crossed $70 million, triggering the first snapshot under its NEAR@3.33 incentive program, which releases 333,333 milestone tokens when the three-day volume-weighted average price reaches $3.33. Bitwise analyst Camran Khosravi called Near and Zcash "complements" — while cautioning that Intents TVL can rise when the price of ZEC already inside the system climbs, even with no new deposits.

The Altseason Signal Fires, and Dominance Goes Nowhere

Analysis September 22, 2026

Glassnode's Altcoin Cycle Signal — which compares the market cap of the 250 largest cryptocurrencies against Bitcoin's — has flipped to favouring altcoin outperformance, with its seven-day rolling mean at 81.25 on a normalized 0–100 scale. "The first rally in August saw altcoins stay relatively flat while BTC moved. However, today's rally has ignited the full breadth of the altcoin market," the firm wrote. The combined altcoin market cap reached $1.19 trillion on Tuesday, its highest since late January, and is up 33% since Aug. 19. The exact methodology behind the metric is not disclosed, which is worth carrying alongside the number.

What makes the signal interesting is what Bitcoin's dominance is not doing. BTC's share of total market cap sits at 59.7% — versus 59.2% on Aug. 19 — and has moved inside a narrow band all month. Trader Matthew Hyland described a state of "complacency" among Bitcoin holders, arguing dominance set a macro high at 66% in June 2025 and that investors have been unwilling to accept how little ground BTC has taken against alts since. The counter-reading is simpler than either camp: a market where altcoins run while dominance holds flat is a market rising on new money rather than rotation out of the largest asset.

Zcash Sets November 5 for Its 25-Second Blocks

Protocol September 22, 2026

Zcash developers are targeting Nov. 5 for NU7 mainnet activation after organisations and engineering teams reached "unanimous agreement" on its contents and timeline. Developer Sean Bowe said the upgrade is scheduled to activate on testnet Oct. 6, with a final decision on mainnet and its activation height set for Oct. 20 after assessing performance. NU7 would cut target block spacing from 75 seconds to 25 seconds, disable version 4 transactions and integrate the Network Sustainability Mechanism, configured to preserve Zcash's halving schedule with previously removed supply reintroduced from February 2031.

Bowe said NU7 introduces no new transaction formats and should not significantly affect wallets, though full nodes, indexers and block explorers may need adjustments. The direction was endorsed emphatically: 98.9% of holders backed keeping the halvings and 99.9% of the ZEC-weighted vote supported the faster-block proposal. It lands amid a historic run for the privacy sector — on Thursday ZEC rose about 20% in a day, and Glassnode data showed the sector sitting 213% above its level at Bitcoin's October 2025 peak, versus about 85% for a basket excluding ZEC.

✦ Does Anyone Actually Own the Satoshi Fortune? ✦

Nearly 1.1 million Bitcoin is widely attributed to Satoshi Nakamoto — a fortune in the tens of billions. The number rests on a forensic trail identifying a mining operation, not a person, and it swings by more than 200,000 Bitcoin depending on how strictly a particular "fingerprint" test is applied. That distinction stopped being academic on Sept. 5, when 600 BTC mined in 2010 moved after sixteen years. The coins came from twelve long-dormant block rewards mined over four days in March 2010; someone controlling the private keys spent them one by one within half an hour, about $46 million at the time.

It almost certainly was not Satoshi. Onchain tracker Whale Alert found no connection between the 600 BTC and the creator's presumed stash. Blockchain research firm Bitquery found that ten of the twelve blocks did not match the distinctive mining pattern associated with Satoshi's operation, known as "Patoshi," and researcher Gaurav Agrawal said the two remaining blocks showed only weak matches that could occur by chance. The rewards were mined by a single machine, and whoever spent them this month held the keys — but as Agrawal put it: "What the chain cannot say is whether the hand in 2026 belongs to the person who ran the machine in 2010." The Patoshi pattern itself remains circumstantial: all the identified transfers came from coinbases in that pattern, which provides "compelling reasons that Patoshi and Satoshi are the same person, although not proof." Whoever was mining it started at the very earliest beginning, which is the entire basis for the attribution — and the entire reason a $46 million transfer can move markets on a rumour.

Brussels Is Asked to Stop Putting Stablecoin Reserves in Banks

Regulation

The ECB and EU central banks want to remove MiCA's mandatory bank-deposit thresholds for stablecoin reserves — at least 30%, and 60% for significant tokens — and substitute minimum liquidity thresholds for reserve assets maturing within one and five working days. The ESCB's response to the Commission's MiCA review, published Tuesday, argued the existing rule "creates a direct link between issuers and credit institutions" and could leave banks exposed if a stablecoin run forced an issuer to pull deposits quickly. Overnight reverse repos and short-term sovereign bonds were floated as substitutes, echoing draft EBA thresholds from 2024 — 40%/60% for significant tokens, 20%/30% otherwise. The central banks also flagged "material challenges" enforcing MiCA, noting non-compliant firms can still reach EU customers.

Circle Opens Bitcoin-Backed Borrowing for Institutions

Stablecoins

Circle launched Digital Asset-Backed Borrowing, allowing eligible Circle Mint customers to deposit Bitcoin, mint its wrapped cirBTC token and supply that as collateral to third-party onchain lending markets on Arc or Ethereum. Morpho is the first protocol supported, with Aave and others to follow; borrowed USDC lands directly in the customer's Mint balance, while rates, collateral requirements and liquidation thresholds are set by the lending market, not by Circle. Positions are overcollateralised and collateral stays in a customer-controlled wallet. New York clients are excluded. The token is backed 1:1 by Bitcoin held at Circle National Trust, and the launch follows Arc mainnet, which uses USDC as its native gas token.

The Eurosystem Puts Its Own Money on a Tokenized Ledger

Infrastructure

The ECB is preparing to invest a "small portion" of its own funds in tokenized public-sector securities, settling the purchases in central bank money through Pontes — the Eurosystem's DLT settlement system launched Monday. The central bank said the exercise gives it first-hand experience across the full investment lifecycle: trade execution, settlement, systems and portfolio management. Initial purchases focus on euro-denominated securities issued by euro-area central, regional and government bodies, public agencies and European supranational institutions, funded from a non-monetary-policy portfolio that earns income to cover operating expenses. The Executive Board will fix timing and operational details once preparatory work closes. The European Stability Mechanism said Pontes would connect emerging DLT platforms to established central bank infrastructure, preserving central bank money's role as the settlement asset.

White Hats Move 52 Bitcoin Into a Recovery Trust

Security

White hats have transferred 52.37 Bitcoin recovered from wallets exposed by the Coldcard vulnerability into an address controlled by the Wyoming-based Crypto Recovery Trust, Galaxy Digital head of research Alex Thorn said. About 40% of the Bitcoin associated with the exploit's second wave was swept by white hats to protect victim funds; 3.0134 BTC of the transfer came from addresses Galaxy had not previously tracked, with their origin unconfirmed. Security researcher and SEAL 911 incident responder Nick Bax said on Sept. 9 that he helped rescue about 50 Bitcoin in late July because the funds were "imminently going to be stolen" due to the entropy flaw. Thorn cited a published total of 1,830 BTC across 9,162 addresses linked to the vulnerability. Victims can check the trust's website to see whether it controls their funds.

The Case That the Violent Cycles Are Over

Opinion

Ben Nadareski, CEO of the Solana-based DeFi platform Solstice, says crypto is unlikely to return to its extreme boom-and-bust cycles because deeper liquidity has changed the market's structure. Speaking on Cointelegraph's Chain Reaction, he said liquidity across major pairs has increased even in bear markets, and that crypto is increasingly a market for institutional capital and household wealth rather than speculation: "We don't want to go through 2017. We don't want to go through 2021. We don't want to go through these massive fluctuations." The data partly agrees — a December 2025 report from Glassnode and Fasanara Digital found Bitcoin's one-year realized volatility had fallen from 84.4% to 43%, with daily spot volumes rising to $8–22 billion from $4–13 billion in the prior cycle.

Litigation
