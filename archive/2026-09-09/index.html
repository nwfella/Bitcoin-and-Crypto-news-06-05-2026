★ The Clarity Ultimatum — 36 Days to Save the Bill ★

The Senate's Reckoning: CLARITY Act Faces a Filibuster Test It May Not Survive

Senators return Monday from a month of state work periods, and Majority Leader John Thune has scheduled a Tuesday cloture vote on the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act. Sixty votes are needed. Senator Cynthia Lummis warns the next real opportunity may not arrive until 2030.

By The Daily Cryptograph Staff | September 9, 2026

The legislation the crypto industry has chased for years arrives at its narrowest gate yet. Senator John Thune, the Republican majority leader, has scheduled a cloture vote on the Digital Asset Market Clarity (CLARITY) Act for Tuesday, and his conference will need support from a handful of Democrats to reach the 60-vote threshold that breaks a filibuster. The chamber returns Monday after more than a month in which lawmakers scattered to their home states.

Should the bill fail to advance with a three-fifths supermajority, the Senate will have fewer than 36 days of business before 2027 — when a new Congress is sworn in, one that Democrats could control depending on November's midterm results. Senator Cynthia Lummis, among CLARITY's loudest champions, warned on Sept. 6 that the "next real opportunity" for the bill might not come until 2030 if lawmakers cannot reach agreement and send it to the president's desk. She is not seeking reelection this year, and with all 435 House seats and 33 Senate seats on the ballot, event contracts on prediction markets currently give Democrats the odds on retaking the House while the Senate is roughly a coin flip.

The stakes are a function of what arrived before it. When Republicans took the Senate in 2024 the party held a legislative trifecta — Senate, House and presidency — and used it to pass the Guiding and Establishing National Innovation for US Stablecoins (GENIUS) Act. A reversal of that control would leave the party approaching crypto bills on Democrats' terms beginning next year. Among those watching the swing seats: Sherrod Brown, the Ohio Democrat who once chaired Senate Banking, who was ousted in 2024 amid a torrent of Fairshake-funded advertising and is now running in a special election against Republican Jon Husted to complete the term won in 2022 by now-Vice President JD Vance.

Industry One House Becomes Two

Consensys to Split Into MetaMask and a Separate Institutional Blockchain Company

Consensys Software Inc., the Ethereum software company behind MetaMask, plans to split into two independent companies, separating its consumer business from its institutional blockchain infrastructure operations. According to Wednesday's announcement, the separation is expected to be completed by the end of 2026, with Joe Lubin serving as chairman and CEO of MetaMask and executive chairman of the new Consensys.

The new Consensys will house the company's protocols and institutional infrastructure businesses — including Linea, Besu and Teku — and will be led by CEO Mike Kriak and President David Cunningham. Its focus: Ethereum infrastructure and helping financial institutions deploy blockchain technology for tokenization, stablecoins and other onchain financial services. MetaMask, meanwhile, will remain anchored in consumer self-custody while expanding beyond crypto into payments, savings, investing and traditional financial products.

MetaMask has recorded more than 100 million downloads across roughly 190 countries and has facilitated trillions of dollars in transaction volume, according to the company, which said the restructuring reflects increasingly divergent priorities for its consumer and institutional arms. Launched in 2016 as an Ethereum browser extension for reaching decentralized applications and managing crypto assets, MetaMask has spent the past year broadening into payments, yield and tokenized traditional assets — a consumer franchise now large enough to stand alone.

Charters Builders Bank & Trust

Jack Dorsey's Block Seeks a US Trust Bank Charter for Bitcoin and Stablecoin Custody

Jack Dorsey's payments company Block is seeking to establish a federally regulated trust bank to provide custody services for Bitcoin and stablecoins. Block said Tuesday it had submitted an application to the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency to create Builders Bank & Trust, an uninsured national trust bank that would operate under OCC supervision and offer custody and related fiduciary services.

The proposed institution would not accept deposits and would not make loans, setting it apart from a conventional commercial bank. Block said the charter would give its custody operations a consistent national framework as the business expands, and named Lee Woolley — its digital asset strategy lead — as Builders Bank's president and CEO. Woolley said the institution would lean on Block's digital asset operations and its experience running Square Financial Services, the company's existing industrial bank.

Block joins a lengthening queue of fintech and crypto firms chasing national trust charters: Ripple holds conditional approval for a similar charter, while Circle and BitGo have received final approval. Kraken parent Payward and crypto infrastructure provider Zerohash have also submitted applications — a quiet migration of the industry's custody business into the federal perimeter.

✦ Feature: Markets — The Eighty-Thousand Ceiling ✦

Bitcoin struggled below $80,000 on Wednesday as attention refocused on the Japanese yen and on a fresh escalation in the Persian Gulf. TradingView data showed the local upside in BTC/USD reversing as the pair attempted to revisit the $80,000 mark, with Bitcoin down around 0.4% on the day — and colder still by press time at roughly $78,090.

US stocks drifted lower at the Wall Street open, nudged by fresh American strikes on Iranian oil tankers. The tensions carried crude to new three-month highs, building on the prior day's gains: WTI traded above $96 a barrel while Brent surged past $101 for the first time since late July. Traders also watched the yen, which reached its highest level against the dollar since February, up 6.5% since the start of August — the kind of funding-currency strength that has reliably drained risk appetite from crypto's higher-beta corners.

The pullback lands atop a market that had been quietly strengthening on its own ledger. CoinShares head of research James Butterfill argued in his latest update that "Bitcoin is trading like gold again, but the Fed still sets the ceiling" at around $80,000 — and that fund flows show investors trading the rate path rather than leaving the asset class. Fed Funds futures implied roughly a 60% chance of a rate hike following next week's FOMC meeting.

Banking USBDC on Stellar

U.S. Bank Tests Its Own Stablecoin in a Live Cross-Border Stellar Payment

U.S. Bank, the fifth-largest commercial bank in the United States, has completed a live cross-border payment using its proprietary USBDC stablecoin on the Stellar blockchain. The pilot moved funds between U.S. Bank entities in North America and Europe, with USBDC issued and transferred on the public Stellar network.

The test also exercised the stablecoin's minting, redemption, freezing and clawback functions while integrating with the bank's existing risk, compliance and operations systems — the unglamorous machinery that decides whether a tokenized deposit can survive a regulator's questions. The transaction validated U.S. Bank's internally developed Digital Asset Platform, which connects tokenized assets to its traditional banking infrastructure, the Minneapolis-based lender said Wednesday. It is now exploring cross-border treasury operations, liquidity management and moving collateral onchain.

The pilot builds on an October 2025 decision to establish a dedicated Digital Assets and Money Movement unit and on stablecoin testing with Stellar that dates to at least November 2025 alongside PwC and the Stellar Development Foundation. It also arrives as the industry splinters: while American banks have pushed back against letting stablecoin issuers offer yield or rewards, 21 major institutions — including Bank of America, Citi, Goldman Sachs, Deutsche Bank and UBS — announced plans on Sept. 1 to form a company that will issue a dollar stablecoin in the first half of 2027, later expanding to other G7 currencies.

Intelligence Doubling Down on the Trace

TRM Labs Doubles Its Valuation to $2B as Blockchain Intelligence Demand Surges

Blockchain intelligence firm TRM Labs has doubled its valuation to $2 billion following an expansion of its Series C round led by Blockchain Capital. The company did not disclose the size of the latest investment but said its annual recurring revenue has quadrupled over the past three years. The expansion follows a $70 million Series C in February, also led by Blockchain Capital, which took the firm past a $1 billion valuation with Citi Ventures and Galaxy among the investors; prior to that round it was valued at $930 million, per Tracxn data.

TRM provides blockchain intelligence and investigation software used by more than 600 government agencies and private-sector institutions across 75 countries, with AI-powered tools aimed at fraud, money laundering, sanctions evasion and other digital crime. The sales pitch has hardened with the numbers: losses reported to the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center rose to $21 billion in 2025 from $16 billion in 2024, and TRM says criminal adoption of AI has risen 40% year over year on its own AI-in-Crime Adoption Index.

The new valuation lands roughly two months after US Immigration and Customs Enforcement awarded TRM a one-year contract worth about $95 million for forensic software and support services to Homeland Security Task Force investigations. Rival Chainalysis challenged the sole-source award in federal court later that month, alleging ICE's decision was "arbitrary, capricious, and unreasonable" — a reminder that the sector's real competition is now fought over government procurement.

Taxation The Twelve-Month Reprieve Ends

Germany Proposes a Flat 25% Crypto Tax Starting in 2028

The German Federal Ministry of Finance has reportedly issued a draft proposal to shift cryptocurrency trading profits to the standard 25% flat-rate tax starting in 2028, applying to all crypto assets acquired after Jan. 1, 2027, per a draft seen by Die Welt. Grandfathering protections would let assets bought before the deadline stay under the old rules — which today make profits entirely tax-free after twelve months of holding, a perk that has made Germany a favored jurisdiction for long-term holders. Finance Minister Lars Klingbeil has said Berlin expects an additional €2 billion (~$2.3B) from the overhaul.

Litigation A Privilege Tax, Challenged

Trade Groups Move to Block Illinois's 0.2% Crypto Tax Before January

The Crypto Council for Innovation and the Blockchain Association have filed a motion for a preliminary injunction in Sangamon County, Illinois seeking to stop Illinois from enforcing a 0.2% tax on cryptocurrency transactions before it takes effect in January 2027. "Companies are being asked to spend millions to build systems for a tax that violates their constitutional rights," said CCI chief executive Ji Hun Kim. Justice The Gaming-Server Cartel

Malone Lam Pleads Guilty in a $245M Crypto Theft Conspiracy

Singaporean national Malone Lam pleaded guilty to a racketeering conspiracy that prosecutors say used social engineering and home break-ins to steal and launder more than $245 million in cryptocurrency. The enterprise was formed through connections on online gaming platforms and operated from no later than October 2023 through at least May 2025, per court documents. Lam entered the plea before Judge Colleen Kollar-Kotelly; a status hearing is set for Dec. 8, with no sentencing date announced. The case traces to the theft of more than 4,100 Bitcoin from a Washington, D.C. resident.

Protocols Seven Years, Then Ethereum

Harmony Proposes Shutting Down Its Layer 1 and Migrating ONE to Ethereum

Seven years after launching its mainnet, Ethereum-compatible layer-1 Harmony has proposed sunsetting its blockchain and migrating its native ONE token to Ethereum. The plan calls for a final network snapshot, ERC-20 ONE tokens issued on Ethereum and migrated exchange listings, with an airdrop to the same addresses and no claims required. Multisig safes, liquidity pools and onchain applications cannot be migrated, and users were urged to exit all smart contracts before Sept. 10. Validators may begin shutting down that day, with $1.372 million set aside to compensate those who stop on time, retain stakes and serve as governors.
