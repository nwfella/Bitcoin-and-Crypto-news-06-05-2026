Congress Moves to Write the Bitcoin Reserve Into Law

A bill locking the federal government's Bitcoin into statute clears the House Financial Services Committee 28–21 — as the CFTC sends crypto rulemaking to the White House for review and the SEC declares it will act with or without the Senate.

By The Daily Cryptograph Staff | September 20, 2026

The American Reserve Modernization Act of 2026 — H.R. 8957 — passed the US House Committee on Financial Services in a 28–21 vote, carrying Washington a step closer to making Bitcoin a lasting line item in the federal balance sheet. The bill would establish a Strategic Bitcoin Reserve and a separate Digital Asset Stockpile inside the Department of the Treasury, holding federally owned Bitcoin alongside other digital assets seized through criminal or civil forfeiture. It was introduced on May 21 by Representative Nicholas Begich, who framed the measure as an accounting and custody problem rather than a monetary one.

"We cannot allow Bitcoin to be held by the federal government to languish in fragmented and inconsistent custody," Begich said. "It poses unacceptable cybersecurity risks and fails to give an adequate accounting of what the federal government actually owns." The United States is estimated to hold 324,527 BTC, worth about $24.7 billion at the time of writing, according to Arkham Intelligence. Under ARMA, coins placed in the reserve would have to be held for a minimum of twenty years — a statutory lock rather than an executive preference.

The bill couples that lock to disclosure. Every federal agency would be required to produce a full accounting of digital assets held or controlled, with quarterly "proof of reserve" reports and third-party audits written into the text. The practical effect is to convert a presidential executive order into permanent law, so that a future administration could not simply disperse the holdings by memorandum. It is the most durable piece of Bitcoin legislation to reach this stage in the United States.

It arrives the same week the regulators stopped waiting for Congress entirely. The Commodity Futures Trading Commission submitted a rulemaking titled "Regulation Crypto Asset Transactions and Regulation Crypto Asset Markets" to the White House Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs on Sept. 17, where it sits at the early "prerule" stage. The filing followed the Senate's Sept. 15 failure to advance the CLARITY Act. A day after that vote, CFTC Chair Michael Selig said the agency was "locked in and ready to ship" rules under existing statutory authority; SEC Chair Paul Atkins said the securities regulator would proceed "with or without legislation." Both agencies acted the following day — the CFTC issuing a no-action position for passive software providers, the SEC granting temporary exemptions for platforms facilitating onchain trading of tokenized securities. Thirteen coins on our tape, twelve of them red: the architecture is being legislated while the market catches its breath.

Circle Opens Arc, Where the Dollar Pays the Toll

Infrastructure September 20, 2026

Circle has launched the mainnet of Arc, a layer-1 blockchain built for stablecoin payments and financial markets, and in particular for agentic transactions. Arc uses USDC as its native gas asset, offers EVM compatibility and advertises deterministic sub-second settlement finality. The network supports more than 20 fiat stablecoins — USDC, EURC, JPYC, KRW1 and TRYB among them — while tokenized assets including BlackRock's BUIDL and Circle's own USYC are native to the chain. Interoperability with more than 20 blockchains is handled through Circle's Cross-Chain Transfer Protocol and Gateway.

CEO Jeremy Allaire called Arc "the single most significant launch in Circle's history since USDC itself," and the company described the network as stablecoin-native infrastructure for developers and institutions — "programmable money, global markets, and agentic economic activity." The public launch follows an October 2025 testnet in which more than 100 companies participated, including BlackRock, Goldman Sachs, Mastercard and Visa, and a private mainnet that drew more than 100 institutional builders. Arc said it plans to broaden participation in network operations and to explore a transition from Proof of Authority to Proof of Stake in 2027. Circle also completed a genesis mint of 10 billion ARC tokens this week, while cautioning that the mint does not commit it to a public token launch.

Consensys Cuts Itself in Two; MetaMask Walks Alone

Corporate September 20, 2026

Consensys Software Inc., the Ethereum company behind MetaMask, will split into two independent companies, separating the consumer wallet from its institutional blockchain infrastructure business. The separation is expected to close by the end of 2026, with Joe Lubin serving as chairman and CEO of MetaMask and executive chairman of the new Consensys. The infrastructure company will house Linea, Besu and Teku, led by CEO Mike Kriak and President David Cunningham, focused on helping financial institutions deploy tokenization, stablecoins and other onchain financial services.

The two halves are being pulled apart by genuinely different businesses. MetaMask will stay with consumer self-custody while pushing beyond crypto into payments, savings, investing and traditional financial products. The wallet reports more than 100 million downloads across roughly 190 countries and trillions of dollars in transaction volume. "The restructuring reflects increasingly different priorities for its consumer and institutional businesses," the company said — a quiet admission that a wallet that must ship fast and a settlement stack that must be audited slowly no longer belong under one roof.

The CFTC Ships Rules Without Waiting for the Senate

Regulation September 20, 2026

The Commodity Futures Trading Commission has submitted a new regulatory action covering crypto asset transactions and markets for White House review, moving to oversee the sector on its own authority. Per a filing with the Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs, the action — formally titled "Regulation Crypto Asset Transactions and Regulation Crypto Asset Markets" — was received on Sept. 17 and is listed at the "prerule" stage: an early point in the process, well short of a formal proposal. The filing discloses no detail of the planned rule.

Its timing is the message. It lands days after the Senate failed to advance the CLARITY Act on Sept. 15. The day after that vote, CFTC Chair Michael Selig posted that the agency was "locked in and ready to ship" rules using existing statutory authority, and SEC Chair Paul Atkins said the securities regulator would move ahead "with or without legislation." Both agencies took action the next day: the CFTC issued a no-action position for providers of passive software, while the SEC announced temporary exemptions for certain platforms facilitating onchain trading of tokenized securities. Selig had flagged the approach in August, telling the CFTC's Innovation Advisory Committee the agency was prepared to build a crypto asset market regime without new law.

The Perpetual Futures Race Spreads to the Stock Market

Derivatives September 20, 2026

Kalshi has filed to offer perpetual futures tied to individual US stocks, joining Coinbase in the race to carry a crypto-native derivative into traditional equities. The prediction market submitted its proposed rule change to the Securities and Exchange Commission and filed with the Commodity Futures Trading Commission for approval on Friday; the CFTC has not yet ruled. The contracts would carry no preset expiration date, using periodic funding payments between longs and shorts to keep prices tethered to the underlying shares. Kalshi said they would be treated as security futures products and cleared through its CFTC-registered clearinghouse, Kalshi Klear.

The filing arrived the same day Coinbase Derivatives submitted its own proposal for perpetuals tied to roughly 50 to 60 individual stocks, Apple, Microsoft, Tesla and Nvidia among them, classified by the CFTC as single-stock futures. Payward, parent of Kraken, has filed through Bitnomial Exchange with plans to offer the products to US traders on Kraken. Kalshi is not new to the structure: it already lists perpetuals on BTC, ETH, SOL and XRP in the United States after receiving CFTC approval for its Bitcoin contract in May.

✦ Zcash's Institutional Turn: A Three-for-One Split and a 25-Second Block ✦

Grayscale has filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission for a 3-for-1 forward share split of its Zcash ETF, ticker ZCSH. Shareholders at the close of trading on Sept. 28 will receive two additional shares for each one they hold. The mechanics are arithmetic and neutral — ten shares at $300 apiece become thirty at $100, a total of $3,000 either way — but the intent is access: the fund's underlying token has risen roughly 2,800% over the past year, and management judged the price per unit too high a door to walk through. Grayscale said the split will reduce the price per share with a proportionate increase in shares outstanding.

The technical side of the same asset is moving just as deliberately. Zcash developers are targeting Nov. 5 for NU7 mainnet activation after reaching "unanimous agreement" on the upgrade's contents, with a testnet activation set for Oct. 6 and a final decision on mainnet height due Oct. 20. NU7 would cut the target block spacing from 75 seconds to 25 seconds, disable version 4 transactions and integrate the Network Sustainability Mechanism, configured to preserve Zcash's halving schedule — with previously removed supply reintroduced from February 2031. Developer Sean Bowe said the upgrade introduces no new transaction formats and should not significantly affect wallets, though full nodes, indexers and block explorers may need adjustments. ZEC holders backed the change decisively: 98.9% supported keeping the halvings and 99.9% of the ZEC-weighted vote backed faster blocks. The bid behind all this has been institutional — Paradigm co-founder Matt Huang has disclosed an unspecified ZEC purchase, calling the asset a "private complement to Bitcoin," and ZEC traded as high as $1,521 on Friday before this weekend's −4.62% pullback to $1,442.56.

Solana Triples the Ceiling on a Single Transaction

Protocol

Solana raised its maximum transaction size from 1,232 bytes to 4,096 bytes, a more than threefold increase meant to fit complex operations into one transaction — zero-knowledge proofs, multi-signature transactions and new onchain signature schemes that previously did not fit. The upgrade activated on mainnet at the start of epoch 1,035, around 1:00 am UTC, and introduced the v1 transaction format, which remains fully backward compatible with legacy transactions; protocols wanting the extra room must opt in. The Foundation said the aim is to let developers "do more" with applications, not merely to relieve congestion. It is the latest in a run of throughput changes: slot times were cut from 400ms to 350ms in August, validators approved a doubling of the annual disinflation rate on Aug. 28, and the network minted a record 263,000 new SPL tokens in a single day this month — roughly five times the daily peak of the 2024 memecoin cycle. Pump.fun accounted for 34,184 of the 40,360 tokens issued through launchpads, and earns $1.8 million a day.

Ethereum and Base Part Ways on What a Wallet Is

Governance

Efforts to agree on a shared account abstraction standard broke down last week, and Ethereum and Base will now implement different ones. Derek Chiang, a founding member of Ethlabs and co-author of Ethereum's EIP-8141, said interoperability standards became secondary to each chain's core goals, leaving both to go their separate ways and "putting the burden on wallets." The practical consequence is that wallet developers may have to support separate transaction formats to deliver a consistent experience across networks. Ethereum is advancing Frame Transactions under EIP-8141 as a "headliner" of its Hegotá upgrade, which would bring native account abstraction and a path toward post-quantum authentication; Base is building native account abstraction via Keystore under EIP-8130, now live on devnet. Chiang argued the split is not necessarily a loss — both chains are "free to innovate on AA to the maximal extent in accordance with their own visions" — and framed it as a difference of priorities, with layer-1s optimising for censorship resistance, privacy and security while scalability-focused layer-2s favour a different design.

Celsius Pursues BitMEX Over 6,360 Bitcoin

Litigation

The Celsius bankruptcy estate has sued five BitMEX-linked companies, alleging fraud, market manipulation and wrongful liquidation during the March 2020 crash. The complaint, filed Sept. 12 in the US Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York through estate representative Blockchain Recovery Investment Consortium, names HDR Global Trading, ABS Global Trading, Shine Effort, 100x Holdings and HDR Global Services. The estate claims BitMEX wrongfully liquidated and seized 1,325.84 BTC of Celsius collateral on March 12, 2020, and 5,034.33 BTC from the fund JST the next day, with JST assigning the claims to the estate. It seeks the recovery of Bitcoin worth nearly $490 million at the time of writing. The central allegation is control of the machinery: that BitMEX set the prices triggering liquidations, ran the engine executing them and held the insurance fund that took the proceeds — and that some liquidation sell orders were placed at prices more than 24% below the next-best ask on the platform. The estate points to the timing of BitMEX's March 13, 2020 outage as evidence, arguing that liquidations stopped when the platform went down and Bitcoin then recovered. The suit was filed eleven days before BitMEX is scheduled to cease exchange services on Sept. 23.

A Leveraged Bet on the People Who Buy Bitcoin

Funds

REX Shares and Tuttle Capital Management launched a leveraged fund delivering twice the daily performance of Strive, the publicly traded Bitcoin treasury company and asset manager. The T-REX 2X Long ASST Daily Target ETF began trading Friday under ticker ASSX on Cboe, seeking 200% of Strive's daily share-price move before fees. The fund resets leverage daily, meaning returns over periods longer than one trading session can diverge substantially from twice the underlying performance — and unlike a spot Bitcoin ETF it holds no Bitcoin and does not track its price. REX and Tuttle also offer 2x funds tied to Strategy, BitMine, Cipher Mining, Circle and SharpLink. Strive holds 25,000 BTC, the fifth-largest corporate Bitcoin position among public companies, and financed its latest 469 BTC purchase through sales of SATA, its perpetual preferred stock. Its shares closed Friday at $30.09, up 6.4% and just above the $29.40 average 12-month analyst target.

A "Federal Bank" Called Sky, Priced Five Times Higher

Analysis

Standard Chartered initiated coverage of Sky's SKY token with a $0.325 target for the end of 2028 — fivefold the $0.065 cited in a Friday report. Geoff Kendrick, the bank's global head of digital assets research, likened Sky, the DeFi platform formerly known as MakerDAO, to a "federal bank": it issues stablecoins, sets a governance framework and charges borrowers a wholesale interest rate. Kendrick wrote that Sky returns value to holders primarily through staking rewards, with buybacks a smaller share. Sky ranks as the third-largest stablecoin issuer behind Tether and Circle and the largest issuer of yield-bearing stablecoins; its sUSDS token holds $4.5 billion in total value locked at a 3.6% annual yield. The forecast implies SKY roughly keeps pace with Ether and outperforms Bitcoin through 2028 — the bank sees ETH at $18,000 and BTC at $300,000 by then.
