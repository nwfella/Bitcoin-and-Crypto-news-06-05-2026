Markets Ethereum ETFs

Ethereum ETFs Close Week in Red, End Five-Day Inflow Streak

US-listed spot Ethereum exchange-traded funds logged $70.62 million in net outflows on Friday, ending a five-day inflow streak, according to SoSoValue data. Ethereum funds had seen $211.25 million in net inflows over the previous five sessions from July 17 through Thursday. Despite Friday's reversal, ETH ETFs still posted $103.9 million in net inflows for the week and extended their weekly inflow streak to three straight, having attracted $337.74 million in net inflows so far in July. Spot crypto ETF flows have become one of the market's most closely watched gauges of demand for Bitcoin and Ether through traditional investment products. The reversal followed a similar pattern in Bitcoin ETFs, which ended a seven-day inflow streak on Thursday and recorded another $240.08 million in net outflows on Friday. Bitcoin ETFs also extended their net inflow streak to three consecutive weeks, adding $103.90 million during the week ended Friday. BTC traded just under $64,000 at the time of writing, down from the week's high of $66,892 on Tuesday.

Regulation CLARITY Act

Fidelity Joins Growing Push for Senate Passage of CLARITY Act

Fidelity, the world's third-largest asset manager with $7.1 trillion in managed assets, called on the US Senate to pass the CLARITY Act on Friday, saying clear digital asset regulations are needed to strengthen investor confidence and reinforce US leadership in global crypto markets. The company joins a growing coalition including the Crypto Council for Innovation, the Digital Chamber and the Blockchain Association, which sent a joint letter to Senate Majority Leader John Thune and Minority Leader Chuck Schumer urging them to bring the bill to the floor before the August recess. Coinbase CEO Brian Armstrong has also called for a full Senate floor vote. The CLARITY Act would establish the first comprehensive federal framework for digital assets, replacing what industry leaders describe as a fragmented regulatory system. The legislation requires 60 votes to pass, where Republicans hold a 52-47 majority, but some Democrats argue the ethics provisions do not go far enough to address conflicts of interest.

Exchanges Lawsuit

BitMEX Hit With 623 BTC Lawsuit on Day It Announces Shutdown

BitMEX is facing a proposed class action lawsuit accusing the crypto derivatives platform of fraudulently engineering customer liquidations to seize traders' Bitcoin collateral. BKX Services Inc. and David Namdar filed the complaint in the US District Court for the Southern District of New York, alleging losses of a combined 622.66 BTC through forced liquidations. The lawsuit revives long-running allegations about the platform's internal trading operations and its liquidation engine, claiming an internal trading desk had access to private customer information and could continue trading during server freezes that prevented ordinary users from accessing or closing their positions. "BitMEX deliberately developed a system that profited from the liquidations," the plaintiffs alleged. The lawsuit was filed on the same day BitMEX announced it would close after 11 years of operation, ceasing services on Sept. 23. BitMEX called the claims "opportunistic" and said it would vigorously defend itself.

Crime North Korea

North Korea Arrests Bank Hacking Ring Tied to Crypto Laundering

North Korean authorities have reportedly arrested a group of former state cyber operators and IT specialists accused of hacking two state banks and laundering stolen funds through cryptocurrency. South Korean outlet Daily NK reported Thursday, citing an anonymous source in Pyongyang, that the group allegedly hacked the internal networks of North Korea's central bank and the Foreign Trade Bank, converting stolen state funds into cryptocurrency before laundering them through China-based brokers. Cointelegraph could not independently verify the report. If confirmed, the arrests would mark a rare case of North Korean cyber operators being accused of stealing from their own government's financial institutions. Pyongyang is widely accused of directing state-backed hacking groups to steal from crypto companies to generate revenue and circumvent international sanctions.

Mining Bankruptcy

Bitcoin Mining Pool Poolin Files for Chapter 11 Bankruptcy

Singapore-based Bitcoin mining pool Poolin, once the world's largest mining pool in 2019, has filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in a New Jersey court. The filing shows estimated liabilities of $100 million to $500 million, assets of $1 million to $10 million, and 10,001 to 25,000 creditors. Poolin now ranks as the 17th largest mining pool operator by hashrate with just 0.2% market share. The company is seeking court approval to sell two West Texas mining sites — Tarbush and Pyote — to Thor CALAP LLC under a proposed $52 million stalking-horse bid, including $37 million for the Tarbush assets and $15 million for the Pyote site. The proposed sale would be subject to a court-supervised auction. Bitcoin mining operations face growing financial constraints from rising electricity costs, with many pivoting to AI and high-performance computing. Hut 8 and IREN recently announced major AI infrastructure deals totaling over $12 billion.

Sanctions European Union

EU Authorities Include HTX Exchange in Russia Sanctions Package

The European Union has listed cryptocurrency exchange HTX, formerly Huobi Global, as part of a package of sanctions targeting Russia amid the country's war on Ukraine. In a Thursday decision, the European Council amended its previous measures to include HTX in a list of 18 entities "providing crypto-assets services or payment services established outside of the Union that are significantly frustrating the purpose of the prohibitions" against Russia. HTX has previously told Cointelegraph that "regulatory compliance remains [its] absolute top priority." The sanctions came the same day EU officials announced they would prohibit Belarusian nationals and residents from owning, controlling or managing crypto exchanges and digital asset service providers under the MiCA framework. The UK government imposed similar sanctions on HTX in May over reasonable grounds to suspect the exchange supported Russia's government.

✦ Hyperliquid's RWA Markets Become Platform's Largest Trading Category ✦

Tokenized real-world asset (RWA) trading became Hyperliquid's largest trading category for the first time, with RWAs generating $25.1 billion in volume from July 13–19 — a full 52% of the DEX's total weekly volume of $48.2 billion. "Hyperliquid's RWA market alone was larger than the combined crypto perpetual volume of every other DEX," wrote ARK Invest analyst Lorenzo Valente. Over the past month, RWA holders grew 32% to 1.25 million users while total tokenized RWA value rose 3.5% to $36.7 billion. Circle CEO Jeremy Allaire called the trend a "major structural shift" in crypto markets, moving away from speculating on endogenous digital commodities. HYPER generated $7.6 million in weekly revenue, ranking third behind Tether and Circle. The milestone signals that institutional-grade tokenized assets — ranging from US Treasury bills to private credit — are increasingly displacing traditional crypto perpetual swaps as the primary driver of on-chain trading volume.
