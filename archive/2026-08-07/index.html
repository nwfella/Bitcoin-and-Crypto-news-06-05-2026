Markets Macro — Payrolls Miss

Payrolls Plunge Flips Fed Bets to a Pause — Bitcoin, Stocks, All of It Rallies

The US economy lost 23,000 jobs in July, per Bureau of Labor Statistics data, with the unemployment rate holding at 4.1% — and the BLS also slashed its May and June figures by a combined 103,000. Traders immediately read the weak print as a green light for policy softening: CME's FedWatch Tool now shows markets expecting the Federal Reserve to hold rates at September's meeting, where yesterday's majority odds had favored a 0.25% hike.

The S&P 500 opened 0.5% higher while the Nasdaq added just over 1%, and BTC/USD hit $65,340 on Bitstamp, up 1.3% on the day and a new month-to-date high. Sygnum Bank CIO Fabian Dori noted the print cuts both ways: "An orderly slowdown supports the liquidity relief case, while a print weak enough to raise growth concerns can still pressure risk assets even as rate odds move."

QCP Capital described the macro picture as "uncertain" for Bitcoin, but praised the tape: "For crypto, the week's price action points to resilience rather than clear directional confirmation." The Coldcard exploit fallout and corporate BTC sales, QCP noted, sparked only "limited demand for panic protection" on options markets.

Security The Coldcard Reckoning

Coldcard Exploit Pushes July Losses to $247M, Second-Worst Month of 2026

Hackers stole $247.4 million in crypto during July — the most this year after April's $644 million haul, and more than triple June's $75 million — according to DefiLlama data. The month's biggest single exploit was the Coldcard wallet compromise: at least $100 million in Bitcoin taken from 7,300 wallets across three confirmed attack waves, per Galaxy Digital, which also flagged a suspected fourth wave that could push the total toward $130 million.

"July showed that even cold storage does not eliminate technological risks, which can put thousands of wallets at risk simultaneously," research platform CryptoRank said. The month's other notable exploits included a $9 million hack of Bonzo Lend, $24 million stolen from Arbitrum-based perpetual exchange AFX, $7.5 million via the Verus Ethereum Bridge and $2.6 million from Cardano wallet SecondFi.

Regulation CLARITY Delayed

Senate Pushes CLARITY Act Vote to September as August Recess Looms

Senate Republican leaders are expected to leave for their August recess without voting on the CLARITY Act, delaying consideration of the crypto market-structure bill until at least September, according to a report from Politico. Senate Majority Leader John Thune confirmed the chamber would not vote before recess, citing Democratic opposition: "The Dems are insistent on no Clarity vote," he said, adding that Senator Lummis "was great" and the bill is "getting queued up first thing when we come back."

The postponement leaves one of the industry's top legislative priorities unresolved and compresses the Senate's timetable — without sufficient Democratic support, Republicans may struggle to reach the 60 votes generally needed to overcome a filibuster.

Industry Asia's Opening

CLARITY Delay Gives Asian Financial Hubs an Opening: First Digital CEO

The Senate's delay of a vote on crypto market structure legislation could give Hong Kong and Singapore more time to strengthen their positions as digital asset hubs, according to First Digital founder and CEO Vincent Chok, whose firm issues the FDUSD stablecoin. "Markets can adapt to slower timelines, but what they struggle with is prolonged uncertainty," Chok said, arguing the postponement leaves institutions without clear rules on market structure, custody and oversight.

He said regulatory progress outside the US would continue regardless of the CLARITY Act's timetable: "For Asia, this delay gives regional financial hubs like Hong Kong and Singapore additional time to demonstrate that clear regulation can coexist with innovation."

ETF Custody Debate

Bitcoin ETF Inflows Surge After Coldcard Hack, but Link Is Unclear: Bloomberg Analyst

US spot Bitcoin ETFs — including BlackRock's IBIT, Fidelity's FBTC, Bitwise's BITB and ARK 21Shares' ARKB — have attracted inflows every trading day since the weekend hack, totaling roughly $620 million. Bloomberg analyst Eric Balchunas stressed there is no evidence connecting the two events: "I'm not saying it's connected, we just don't know. [Although] long-term I can't imagine there aren't some who migrate over."

The Coldcard exploit stole more than $116 million in Bitcoin from over 5,200 wallet addresses, per TRM Labs, reviving the custody debate — Binance co-founder Changpeng "CZ" Zhao argued centralized exchanges may now be "statistically safer" than self-custody, while acknowledging many self-custody losses go unreported.

✦ Analysis: Ethereum's Staking Showdown — EIP-8363 ✦

A group of six Ethereum researchers and developers, including Ethereum Foundation's Justin Drake, has proposed the "Tapered Issuance Burn" (EIP-8363) — a draft that would burn an increasing fraction of validators' consensus rewards as staked ETH approaches a fixed threshold of 60.25 million ETH (around 50% of supply), hitting a 100% deduction at the threshold and phasing in over 18 months. Author Jérôme de Tychey argues unchecked issuance is a dilution tax: "Ever-growing issuance is a dilution tax on every holder: stake, or be diluted."

The backlash came fast. Aave founder Stani Kulechov said cutting rewards "doesn't achieve the outcome it tries to achieve and is actually hurtful for Ethereum," while Ether.Fi CEO Mike Silagadze warned it "will self evidently push out solo stakers... It will essentially guarantee that the only ones staking are large centralized entities." Grayscale research head Zach Pandl has backed the direction, saying limiting staking incentives would be "positive for the price of Ether over time." The proposal is not approved or scheduled for the Hegotá upgrade, which community organizer Trent Van Epps says is likely to reach mainnet in Q2 2027.
