Bitcoin Slips Under $84,000, Then Climbs Back as the Ten-Year Yield Sets a Nineteen-Year High

The US 10-year Treasury closed at 5.11% — its highest since 2007 — and reached 5.13% intraday, dragging Bitcoin to $83,200 in Asian hours before the coin recovered above $84,000 into the American session. Eleven of the thirteen names on our tape are green tonight, led by Cardano and Chainlink, with only Zcash and TRON lower.

By The Daily Cryptograph Staff | September 24, 2026

Bitcoin fell below $84,000 during Asian trading hours on Thursday and slipped to $83,200 after the US 10-year Treasury yield climbed above 5.1% — its highest level since 2007. The benchmark closed Wednesday at 5.11%, up from 4.96% the prior session, and reached 5.13% intraday; CME attributed the bond selloff partly to stronger US business data and rising oil prices. Rising yields offer investors better returns on government debt and raise borrowing costs, a combination that has historically weighed on risk assets. By our press time the coin had climbed back to $84,388, up 0.88% over twenty-four hours and some $318 above the $84,070 this paper carried yesterday morning.

The tape has turned green in eleven of thirteen names. Ether added 1.51% to $2,682.57, Solana rose 2.31% to $116.23, XRP firmed 0.61% to $1.51 and Cardano led the field at $0.2492, up 5.60%. Chainlink gained 5.31% to $12.78, Dogecoin 4.22% to $0.0956, Stellar 3.36% to $0.2084 and BNB 2.88% to $780.03. Hyperliquid changed hands at $93.45 (+1.34%), Monero at $548.44 (+0.04%). The two laggards were the day's former darlings: Zcash at $1,514.90, down 0.94% after a fortnight in which privacy assets ran hardest, and TRON at $0.3389, down 0.11%.

The bond market is the story, and the levels it sets are the ones to watch. James Stanley, senior market analyst for global macro at FOREX.com, wrote Wednesday that "BTC has held up well even with surging rates and a strong USD," and named $82,833 as the next level of consequence should the pullback deepen. The US Treasury announced a $6 billion ceiling for its Thursday buyback of bonds with twenty to thirty years remaining, part of an expanded program meant to improve liquidity in long-dated debt. Odds of a Federal Reserve hike in October have reached roughly 70%, a policy path that would ordinarily sit badly with an asset that pays no coupon.

And yet the flow evidence keeps arguing the other way. US spot Bitcoin ETFs drew about $347 million on Wednesday, a fifth consecutive positive session worth roughly $2.65 billion in aggregate, even as the coin sat below $84,000. Bitwise reported that none of the fifteen institutions it interviewed cut crypto allocations through a drawdown of about 50%, and that none named falling prices among the reasons they would sell. The two readings are not in conflict so much as in sequence: an institutional bid building underneath the market, a leveraged one being flushed out above it. The line that separates them remains $82,833.

Brussels Is Asked to Write Rules for Lending What Nobody Can See

Regulation September 24, 2026

The European Banking Authority has asked the European Commission to consider bringing crypto lending inside the MiCA framework, including the case of firms that broker their clients' access to DeFi lending protocols. In its response to the Commission's targeted consultation, the regulator recommended a cost-benefit analysis of legislative changes that would add intermediating crypto borrowing and lending to the list of regulated services — and, beyond that, a set of conditions: suitability tests for users, leverage limits, fuller disclosure, and the possibility of restricting access to lending involving asset-referenced or e-money tokens that require MiCA authorization.

The most consequential suggestion is a certification regime for DeFi lending protocols themselves, a structure supervisors have so far avoided because it implies a register of code. The EBA said borrowing and lending activity has been identified in at least 16 EU member states, and that easier access to DeFi through crypto firms and artificial intelligence tools is blurring the line between centralized and decentralized finance — precisely the boundary the existing rulebook draws. The recommendations sit inside the Commission's wider review of MiCA, which also takes in stablecoin rules, asset classification and reporting.

IBM Wires the Bank Vault to the Swift Ledger

Infrastructure September 24, 2026

IBM has added beta connectivity between its Digital Asset Haven platform and Swift's blockchain-based ledger for tokenized deposits, plus a beta option for banks to run the platform inside their own data centres. The new messaging adapter lets institutions instruct tokenized deposit transactions using ISO 20022, the global payments standard, so banks can use the formats and compliance processes they already run instead of building blockchain-specific workflows. Institutions taking part in Swift's program have tested tokenized deposits on the ledger through the platform, IBM said.

Swift built the ledger with more than 40 financial institutions and launched it in July with 17 banks, among them HSBC, Citi, BNP Paribas, UBS and Standard Chartered; HSBC and Standard Chartered completed the first live cross-border transaction on it in August. The on-premises variant runs on IBM Z and LinuxONE infrastructure without public cloud services, keeping key management behind the bank's own walls. "The financial services industry is entering a new era where tokenized and traditional assets will need to move side by side," said Tom McPherson, general manager of IBM Z and LinuxONE.

Albany Sues Polymarket, and the States Draw Their Line

Litigation September 24, 2026

New York Attorney General Letitia James sued Polymarket on Thursday, alleging the platform's prediction market event contracts violate state gambling law. The filing points to the sporting event contracts on Polymarket's mobile app, launched in December 2025, and to the company's advertising to New Yorkers; state officials said they could also seek to block the platform for residents. "[Polymarket] seeks to avoid the legal and financial consequences of New York's close regulation of gambling by offering what is quintessentially wagering under the guise of 'event contracts' on a 'prediction market,'" the filing reads.

It is the latest move by state authorities against a federal claim of exclusive oversight. CFTC Chair Michael Selig has said the agency holds that jurisdiction; New York's action follows a similar suit against Kalshi in July and litigation over platforms run by Coinbase and Gemini in April. New Jersey has already petitioned the US Supreme Court to take up its own Kalshi case, and the court has not said whether it will.

A Commissioner Explains Why the Cases Were Dropped

Policy September 24, 2026

Mark Uyeda, who served as acting chair of the SEC from January to April 2025, said the agency abandoned civil cases against crypto companies because it was preparing a "180-degree change" in rulemaking and could not have its litigators argue the old position in court. "I'm not about to have our litigators... stand up in court and have a commission interpretation be issued that is a 180-degree change from what they'd been arguing for that court," he said. "I think that hurts [our] credibility as an agency."

Uyeda was speaking at the Psaros Center for Financial Markets and Policy's Financial Markets Quality Conference. Under his tenure the commission dropped actions against Kraken, Ripple Labs, Coinbase and others — steps many critics read as repayment for industry support of the president's 2024 campaign. He added that there had been "significant concerns" over whether the cases were "justifiable under law." Uyeda has been a commissioner since 2022 and now serves alongside Chair Paul Atkins and Commissioner Hester Peirce, whose expected departure in November would leave the five-member panel with two sitting members and no announced nominations to fill it.

✦ The Last Resort Gets Cheap Enough to Use ✦

Two weeks ago the idea of quantum-proofing a Bitcoin transaction without touching the network's consensus rules was a $320 curiosity. After a weeklong optimization contest, StarkWare says the estimated compute cost of preparing such a transaction has fallen below $67 in benchmark testing — a reduction of 79%. The first such transaction, mined on Aug. 26 and submitted through MARA's Slipstream service, consumed roughly 3,100 GPU-hours across about 100 GPUs, excluding network fees.

The design, published in April by StarkWare researcher Avihu Levy, layers hash-based protection over existing Bitcoin outputs so that a holder can move funds defensively if a quantum threat ever materializes — no fork, no new opcodes. Levy himself called it a "last resort measure" at the time, and still argues the durable fix belongs at the protocol level. That caution matters: the new number comes from a benchmark, not from mainnet, and a single cheap transaction is not the same as a network that can migrate.

Washington Weighs Exporting the Dollar, Digitally

Policy

The Trump administration is reportedly considering an overseas push for dollar-backed stablecoins, an effort to reinforce the dollar's reserve status — and, with it, demand for US Treasurys, the reserve asset most stablecoin issuers hold. Bloomberg reported Wednesday that the government could support projects through joint ventures with private firms, involving the Treasury Department, the State Department and the US International Development Finance Corporation. The timing is competitive: China's digital yuan is among the currencies used in Project mBridge for cross-border CBDC transactions, and the European Central Bank is preparing a twelve-month digital euro pilot expected to begin in the second half of 2027. Cointelegraph sought comment from the Treasury, the DFC and several US stablecoin issuers and did not receive a response before publication.

Through a 50% Drawdown, Nobody Sold

Institutions

Not one of the fifteen institutions Bitwise interviewed cut its crypto allocation during a drawdown of roughly 50%, and several bought more. Every institution holding crypto owned Bitcoin, usually as its largest and longest-held position, with Ether and Solana held as smaller, shorter-horizon bets. Asked what would prompt a sale, none cited price: they named a regulatory reversal, a sector-wide credibility crisis, or a failure of the investment thesis; some said they would exit ETH or SOL if network growth stopped accruing to the tokens. Allocations ran from 0.5% to 13% of investable assets, most between 1% and 2%, and nearly all institutions either use or plan to use spot crypto ETFs.

Onchain Settlement, Behind the Cashier's Back

Payments

SoFi has begun settling debit and credit card transactions with Mastercard using its SoFiUSD stablecoin, migrating a card program expected to process more than $25 billion in annualized volume onto blockchain rails. A SoFi spokesperson told Cointelegraph the change does not remove intermediaries so much as add an alternative settlement rail; customers keep swiping as before while the money between institutions moves around the clock. The pattern is spreading at the network level — Visa said in April its stablecoin settlement pilot had reached a $7 billion annualized run rate across nine blockchains, calling it a "viable complement to traditional settlement rails."

A Deadline for Old Zcash Wallets

Protocols

Zcash users holding ZEC in the legacy Sprout shielded pool are being urged to move it before the planned NU7 upgrade. Under Zcash Improvement Proposal-2003, the upgrade stops accepting the older transaction format that allows Sprout funds to be spent, making anything left behind unspendable. The proposal does not burn those coins and leaves open the possibility of restoring access, but warns holders may never be able to spend them. NU7 is scheduled for testnet activation on Oct. 6, with a decision on the targeted Nov. 5 mainnet activation expected Oct. 20. The upgrade would also cut target block times from 75 seconds to 25.

Casino States Ask the Justices to Step In

Litigation

The National Council of Legislators from Gaming States filed an amicus brief urging the Supreme Court to take up New Jersey's case against Kalshi, arguing that a ruling for the platform would "[render] states powerless" over sports betting conducted through prediction markets. "Casinos, pari-mutuel operators, and other heavily regulated entities are certain to amend their business and products to seek the same status," the lawmakers wrote, warning that state regulatory regimes "will have to be reconsidered." New Jersey petitioned for certiorari on Sept. 2 after a Third Circuit decision; Kalshi, which has told Cointelegraph it cannot be "regulated by 50 different regulators," has until Nov. 9 to respond. The court has not said whether it will hear the case.
