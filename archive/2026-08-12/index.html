ETF Wall Street — The $2.25B Print

Goldman Sachs to Acquire ETF Manager NEOS in $2.25B Deal

Goldman Sachs has agreed to acquire exchange-traded fund manager NEOS Investments for up to $2.25 billion, a deal that would fold NEOS' $30 billion portfolio of 19 options-based income ETFs — including the Bitcoin High Income ETF (BTCI), Boosted Bitcoin High Income ETF (XBCI) and Ethereum High Income ETF (NEHI) — into Goldman Sachs Asset Management.

The acquisition is expected to close in the first quarter of 2027, subject to regulatory approval, with NEOS co-founders Troy Cates and Garrett Paolella joining Goldman. Together with its purchase of Innovator Capital Management, the bank's global ETF platform would reach roughly $130 billion in assets, making it the eighth-largest active ETF manager.

Bloomberg ETF analyst Eric Balchunas called the deal a "semi-shock", noting NEOS' rapid growth since its 2022 founding — even as Goldman itself trimmed disclosed crypto ETF exposure in Q1, exiting XRP- and Solana-linked funds while still reporting more than $700 million in Bitcoin ETF holdings.

Ethereum The Staking Amendment

Fidelity Files With SEC to Add Staking to Ethereum ETF

Fidelity Investments plans to add staking to its spot Ether exchange-traded product, the Fidelity Ethereum Fund (FETH), according to its filing with the US Securities and Exchange Commission. The asset manager said FETH could stake up to 100% of its Ether under normal conditions, excluding ETH reserved for redemptions, expenses and liquidity needs.

The fund would retain 85% of staking rewards, with 15% going toward staking fees, and plans quarterly cash distributions — although payouts are not guaranteed. Fidelity expects staking to begin "as soon as practicable" after the prospectus date; the preliminary prospectus remains subject to change before the registration becomes effective.

Seeking Alpha contributor Ryne Mauck argued in May that FETH's lack of staking put it at a "relative disadvantage" to staking-enabled products from Grayscale and BlackRock — a gap today's filing aims to close.

Regulation The Emergency Order

CFTC Invokes Emergency Powers to Keep Kalshi Operating in New York Fight

The US Commodity Futures Trading Commission invoked its emergency authority, ordering prediction market Kalshi to continue operating as New York seeks a temporary restraining order and at least $36 billion in compensatory damages over event contracts. The CFTC argued New York's enforcement action itself constituted a market emergency under the Commodity Exchange Act.

New York's requested TRO would bar Kalshi from offering contracts tied to sports, culture, elections and other events in or from New York — which the CFTC warned could effectively halt all event contracts nationwide because Kalshi is based in the state. The commission said the CEA requires a uniform national derivatives market, with Chair Michael Selig arguing Congress never intended a "patchwork of state gaming laws."

The confrontation is the latest front in a national fight over whether federal derivatives law preempts state gambling rules as applied to event contracts on federally regulated exchanges.

Stablecoins Hong Kong's First HKD Peg

HashKey Begins Beta Distribution of Hong Kong-Regulated HKDAP Stablecoin

Hong Kong's regulated stablecoin market has moved from licensing to distribution: HashKey Exchange became an authorized distributor for Anchorpoint Financial's Hong Kong dollar-backed HKDAP stablecoin, opening beta access to eligible institutions and professional investors — and has already completed its first minting and redemption transaction, including fiat on- and off-ramping.

HKDAP — short for "HKD At Par" — is issued by Anchorpoint, a joint venture of Standard Chartered Bank (Hong Kong), HKT and Animoca Brands, one of the first firms licensed by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority to issue stablecoins. HashKey and Anchorpoint plan to broaden distribution and explore cross-border payments, settlement and tokenized finance.

Citi has estimated Hong Kong dollar stablecoin circulation could eventually reach $16 billion, though current adoption data remains limited.

✦ Magazine: The Fake Startup That Fooled North Korean IT Workers ✦

It isn't often a reporter gets asked to pose as a venture capitalist to fool suspected North Korean IT workers — but in June, Cointelegraph joined a Zoom call as "Aelin Ashriver," an investor from the fictitious Definitive Communications, meeting the developers of crypto startup Ballena Azul. In reality, the suspected workers had spent weeks inside a fake company built by cybersecurity researchers Mauro Eldritch (BCA LTD) and Heiner García (Telefónica Tech/NorthScan) purely to study their methods and infrastructure.

The five-week ruse exposed external servers the workers used as intermediary points — infrastructure linked to InvisibleFerret and BeaverTail/OtterCookie malware families tied to North Korean credential- and wallet-stealing campaigns, including some "totally new" servers with zero prior intelligence. The operation also showed the group leaning on AI: ChatGPT for writing and coding, Google Gemini for image alteration and document forgery. The US Treasury said North Korean IT worker schemes generated nearly $800 million in 2024 to fund the Pyongyang regime's weapons programs.
