The Senate Says No: CLARITY Falls 49-50, and the Industry Turns to the Regulators

Eleven votes short of cloture, the market-structure bill stalls in the upper chamber — and Bernstein tells clients to expect the SEC and CFTC to fill the gap with "aggressive and swift" rulemaking of their own.

By The Daily Cryptograph Staff | September 16, 2026

The Senate failed to advance the Digital Asset Market Clarity (CLARITY) Act on Tuesday, voting 49-50 on a motion to invoke cloture — well short of the 60 needed to bring the market-structure bill to the floor. Democrats raised concerns over President Donald Trump's crypto investments, and with limited legislative time remaining before a new Congress is sworn in, the bill's prospects for this year are in doubt. Crypto markets declined around the vote, with Bitcoin dropping below $76,000 as the motion failed. The measure would have divided federal oversight of digital assets between the Securities and Exchange Commission and the Commodity Futures Trading Commission, and would have rewritten the treatment of stablecoin yield and the Blockchain Regulatory Certainty Act.

Industry leaders pointed almost immediately to agency rulemaking as the next best source of clarity. Bernstein analysts told clients Wednesday they expect "aggressive and swift" rulemaking from the SEC and CFTC to "make up for the time lost negotiating the CLARITY Act," flagging token taxonomy for raising capital, developer protections for DeFi and self-custodial protocols, innovation exemptions for equity tokenization, faster approvals for real-world asset perpetual futures, and revisions to federal sports-contract rules. Ripple CEO Brad Garlinghouse said regulators will "continue to work hard to issue rules to fill the legislative gap." Not everyone is comforted: NEAR chief legal officer Abhishek Vaidyanathan warned that rejecting the bill "leaves firms completely dependent on agency guidance and ongoing administrative discretion," with 2027 budgets priced in regulatory uncertainty.

The market's read is precise about who hurt most. Saxo Bank strategist Ruben Dalfovo called Coinbase the most directly exposed name, since market-structure rules determine registration requirements, tradable assets and who may participate. Shares of Coinbase, Circle and Strategy fell between 5% and 10% after the procedural vote and slid a further 2% to 6% early Wednesday. Polymarket traders had already priced the outcome: odds of the bill becoming law this year spiked to 35% on a revised Republican offer with expanded ethics provisions, then collapsed to 16% as key Democrats said the text was not "near enough."

Four Hundred Fifty Million Leaves the Bitcoin ETFs in a Day

Markets September 16, 2026

Spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds recorded their largest day of outflows since late June after the market-structure bill stalled in the Senate. The 13 US-listed funds shed a net $450.4 million on Tuesday, having attracted $159.9 million on Monday, according to Farside data — the biggest single-day exit since June 24, when the funds lost $469 million as a technology-stock selloff pressured risk assets.

The distribution is instructive. Fidelity's FBTC took the largest hit at $214.8 million, followed by $161.7 million from BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust, $44.1 million from Grayscale's GBTC, $17.4 million from the ARK 21Shares Bitcoin ETF and $12.4 million from Bitwise's fund — a broad retreat rather than a single manager's problem. Bitcoin traded at $75,700 at the time of writing, down 2.5% over 24 hours, and our own tape this morning shows $75,765, off 0.91%.

Circle Opens Arc, and the Stablecoin Pays the Gas

Stablecoins September 16, 2026

USDC issuer Circle has launched the mainnet of Arc, a layer-1 blockchain aimed at stablecoin payments and financial markets — with particular attention to agentic transactions. Arc uses USDC as its native gas asset and offers Ethereum Virtual Machine compatibility alongside deterministic sub-second settlement finality, according to an Arc blog post published Wednesday.

The asset shelf is unusually wide for a launch. Arc supports more than 20 fiat stablecoins, including USDC, EURC, JPYC, KRW1 and TRYB, while tokenized assets such as BlackRock's BUIDL and Circle's USYC are available natively, and Circle's Cross-Chain Transfer Protocol connects it to more than 20 other blockchains. CEO Jeremy Allaire called Arc "the single most significant launch in Circle's history since USDC itself," describing infrastructure built for "programmable money, global markets, and agentic economic activity" — a phrase that reads as a bet on machine-to-machine payments. The public testnet debuted in October 2025 with more than 100 companies participating, among them BlackRock, Goldman Sachs, Mastercard and Visa.

Zcash Holders Choose Speed: Twenty-Five Seconds to a Block

Privacy September 16, 2026

Zcash token holders have backed cutting the network's target block time to 25 seconds from 75 seconds and preserving the existing halving schedule, in a coinholder poll on the next major upgrade. The faster-block proposal drew 99.9% of the ZEC-weighted vote, while 98.9% supported keeping halvings, according to results published Monday; voting power reflected eligible ZEC holdings and both percentages include abstentions.

The shorter interval cuts the expected wait for a transaction's first confirmation, and per-block issuance would fall so that scheduled daily issuance stays unchanged — the crucial detail for anyone modeling supply. The changes are proposed for NU7, whose activation date remains undetermined; holders also favored excluding features not implemented by Sept. 30, and the coinholder vote ran separately from polls of ZecHub, the Zcash Community Advisory Panel and other groups. Eligibility was limited to spendable ZEC in the Ironwood shielded pool at the voting snapshot. The market has already voted differently: ZEC $1,248.94, up 11.32% this morning.

Consensys Divides in Two: MetaMask Walks Alone

Industry September 16, 2026

Consensys Software Inc., the Ethereum software company behind MetaMask, plans to split into two independent companies, separating its consumer business from its institutional blockchain infrastructure operations. The separation is expected to complete by the end of 2026, with Joe Lubin serving as chairman and CEO of MetaMask and executive chairman of the new Consensys.

The new Consensys takes the protocols and institutional plumbing — Linea, Besu and Teku — under CEO Mike Kriak and President David Cunningham, focusing on Ethereum infrastructure and helping financial institutions deploy onchain. MetaMask keeps consumer self-custody and pushes beyond crypto into payments, savings, investing and traditional financial products, leaning on more than 100 million downloads across roughly 190 countries and trillions of dollars in transaction volume. The company said the restructuring reflects increasingly different priorities for its consumer and institutional businesses — the same divergence now reshaping much of the industry.

Solana Triples Transaction Size to 4,096 Bytes

Protocol

Solana raised its maximum transaction size from 1,232 bytes to 4,096 bytes, letting developers fit more complex operations into a single transaction — zero-knowledge proofs, multi-signature transactions and new onchain signature schemes that previously could not fit. The upgrade activated on mainnet Tuesday at the start of epoch 1,035, around 1:00 am UTC, per Solana Foundation data, and introduced a v1 transaction format that keeps full backward compatibility: legacy transactions still work, but protocols wanting the size increase must update. It follows an August reduction of slot time from 400 to 350 milliseconds, and an Aug. 28 validator vote to double the network's annual disinflation rate.

The House Tax Bill Leaves Miners and Stakers Waiting

Policy

The House Ways and Means Committee will consider a 114-page crypto tax package Wednesday that omits a provision letting miners and stakers defer taxation of rewards until the tokens are sold. The Digital Asset Tax Certainty Act, H.R. 10357, published alongside the markup notice Monday, leaves out the reward-timing language from Representative Mike Carey's Tax Clarity for Mining and Staking Act. Without it, mining and staking rewards remain taxable when received or brought under the recipient's control — potentially before they are sold for cash. The bill retains some validator provisions, classifying validator income as ordinary income, determining US sourcing, and letting qualifying investment trusts stake assets.

Tenev: Going Onchain Shouldn't Hand Issuers a Veto

Tokenization

Robinhood CEO Vlad Tenev says companies should not hold veto power over tokenized stock products that do not change shareholder rights, issuer obligations or a company's official stock ledger. In a post on X, he argued the question of issuer consent turns on whether the product alters the rights attached to underlying shares or creates new obligations for the company or its transfer agent — in which case the issuer should be involved. Where the product is a separate instrument holding or referencing freely transferable shares without touching the cap table, consent should not be required. The comments follow AMC Entertainment CEO Adam Aron criticizing Robinhood's tokenized offerings on Sept. 4. "Going onchain shouldn't give the issuer a veto it never had offchain," Tenev wrote.

The BIS Finds a Sixfold Gap in Bitcoin's Onchain Numbers

Research

Researchers at the Bank for International Settlements found that estimates of Bitcoin onchain transfer values can vary by as much as sixfold depending on how transactions are measured — a finding about onchain transfers, not exchange trading volume. Most of the discrepancy comes from Bitcoin's transaction structure: when users spend coins, unspent funds are often returned to the sender as change, which can be counted as another output even though nothing was transferred to a third party. The measurement problem extends to market capitalization, which at times ran as much as four times higher than realized capitalization. "Metrics such as transaction volumes, market capitalisation and total value locked often suggest a degree of accuracy that is not supported by the nature of the underlying data," the researchers wrote.

Ethereum Names Two "Must Ship" Proposals for Hegotá

Protocol

The Ethereum Foundation has identified two "must ship" proposals for the Hegotá upgrade, prioritizing censorship resistance and more flexible account authentication while narrowing the fork's scope. The ranking weighed 62 proposed EIPs with input from roughly 60 researchers and engineers in the Foundation's Protocol cluster — the first time that group has published a unified view rather than separate team opinions. The pair: EIP-7805, FOCIL, and EIP-8141, Frame Transactions. FOCIL gives users a path to have eligible transactions included without relying on centralized block builders; Frame Transactions introduces native account abstraction and a route toward post-quantum authentication. If either is at risk, the Foundation said Hegotá's schedule should adjust before the proposal is dropped.
