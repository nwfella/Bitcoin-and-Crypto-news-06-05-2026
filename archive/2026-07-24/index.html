Bitcoin 'Plunge Protection Team' Returns as BTC Slides Under $64,000

Bitcoin (BTC) fell more than 1.6% on Friday as its latest price correction accelerated after Wall Street opened, approaching the $64,000 mark. Trading firm Mosaic Asset Company said rising US Treasury yields were a key driver of the sell-off, noting that the two-year yield at 4.31% sits "well above the Federal Reserve's target range." The CME FedWatch Tool shows markets now pricing in a 0.25% hike in September as one of two increases expected before the end of 2026. Meanwhile, crypto trader Killa observed that a Binance "plunge protection team" reemerged with bid liquidity to avoid a deeper BTC price rout, repeating a familiar short-term trading pattern. Geopolitical tensions and macroeconomic headwinds continued to weigh on risk assets across the board.

RWAs Become Hyperliquid's Largest Trading Category, Surpassing All Others Combined

Tokenized real-world asset (RWA) trading became Hyperliquid's largest trading category for the first time, with RWAs generating $25.1 billion in volume from July 13–19 — a full 52% of the DEX's total weekly volume of $48.2 billion. "Hyperliquid's RWA market alone was larger than the combined crypto perpetual volume of every other DEX," wrote ARK Invest's Lorenzo Valente. Over the past month, RWA holders grew 32% to 1.25 million users, while total tokenized RWA value rose 3.5% to $36.7 billion. Circle CEO Jeremy Allaire called the trend a "major structural shift" in crypto markets, moving away from speculating on endogenous digital commodities. HYPER generated $7.6M in weekly revenue, ranking third behind Tether and Circle.

Strategy-Led Consortium Pledges $15M to Quantum-Proof the Bitcoin Network

Michael Saylor's Strategy, alongside BlackRock, Coinbase, Fidelity, Block, Blockstream, Galaxy, ARK Invest and others, announced the launch of the Bitcoin Security Consortium — pledging $15 million over three years to support developers securing Bitcoin against quantum computing threats. Galaxy Digital separately pledged up to $5 million in grants for quantum-security developers and elected a council of quantum-advisory experts. The timeline of a quantum breakthrough remains hotly debated: Blockstream CEO Adam Back said Bitcoin faces no meaningful quantum threat for 20–40 years, while Bernstein estimates Bitcoin has just three to five years to prepare for a post-quantum security upgrade.

Dem Senator Calls GOP's CLARITY Ethics Proposal a 'Piece of S---' as Debate Intensifies

Democratic Senator Ruben Gallego blasted Senate Republicans' proposed ethics provisions for the Digital Asset Market Clarity (CLARITY) Act, telling Politico: "Whatever piece of shit they sent back to us, that was not a serious effort." The GOP's 616-page bill text includes language barring all federal officials — including President Donald Trump — from issuing or sponsoring any digital asset through Jan. 20, 2029. Gallego said he will work on a counterproposal with Republicans. Meanwhile, Goldman Sachs CEO David Solomon backed the CLARITY Act as "not perfect" but necessary to create a "level playing field to enhance market stability," standing apart from many peers who oppose the bill over stablecoin provisions.

Bitcoin Mining Pool Poolin Files for Chapter 11 Bankruptcy, Seeks $52M Texas Site Sale

Singapore-based Bitcoin mining pool Poolin, once the world's largest in 2019, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in a New Jersey court with estimated liabilities of $100–$500 million. The operator now ranks as the 17th largest mining pool with just 0.2% market share. Poolin is seeking court approval to sell two West Texas mining sites — Tarbush and Pyote — to Thor CALAP LLC under a proposed $52 million stalking-horse bid. The filing comes as Bitcoin miners face growing financial constraints from rising electricity costs, with many operations shifting to AI and high-performance computing to diversify revenue streams.

✦ Home Invasions Surge as Top Crypto 'Wrench Attack' in H1 2026 ✦

Crypto home invasions skyrocketed from just one incident in all of H1 2025 to 20 confirmed cases in H1 2026, making it the most common form of "wrench attack" — physical coercion to obtain crypto assets — according to blockchain security firm CertiK. The firm verified 52 wrench attacks worldwide in the first half of 2026, up 33% year-over-year, with associated financial exposure reaching approximately $124.1 million. France accounted for 33 of the 52 verified incidents — nearly two-thirds of the global total — driven by its visible crypto ecosystem and data linking identities with perceived wealth. French authorities have recorded 77 crypto-linked kidnappings and extortion cases in H1 2026, up from 45 in all of 2025, and have launched a dedicated prevention platform with 200 arrests already made. CertiK recommends multisignature arrangements, geographically separated signers, and spending limits as countermeasures.
