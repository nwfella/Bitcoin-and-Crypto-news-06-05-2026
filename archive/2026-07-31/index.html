594 BTC Swept in 25 Minutes as Coldcard
Seed-Entropy Flaw Is Exposed

No phishing, no leaked seeds, no stolen devices — the attacker simply recomputed the victims' wallets. Coinkite now warns that Coldcard seeds generated from March 2021 onward were born weak, and fixed firmware cannot repair what was already generated

By Ezra Reguerra & The Daily Cryptograph Staff | July 31, 2026

☠ If You Use a Coldcard — Do This Now
  • Assume exposure if your seed was generated on any Coldcard (Mk2/Mk3/Mk4/Mk5/Q) after March 2021 — unless you entered at least 50 independent, private dice rolls or protected the wallet with a strong, unique BIP-39 passphrase.
  • Updating firmware does NOT repair existing seeds. It only fixes new ones. You must migrate funds to a brand-new seed.
  • Migrate calmly: install fixed firmware (Mk3 → 4.2.0 · Mk4/Mk5 → 5.6.0 or 6.6.0X Edge · Q → 1.5.0Q or 6.6.0QX Edge), generate a new seed, verify the backup and a receive address on the device screen, send a small test transaction, then move the rest.
  • A bad seed stays bad even if restored into a Trezor or Ledger — the weakness lives in the numbers, not the hardware.

Coldcard Issues Mk3 Warning as Experts Examine $38M Bitcoin Wallet Drain

In the small hours of Thursday, July 30, an automated operation swept 1,324 unspent transaction outputs from 500 single-signature Bitcoin addresses within a three-block window, moving 594.48 BTC — roughly $38.3 million — in about 25 minutes. AnchorWatch CEO Rob Hamilton, who documented the sweep, noted that 562 BTC was later consolidated into a single address and that every affected address was single-signature: "At a glance, this looks like there was flawed entropy in wallet generation somewhere along the way."

By end of day, Canadian hardware maker Coinkite had confirmed the fear. Its Mk3 Security Advisory warned that seeds generated on a Coldcard Mk3 running firmware 4.0.1 (March 2021) or later may put funds at risk. The company's July 31 update extended the scope to every current model: seeds generated on Mk4 and Mk5 before version 5.6.0 (standard) or 6.6.0X (Edge), and Q before 1.5.0Q (standard) or 6.6.0QX (Edge), carry roughly 72 bits of effective entropy instead of the expected 128 bits.

The root cause, per Coinkite's technical backgrounder, is a subtle firmware regression from the March 2021 migration to Bitcoin Core's libsecp256k1: wallet seed generation silently resolved to MicroPython's non-cryptographic software PRNG rather than the STM32 hardware TRNG. On Mk3 devices the effective search space collapsed to roughly 2⁴⁰ — about 1.1 trillion combinations, small enough for a well-resourced attacker to compute every possibility offline. The company assumes AI-assisted review of its open-source firmware history found the flaw.

Wizardsardine CEO Kevin Loaec's hypothesis matches the on-chain fingerprint: a low-entropy random-number generator, exploited by an automated script searching a limited range of BIP-84 derivation paths — which would explain the concentration in native SegWit addresses and the partial drains. He warned that partially drained wallets may remain at risk. Coinkite stressed that seeds used with a strong, unique BIP-39 passphrase face minimal risk — the passphrase, not the PIN — and that users who added 50 or more independent, private dice rolls are similarly protected. "Out of an abundance of caution," affected users are urged to generate a new seed on an unaffected device, verify backup and receive address, send a small test transaction, and only then move remaining funds.

Bank of Italy Finds No Consistent Cost Advantage for Stablecoin Remittances

A Bank of Italy study found that stablecoin-based remittances did not offer a systematic cost or speed advantage over traditional payment channels, as fiat on- and off-ramp frictions accounted for most costs and transfer delays. Researchers tested 200 USDC remittances across 10 bidirectional payment corridors linking Italy with Brazil, Argentina, Japan, the UAE and South Africa, comparing end-to-end costs and settlement times with traditional services. Total costs ranged from 0.3% to nearly 9% depending on the corridor, with transfers settling in under 20 minutes where instant payment systems were available and one to two business days where they were not. Against the World Bank's global average remittance cost of 6.65%, stablecoin transfers were cheaper in most corridors examined, though less expensive than Wise in only three of seven comparable corridors. The authors argued the economic advantages would be substantially higher if stablecoins could be spent directly in the real economy without reconversion into fiat currency.

Tether Earns $1.5B in Q2 as US Treasury Holdings Fuel Profits

Tether's latest quarterly attestation, released Friday, reported a net operating profit of $1.5 billion for the second quarter, driven primarily by interest earned on US Treasury holdings and repurchase agreements. The company also reported a reserve buffer of $4.11 billion as of June 30 — meaning assets exceeded liabilities by that amount. Despite a broader contraction in the stablecoin market, the circulating supply of USDt increased by $446 million during the quarter to $184.6 billion, allowing Tether to maintain more than 60% of the global stablecoin market. The total stablecoin market was valued at roughly $307 billion as of Friday, according to DeFiLlama data. Tether remains one of the world's largest holders of US Treasury securities, which have become the backbone of its reserve portfolio, benefiting from elevated short-term interest rates that boosted income from Treasury bills and related cash-equivalent assets.
