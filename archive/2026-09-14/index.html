Republicans Send Their "Final" CLARITY Act Offer, and the Senate Votes Tuesday

A 635-page rewrite — carrying new ethics strictures the President has agreed to, and 126 changes Democrats asked for — lands two days before a procedural vote that will decide whether the market-structure bill can reach the floor.

By The Daily Cryptograph Staff | September 14, 2026

Senate Republicans on Sunday released revised text of the CLARITY Act aimed at swaying Democrats ahead of a procedural vote on Tuesday, featuring major changes to rules governing government officials' involvement with digital assets. The 635-page proposal was issued by Senate Banking Digital Assets Subcommittee Chair Cynthia Lummis alongside Chairmen John Boozman and Tim Scott, and it also rewrites the Blockchain Regulatory Certainty Act (BRCA) and the provisions governing stablecoin yield. Lummis said the new ethics language had been agreed to by President Donald Trump.

"After a year of intense daily bipartisan negotiations, this bill is ready," she said. "President Trump voluntarily agreed to unprecedented ethics restrictions, holding every federally elected official, judge, and their spouses to some of the toughest ethics restrictions in US history." The revised rules would allow state attorneys general to enforce bans on federal officials issuing, sponsoring or holding significant financial interests in digital assets — and on exchanges that list assets in violation of those bans. Lummis said the text reflects 126 changes made at the request of Democrats across a year of talks, and a Republican aide told reporters on Sunday the proposal is a final offer.

The timing is tight and the arithmetic unforgiving. The procedural vote is set for Tuesday at 2:15 p.m. ET and will determine whether the Senate can advance the measure toward floor consideration. It arrives in the same week as a Federal Reserve decision expected to raise interest rates by a quarter point — a pairing traders are watching closely. Bitcoin began the third week of September below key weekly support levels, having finished last week beneath its 50-week moving average near $77,400, even as a bullish RSI divergence continued to play out.

Twenty-Four Hours to Confess: Brussels Turns the Screw on Wallet Makers

Regulation September 14, 2026

The European Union is telling cryptocurrency hardware and software wallet providers that they have 24 hours from awareness to report actively exploited bugs or severe security vulnerabilities in their products. The measure is part of the bloc's Cyber Resilience Act, which took effect on Friday, according to an announcement from the European Commission.

The deadlines escalate quickly. Manufacturers owe an early warning within 24 hours, a full notification within 72 hours, and a final report 14 days after corrective or mitigating measures are available — or within one month for severe incidents. Non-compliance carries substantial administrative fines. The Commission says the requirements aim to better protect consumers and businesses from cyber threats, and they extend to all products "with digital elements made available in the EU," building on the EU's broader cybersecurity strategy. For an industry whose last two years have been defined by shipping-vendor breaches and firmware scares, the clock now starts the moment a flaw is known.

Bitmine Builds an Annuity Out of Ether: $334 Million a Year

Treasuries September 14, 2026

Bitmine Immersion Technologies added to its Ether holdings last week while projecting hundreds of millions of dollars in annual staking revenue — the clearest illustration yet of how a massive ETH treasury can generate income even through volatile markets. In a Monday announcement the company said it acquired 27,180 ETH, bringing holdings to more than 5.95 million ETH, worth roughly $15.4 billion and about 4.9% of Ether's circulating supply; total holdings including cash and other crypto assets stand near $15.8 billion.

The yield engine is the point. More than 5.06 million ETH is now staked, generating an estimated $334 million in annualized staking revenue at current rates — about 85% of the company's Ether. For comparison, Grayscale's Ethereum Staking ETF (ETHE) has 84.6% of its Ether staked. The structure offers an advantage Bitcoin treasury companies cannot match, since BTC holdings throw off no native staking yield — a distinction worth watching as Ether trades near $2,507. Bitmine shares were little changed on Monday near $25, up nearly 38% over the past month but still down year to date.

Consensys Divides in Two: MetaMask Walks Alone

Industry September 10, 2026

Consensys Software Inc., the Ethereum software company behind MetaMask, plans to split into two independent companies, separating its consumer business from its institutional blockchain infrastructure operations. The separation is expected to complete by the end of 2026, with Joe Lubin serving as chairman and CEO of MetaMask and executive chairman of the new Consensys.

The new Consensys takes the protocols and institutional plumbing — Linea, Besu and Teku — under CEO Mike Kriak and President David Cunningham, focusing on helping financial institutions deploy blockchain for tokenization, stablecoins and other onchain financial services. MetaMask keeps consumer self-custody and pushes beyond crypto into payments, savings, investing and traditional financial products, leaning on a franchise of more than 100 million downloads across roughly 190 countries and trillions of dollars in transaction volume. The company said the restructuring reflects increasingly divergent priorities for its consumer and institutional businesses — the same divergence now reshaping much of the industry.

Threat actors who obtained sensitive Revolut customer information appear to have begun posting it publicly and are threatening to release more data every day until "revolut pays." ✦ The Privacy Trade — Zcash Reaches a Decade High, and $20 Billion ✦

Zcash climbed to its highest price since 2016 this week, extending a rally that has pushed the privacy-focused cryptocurrency's market capitalization above $20 billion. ZEC reached $1,249.28 before retreating to about $1,195 on Monday, per CoinGecko, and it traded near $1,135 as of this morning's ticker — up some 45% over the past week and 138% over 30 days. The token remains below its launch-era record of $3,191.93 on Oct. 28, 2016, a print made when only a small supply was available.

The mechanics are the story. Zcash lets users choose between public and "shielded" transactions, the latter using zero-knowledge proofs to verify a payment without revealing sender, recipient or amount. "For users that prioritize privacy, this could become a 'must have' feature," wrote Grayscale head of research Zach Pandl, who argues that AI could increase demand for financial privacy by making it easier to link public blockchain transactions to real identities. It is a thesis the market is now pricing aggressively — while Monero, the older privacy standard, slipped 4.33% to $512.45 on the same day, a reminder that this is a rotation, not a tide.

Ethereum Names Two "Must Ship" Proposals for Hegotá

Protocol

The Ethereum Foundation has identified two "must ship" proposals for the Hegotá upgrade, prioritizing censorship resistance and more flexible account authentication while narrowing the fork's scope. The ranking weighed 62 proposed EIPs with input from roughly 60 researchers and engineers in the Foundation's Protocol cluster — the first time that group has published a unified view rather than separate team opinions. The pair: EIP-7805, FOCIL, and EIP-8141, Frame Transactions, designated the consensus- and execution-layer headliners. FOCIL gives users a path to have eligible transactions included without relying on centralized block builders; Frame Transactions introduces native account abstraction and a route toward post-quantum authentication, with companion proposals laying protocol-level blocks for privacy applications. If either is at risk, the Foundation said Hegotá's schedule should adjust before the proposal is dropped.

Tenev: Going Onchain Shouldn't Hand Issuers a Veto

Tokenization

Robinhood CEO Vlad Tenev says companies should not hold veto power over tokenized stock products that don't change shareholder rights, issuer obligations or a company's official stock ledger. Writing on X, Tenev argued the question of issuer consent turns on whether the product alters the rights attached to underlying shares or creates new obligations for the company or its transfer agent — in which case the issuer should be involved. Where the product is a separate instrument holding or referencing freely transferable shares without touching the cap table, he said, consent should not be required. The comments follow AMC Entertainment CEO Adam Aron criticizing Robinhood's tokenized stock offerings on Sept. 4, saying AMC had no affiliation with them and would ask securities counsel to review. Robinhood Stock Tokens use a third-party structure, separately issued and backed 1:1 by underlying shares. "Going onchain shouldn't give the issuer a veto it never had offchain," Tenev wrote.

India Puts Corporate Bonds on a Ledger: $107M Issued

Tokenization

India's securities regulator and central bank have launched a tokenized corporate bond pilot, with three companies issuing a combined 10.25 billion rupees (about $107 million) through the new market infrastructure. The Securities and Exchange Board of India said Demat 2.0 lets corporate bonds be issued and held as digital tokens on a distributed ledger owned by the country's statutory depositories, connecting to the Reserve Bank of India's wholesale CBDC through its Unified Market Interface. First out of the gate was state lender REC, raising 5 billion rupees from 18 investors; engineering conglomerate Larsen & Toubro matched that with 5 billion from four investors, and non-bank lender IIFL issued 250 million rupees to one. SEBI said issuers now receive funds on the day of bidding rather than two to three days later, with atomic settlement removing the gap and smart contracts automating interest and redemption. Later phases will add secondary trading and retail access.

Stablecoins

Uzbekistan has launched a pilot for payments in a som-pegged stablecoin backed by government securities. The National Agency for Prospective Projects said it registered Humo Digital under a special regime run jointly with the central bank, testing issuance, circulation and redemption of HUMO at one token to one som, with more than 20 merchants ready to accept it for goods and services. The WTO: Stablecoin Trade Is Held Back by Patchwork Rules

Policy

Fragmented regulation — not technology — is what limits stablecoin adoption in international trade, according to Juan Marchetti, director of the trade in services and investment division at the World Trade Organization. "The constraint is not technology. It is actually regulation and the lack of development of regulatory frameworks," he said in Geneva at the launch of the WTO's study on stablecoins in world trade, citing a Financial Stability Board report finding that only 39%, or 11 of 28 surveyed jurisdictions, have finalized their stablecoin rules. Stablecoins now account for just 3% of total international payments. The WTO's report identifies five friction points stablecoins might ease: high costs, low speed, limited access, insufficient transparency and foreign-exchange limitations.

Britain Asks Whether Tokenized Gold Belongs in the Fund Rulebook

Regulation

The UK's Financial Conduct Authority is seeking feedback on whether some tokenized gold products should be exempt from fund rules, as regulators examine their use in wholesale markets. In a call for input published Monday, the FCA said it is looking at whether tokenization could improve how gold is traded, transferred, pledged and held in UK markets, including its potential use as wholesale collateral. The review focuses on products representing ownership of physical bullion with transparent backing, clearly defined ownership rights and reliable redemption arrangements. Uncertainty over whether such products fall inside the UK's collective investment scheme or alternative investment fund perimeter may be holding back certain use cases, the FCA said. The consultation runs until Oct. 23 and could produce clearer guidance, targeted exemptions or a bespoke regime.
