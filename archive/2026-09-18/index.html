Bitcoin Storms Back to $81,000 as a Fuel Crisis Reprices Every Risk Asset

A five-percent move fills pockets of upside liquidity and burns a quarter-billion dollars of shorts in four hours, as the International Energy Agency warns the world may have no choice but to cut its oil use.

By The Daily Cryptograph Staff | September 18, 2026

The Bitcoin price jumped past $80,000 around Friday's Wall Street open as fuel-crisis woes spread through global markets. Data from TradingView showed BTC/USD filling pockets of upside liquidity to reach local highs of $81,034 on Bitstamp — a cluster of short positions above the spot price coming under fire as a result. Per data from CoinGlass, cumulative cross-crypto short liquidations sat near $250 million over four hours. The trigger was not in the crypto market at all. US WTI crude fell to lows of $94.8 per barrel, only to begin climbing again through the Asia session and circle $98 at the time of writing. In a report on Friday, the International Energy Agency warned that countries may have no choice but to cut usage — recalling March, when the agency released 400 million barrels from its emergency reserves amid the closure of the Strait of Hormuz.

Bitcoin has spent the past several weeks coiled below the mid-May range, and Friday's move puts it back at the underside of that resistance — the next test analysts have flagged. The larger backdrop remains a market nearly 39% below its October 2025 record of just over $126,000, still absorbing the Federal Reserve's first rate hike since 2023 and the Senate's failure to advance the CLARITY Act. What Friday showed is that the marginal crypto seller is exhausted faster than expected when an outside shock forces risk back on: the liquidations were shorts, not longs.

The move was broad and led by the far end of the risk curve. Solana rose 10.55%, Monero 10.97% and HYPE 11.07% as the latter set a fresh all-time high above $90 on the back of Hyperliquid's manual-borrowing rollout. Ether added 5.23% to $2,595.05 and Cardano 8.04%, while privacy coin Zcash was the lone shade of red on our tape at −1.72% following a fortnight of extraordinary gains. Thirteen coins, twelve green: a tape that reads less like a bottom forming quietly than like a market that had quietly stopped waiting for one.

Crypto Rules Go to the White House, Prerule and All

Policy September 18, 2026

The Commodity Futures Trading Commission has submitted a new regulatory action covering crypto asset transactions and markets for White House review. According to a filing with the Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs, the action — titled "Regulation Crypto Asset Transactions and Regulation Crypto Asset Markets" — was received on Sept. 17 and is listed at the "prerule" stage, an early point in the rulemaking process at which nothing has yet been formally proposed and no details are disclosed.

The timing is the story. The filing lands days after the Senate failed to advance the CLARITY Act, and a day after CFTC Chair Michael Selig said the agency was "locked in and ready to ship" crypto market rules under existing statutory authority, with SEC Chair Paul Atkins promising to proceed "with or without legislation." Both agencies have since acted: the CFTC issued a no-action position for passive software providers, and the SEC announced temporary exemptions for certain platforms facilitating onchain trading of tokenized securities. In August, Selig told the CFTC's Innovation Advisory Committee he had directed staff to explore a "crypto asset market" designation letting registered and unregistered exchanges offer leveraged crypto trading under agency oversight. This is the machinery of that plan, turning over.

Treasury Sanctions BitBank Over "Hormuz Safe" Bitcoin

Sanctions September 18, 2026

US authorities announced sanctions against the Iranian crypto exchange BitBank, accusing it of processing Bitcoin paid by ships transiting the Strait of Hormuz. The Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control said that as of June the Hormuz Safe Marine Services Authority used BitBank to forward payments it collected to the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps — describing the arrangement as part of the architecture used by Iranian financier Babak Zanjani to move hundreds of millions of dollars in Bitcoin to the IRGC.

Treasury has previously alleged that Hormuz Safe is part of an IRGC-backed scheme forcing vessels to buy maritime insurance for passage, including coverage against seizure by Iran itself. "Today's designations of Iranian digital asset infrastructure make perfectly clear that efforts to finance the Iranian regime using cryptocurrencies are not beyond OFAC's reach," said Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent. The designations also cover BitBank developer Pishtaz Simorgh Electronic Trade Company and three associates of Zanjani. One clarification for the record: this BitBank is not the licensed Japanese exchange of the same name acquired by SBI Holdings in June — Treasury lists the Iranian entity as established in 2024.

Markets September 18, 2026

Arc Goes Live, and USDC Pays the Gas

Stablecoins September 18, 2026

Circle has launched the mainnet of Arc, a layer-1 blockchain aimed at stablecoin payments and financial markets — with conspicuous attention to agentic transactions. Arc uses USDC as its native gas asset and offers EVM compatibility plus deterministic sub-second settlement finality, according to an Arc blog post. The network supports more than 20 fiat stablecoins — USDC, EURC, JPYC, KRW1 and TRYB among them — while tokenized assets including BlackRock's BUIDL and Circle's USYC sit natively onchain, with interoperability to more than 20 blockchains through Circle's Cross-Chain Transfer Protocol and Gateway.

CEO Jeremy Allaire called Arc "the single most significant launch in Circle's history since USDC itself," while the company described it as infrastructure for "programmable money, global markets, and agentic economic activity." More than 100 companies participated in the private mainnet, with BlackRock, Goldman Sachs, Mastercard and Visa among earlier testnet names. Arc plans to broaden participation in network operations and explore a move from Proof of Authority to Proof of Stake in 2027; Circle completed a genesis mint of 10 billion ARC tokens but stressed this is not a commitment to a public token launch.

✦ "Look for the Evidence, Not the Calendar" — The Case That Bitcoin Bottomed at $58,000 ✦

James Check, founder and lead analyst at Checkonchain, argues Bitcoin may have already established its cycle bottom after undergoing two distinct capitulation events — one by price, one by time. Speaking on Cointelegraph's Proof of Thesis show, Check described February's decline toward $60,000 as a "price-pain capitulation," when investors who bought near the top sold at substantial losses. The second came at $58,000 in June and July: a "time-pain capitulation" following months of sideways action that wore holders down without a fresh low to blame. "What's the difference between $58,000 and $59,000 or $60,000? Nothing," Check said. "It's the six months that separated them. That's the actual difference."

The claim runs against the widely held expectation of a fourth-quarter low tied to Bitcoin's historical four-year cycle — a view analyst Benjamin Cowen endorsed in July, citing cycle-duration data and the US midterm calendar. Check's response is blunt: anchoring to four years is a mistake because there is no mechanical reason for the cycle to repeat. "Ask, 'Well, now what do I do?' long before your compass breaks," he said. "It's like a broken clock. It's right twice a day. Just assume it's broken and find something better." Instead he points to cost basis: roughly $300 billion of Bitcoin cost basis sits between $58,000 and $70,000, about 4 million BTC moved from unrealized loss into profit during the recovery, and long-term holders now control roughly 80% of Bitcoin wealth — a cohort, he argues, more likely to wait for substantially higher prices than to sell into a short-term bounce. Grayscale researchers have reached a similar $58,000 conclusion. "Look for the evidence, not the calendar," Check said. For a market that just added five percent in a session on an oil headline, that is one reading of why the tape is behaving this way.

Nearly One in Four EU Crypto Providers Is Now a Bank

Regulation

Banks are rapidly expanding across Europe's regulated crypto market. The number of banks on the EU's MiCA crypto provider list doubled to about 80 from roughly 40 between June 26 and Sept. 16, per a Cointelegraph analysis of ESMA register data, while total listed crypto-asset service providers rose from 243 to 349. Non-banks still dominate in absolute terms but lost relative ground, their share falling from 84% to 77% as banks climbed from 17% to nearly 23%. Germany drove much of it — Deutsche Bank announced institutional digital asset custody on Wednesday and expects MiCA approval in October, while dozens of Volksbank, Raiffeisenbank and VR Bank institutions appeared on the register. Banks enter through a separate notification procedure rather than full CASP authorization.

Celsius Sues BitMEX for 6,360 Bitcoin, Eleven Days Before It Closes

Legal

The Celsius bankruptcy estate sued five BitMEX-linked companies, alleging fraud, market manipulation and wrongful liquidations during the March 2020 crash. A Reserve Bill That Says: Hold for Twenty Years

Policy

US lawmakers moved to put President Donald Trump's executive order establishing a Strategic Bitcoin Reserve into law. The American Reserve Modernization Act of 2026 (H.R. 8957) passed the House Committee on Financial Services 28-21, establishing a Strategic Bitcoin Reserve plus a separate Digital Asset Stockpile at Treasury for federally held assets acquired through criminal or civil forfeiture. Bitcoin in the reserve would be held for a minimum of 20 years. The bill demands a full accounting of digital assets held across agencies, quarterly proof-of-reserve reports and third-party audits, directs a study of budget-neutral acquisition strategies, and allows states to store Bitcoin at the Federal Reserve. "We cannot allow Bitcoin to be held by the federal government to languish in fragmented and inconsistent custody," said Representative Nicholas Begich. The US government is estimated to hold 324,527 BTC, worth about $24.7 billion.

Zcash Sets November 5 for Twenty-Five-Second Blocks

Protocol

Zcash developers are targeting Nov. 5 for NU7 mainnet activation after organizations and engineering teams reached "unanimous agreement" on contents and timeline. Developer Sean Bowe said the upgrade activates on testnet Oct. 6, with a final mainnet decision scheduled for Oct. 20. Solana Triples Its Transaction Ceiling to 4,096 Bytes

Chains

Solana raised its maximum transaction size from 1,232 bytes to 4,096 bytes, letting developers fit more complex operations into a single transaction — zero-knowledge proofs, multi-signature transactions and new onchain signature schemes among them. The upgrade activated on mainnet at the start of epoch 1,035 around 1:00 am UTC and introduced the v1 transaction format, which keeps full backward compatibility with legacy transactions. Protocols wanting the size increase must update to v1. A Solana Foundation spokesperson said the aim is to help developers "do more" by unlocking workloads that previously could not fit. The change follows August's slot-time reduction from 400ms to 350ms, June's stated plan for 200ms, and an Aug. 28 validator vote to double the annual disinflation rate.

The CFTC Frees the Wallet From the Broker's Ledger

Regulation

The CFTC expanded relief for "passive software" providers that connect users to regulated derivatives firms and exchanges. In a no-action position, the agency's Market Participants Division said it would not recommend enforcement against qualifying providers or their personnel for failing to register as introducing brokers or associated persons when facilitating trading with CFTC-registered firms. The position could make it easier for crypto wallets and apps to offer regulated derivatives — perpetual contracts and prediction markets included — without becoming brokers themselves. Providers must meet conditions limiting their role, including restrictions on exercising discretion over users' orders. It extends relief granted to Phantom Technologies in March, and follows July lobbying by Phantom and the Hyperliquid Policy Center for broader protections covering non-custodial wallet providers and onchain infrastructure developers.

Ethereum and Base Stop Agreeing on Wallets

Standards

Ethereum and Base will implement different account abstraction standards after efforts to agree on a shared one broke down. Interoperability became secondary to each chain's core goals, "putting the burden on wallets," wrote Derek Chiang, Ethlabs founding member and co-author of Ethereum's EIP-8141. The divergence could force wallet developers to support separate transaction formats for a consistent cross-network experience. Ethereum is advancing Frame Transactions under EIP-8141 as a "headliner" item for Hegotá, introducing native account abstraction and a path toward post-quantum authentication; Base is building native account abstraction via Keystore under EIP-8130, currently on devnet. Chiang argued the split need not be bad news: L1s are converging on censorship resistance, privacy and security, while scalability-focused L2s align with standards like EIP-8130 — and both are now "free to innovate on AA to the maximal extent."
