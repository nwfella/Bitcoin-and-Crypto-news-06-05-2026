ETF Flows The BlackRock Rebound

BlackRock Drives $217M Bitcoin ETF Rebound as Altcoin Funds Extend Streaks

US spot Bitcoin ETFs returned to inflows on Monday with $216.7 million in net additions, reversing the $201.8 million in withdrawals that ended a nine-session run of more than $3 billion. BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) did nearly all of the heavy lifting with $205.9 million — about 95% of the category's daily total — while FBTC, BITB, Morgan Stanley's trust and Grayscale's Mini Trust chipped in smaller sums.

VanEck's HODL was the only fund to bleed, posting $13.4 million in outflows. Elsewhere, spot Ether ETFs stretched their inflow streak to 11 consecutive sessions with $87.7 million, while XRP and Solana funds each logged a 10th straight positive day — XRP's run now spans every US trading session since Aug. 18, and Solana's daily intake slowed to $925,010. Bitcoin traded near $78,700 at the time of writing, up about 1.5% over 24 hours.

Institutions The 21-Bank Consortium

BofA, Citi, Goldman Sachs Among 21 Institutions Planning Stablecoin Launch

A consortium of 21 major financial institutions — Bank of America, Goldman Sachs, Citi, Deutsche Bank, UBS, Santander, MUFG and Fidelity among them — announced Tuesday it will establish a new company to develop and issue stablecoins, with a US dollar-denominated coin targeted for the first half of 2027. The group plans to expand into other G7 currencies afterward, with a euro offering identified as the next priority.

The stablecoin will target wholesale, institutional and retail use — including cross-border payments and digital asset settlement — and is designed to comply with the US GENIUS Act and the EU's MiCA where applicable. The venture more than doubled since ten banks first floated the idea last October, and lands as the Monetary Authority of Singapore opens its own consultation on admitting jointly issued cross-border stablecoins into its regime.

Security The $30M Bridge

Lazarus Group-Linked Addresses Move $30M Through Hyperliquid

Wallet addresses tied to North Korea's Lazarus Group moved $30 million in digital assets through the decentralized exchange Hyperliquid, according to blockchain data shared by Arkham analyst Emmett Gallic. The wallets deposited Bitcoin, traded into Ether or Solana, bridged out to Tron, Solana or Ethereum, and ultimately landed funds at exchanges KuCoin, Kraken and Lbank along with several unlabeled Tron-based services.

The transfers arrive weeks after President Trump said CFTC Chair Michael Selig is working on a regulatory pathway to bring Hyperliquid into US markets. Lazarus remains the prime suspect in some of the largest crypto heists ever recorded — including the $1.4 billion Bybit hack of 2025 — and North Korea-linked actors were tied to at least $578 million of the $634 million stolen in crypto incidents in April alone.

Regulation The $46B Forecast

Russian Crypto Trading to Bring $46B to Regulated Exchanges in First Year

Russia's crypto trading is expected to deliver 4 trillion rubles ($46.4 billion) in volume to regulated domestic exchanges in the first year after legalization, according to estimates from Sber, the country's largest bank — growing to about 7.5 trillion rubles by 2029. Deputy Chairman Anatoly Popov called the forecast conservative, since a large share of transactions will still bypass organized platforms.

The estimate lands as Russia's new crypto market law — signed by President Putin on Aug. 4 — takes effect Sept. 1, with the central bank proposing Bitcoin, Ether and Tether's USDT for public exchange trading. Non-qualified investors could buy up to 300,000 rubles of crypto per year through each intermediary, while qualified investors face no purchase limits on exchange-traded or OTC crypto.

✦ Feature: The September Rate Pivot ✦

Markets open September pricing a near-60% chance that the Federal Reserve hikes 0.25% at its September meeting — up from 41.4% a week earlier — after new chair Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole debut, in which he declared forward guidance had "overstayed its welcome" and called the inflation picture "too high." Security The RevStealer Strain

Fake Claude Desktop App Spreads Crypto-Stealing Malware

A fake "Claude Opus 5 Free Desktop" project impersonating AI developer Anthropic is distributing RevStealer, Windows malware built to steal crypto, passwords and browser data, according to Morphisec. The strain probes browser databases, cookies, password-manager records, VPN and remote-access settings, messaging data and screenshots — and targets more than 50 cryptocurrency wallets — but first verifies the machine looks like a genuine user device, skipping debugging environments typical of malware analysis. The report follows Kaspersky's discovery of OkoBot, another malware framework aimed at crypto investors.

Stablecoins The Cross-Border Question

Singapore Weighs Recognizing Some Foreign-Issued Stablecoins

The Monetary Authority of Singapore opened a consultation Tuesday proposing that stablecoins jointly issued by Singapore and foreign issuers could qualify as "MAS-regulated stablecoins," provided associated risks are sufficiently mitigated. MAS is also weighing recognition of a limited number of foreign-issued stablecoins regulated under comparable overseas frameworks, citing cross-border wholesale use. The proposals revisit the regulator's 2023 position that qualifying coins must be issued solely in Singapore, and include issuer safeguards such as no interest on regulated stablecoins, stress tests and orderly wind-down plans. Comments close Oct. 16.

Regulation The Retail Door

Thailand SEC Proposes Retail Access to Overseas Crypto Derivatives

Thailand's Securities and Exchange Commission has proposed allowing intermediaries to facilitate retail access to certain overseas digital asset derivatives, provided the products resemble crypto derivatives traded domestically — including underlying assets, maturity, leverage and settlement — and trade on centrally cleared exchanges overseen by regulators in specified international bodies. The consultation, open until Sept. 30, follows March's designation of cryptocurrencies as permissible derivatives underlyings and talks with the Thailand Futures Exchange on contract specs. Products that don't qualify would remain institution-only, with the regulator arguing institutions are better equipped to manage high-risk products.
