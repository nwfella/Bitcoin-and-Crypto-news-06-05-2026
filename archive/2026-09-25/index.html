Three Hundred Fifty-One Million Leaves Bitget in a Single Evening, and the Trail Points Toward Pyongyang

Unauthorised transfers drained roughly $351.6 million from a limited number of Bitget hot wallets at 18:31 UTC on Thursday. Withdrawals are suspended, cold storage is untouched, and the exchange's chief executive says investigators have matched the intruders' IP addresses to the virtual private networks of "a certain DPRK group."

By The Daily Cryptograph Staff | September 25, 2026

Bitget confirmed on Thursday evening that unauthorised transfers had moved approximately $351.6 million out of a limited number of its hot wallets, and suspended withdrawals while it investigates. The exchange said its security systems detected the activity at 18:31 UTC and that emergency response procedures were activated at once. Chief executive Gracy Chen, speaking in a live question-and-answer session on X hours after the incident, said the breach was contained to a portion of the exchange's hot and warm wallet layers and that its cold wallets remained secure. The affected assets included Ether, XRP, USDt, USDC, Avalanche, BNB and USDT0 on Arbitrum, across the Ethereum, XRP Ledger, Avalanche, BNB Smart Chain and Arbitrum networks — with Ethereum carrying the main concentration of the stolen funds.

The figure itself moved during the day, and the movement is worth recording. Early onchain reporting captured a materially smaller sum than the exchange eventually disclosed; Chen said the difference arose because the incident extended beyond the Ethereum activity that first drew analysts' attention. Chen told viewers she expects withdrawals to reopen "in a matter of hours or days," and promised a full incident report within twenty-four hours. She also said the exchange does not believe the breach was an inside job.

The attribution is the part that will travel furthest. "We've identified some IP addresses that match the VPN choices by a certain DPRK group," Chen said, referring to the Democratic People's Republic of Korea. "The pattern looks very much like what the North Korean team did before." The comparison is not an idle one: North Korean hackers have been linked to an estimated $2.02 billion in crypto theft in 2025 alone, a tally that includes the roughly $1.5 billion Bybit breach which the FBI formally attributed to Pyongyang. A United States court has since backed Bybit's bid to trace funds from that haul — a precedent Bitget's advisers will be studying closely this morning.

For the tape, the immediate damage looks contained. Bitcoin sat at $83,715 at our press time, down 0.68% over twenty-four hours — a modest move for a $350 million exchange failure, and one that says the market read the incident as a custodial problem rather than a systemic one. Ether was flat at $2,680.78, while Chainlink led the board at $13.82, up 8.55%, and Stellar added 4.92% to $0.2180. XRP firmed 4.79% to $1.58 and Solana rose 3.27% to $119.84. Only four names on our thirteen-coin tape are lower: Hyperliquid at $90.88 (−2.54%), BNB at $773.64 (−0.77%), TRON at $0.3365 (−0.70%) and Bitcoin itself.

The Bridge Broke, and Now the Bill Goes to Court

Litigation September 25, 2026

KelpDAO filed a lawsuit on Friday against cross-chain protocol LayerZero over the roughly $292 million exploit of its rsETH bridge earlier this year, alleging that failures in LayerZero's security infrastructure contributed to the attack. The complaint also names LayerZero co-founder and chief executive Bryan Pellegrino. Central to the claim is a document trail: KelpDAO says LayerZero failed to disclose risks in its technology and failed to prevent attackers from compromising its infrastructure, and that the protocol had reviewed and endorsed Kelp's deployment and configuration in writing before the exploit occurred.

The filing escalates a dispute that has run since April over whether the loss sprang from the compromise of LayerZero's infrastructure, from KelpDAO's own bridge configuration, or from both. In a statement, Kelp wrote that it must "correct the record, and hold LayerZero and Mr. Pellegrino accountable for the harm they have caused us and the broader DeFi ecosystem." Pellegrino's answer was terse: he called the claim "meritless" and said he would defend the case in Vancouver. Cointelegraph sought further comment from LayerZero and had received no response by publication.

The Fed Fills In the Blanks the GENIUS Act Left Open

Regulation September 24, 2026

The Federal Reserve has proposed capital, redemption and disclosure requirements for the stablecoin issuers it supervises, moving to implement the GENIUS Act. The statute already obliges issuers to hold reserves backing their tokens one-to-one and limits what those reserves may be — cash, bank deposits and short-term US Treasurys — but left the detailed capital, diversification and risk-management standards to the regulators. The Fed's answer is an operational-risk capital charge of 2% on the first $20 billion of stablecoins outstanding, 1.5% on the next $30 billion and 1% on amounts above $50 billion, plus further capital tied to credit and operational risk.

Issuers would generally have to process redemptions within two business days. Should reserves fall below the required one-to-one backing, an issuer must notify the Fed and either restore them under a remediation plan or liquidate the reserves and redeem the outstanding tokens. Monthly reports on outstanding supply and on the value and composition of reserves would have to be examined by a registered public accounting firm and certified by the chief executive and chief financial officer. A companion proposal would establish an application process for Fed-supervised banks seeking to issue payment stablecoins through subsidiaries. Both are open to public comment for 60 days after publication in the Federal Register.

Privacy for Bitcoin Without Asking the Miners' Permission

Protocols September 25, 2026

Researchers at the cryptography firm Alloc Init have proposed a system for bringing Zcash-style private transfers to Bitcoin without a soft fork. The design, published Thursday by Clara Shikhelman, Mikhail Komarov and Aleksei Moskvin and named Shielded Bitcoin, would conceal transaction amounts, senders, receivers and any link to previously spent funds using encrypted notes and zero-knowledge proofs. Rather than having miners enforce the privacy protocol, Bitcoin serves as "a neutral publication and ordering layer," with separate software — indexers — verifying the proofs, checking for double-spends and reconstructing the state of the shielded system.

The proposal is candid about its inheritance: it uses Zcash's architecture of encrypted notes, public nullifiers that mark notes as spent, and zero-knowledge proofs of validity, but unlike Zcash would run no blockchain and no consensus mechanism of its own. Reaction was mixed. Developer Vadim Zavodil argued on X that much of the privacy stack had already been built by Zcash, and questioned how much privacy a new system could offer on day one. "Privacy is a function of the crowd," he wrote. "Zcash has a real shielded pool built over years. A brand new metaprotocol starts at zero, so your first private transfer hides in a crowd of one."

3,832 NFTs Swept Into Protective Custody Overnight

Security September 25, 2026

A single wallet moved 3,832 non-fungible tokens out of hundreds of wallets on Friday, and for a few hours it looked like the largest NFT theft of the year. The community member Cirrus flagged the activity first, noting the transfers appeared as sales through Magic Eden and advising holders to revoke permissions as a precaution. Then 0xQuit, Yuga Labs' pseudonymous vice president of blockchain, identified the movement as a white-hat rescue: the tokens sit in a receiving wallet that is safe, he said, and "will be returned once they are no longer at risk." 0xQuit has run such operations before — in June he helped recover 68 NFTs worth more than $500,000 after an exploit at Flooring Protocol.

Magic Eden later said the exploit involved Limit Break's Payment Processor V2, an NFT trading protocol it stopped using in October 2024 and whose EVM marketplace it closed in the first quarter of 2026. "No live Magic Eden listings were impacted in this exploit," the company said, adding that NFTs listed on the EVM marketplace from roughly February to October 2024 could be affected. Former users were urged to revoke approvals for the contract on Ethereum, Polygon and Base — a step that will not return tokens already moved — while discussions with Limit Break continued over further mitigations, including attempts to pause transfers.

✦ The Bond Market Sets the Ceiling, the Option Market Prices the Floor ✦

The US 10-year Treasury yield rose more than 4 basis points to 5.18% on Thursday — its highest since July 2007 — while the 30-year reached 5.46%, reclaiming levels last seen in 2004. The climb landed on the very day the Treasury was scheduled to buy back up to $6 billion of bonds maturing in twenty to thirty years, part of an expanded programme meant to improve liquidity in long-dated debt. Higher yields make government paper more attractive and weigh on assets that pay no coupon, and Bitcoin obeyed the textbook for part of the session: the coin briefly fell below $83,000 and struggled to reclaim $84,500. Ondo Finance's ONDO token was among the day's best-performing altcoins, reclaiming $0.50.

The cross-border plumbing deserves a line of its own. Mohamed El-Erian noted on X that the yen has weakened back to 159 to the dollar, approaching the intervention zone, and that Japanese intervention typically involves selling US securities to buy yen — "potentially adding yield pressures to an already sensitive Treasury market." Against that backdrop the options market has turned placid. Saxo Bank's Koen Hoorelbeke put IBIT's implied volatility at 37.4% against realised volatility of 45.5% over the twenty sessions to Tuesday, an implied volatility rank of 11.9 that sits near the bottom of its twelve-month range. "The options market appears to be pricing calmer conditions than the recent past produced," he wrote. He marks resistance near $87,000, where the advance stalled on Sept. 21, and support between $76,000 and $77,000. Bitcoin has nonetheless extended its August rally, defying bearish predictions tied to its traditional four-year cycle.

An App From the App Store, and $580,000 Gone

Security

A malicious iOS application distributed through Apple's App Store has been linked to nearly $580,000 in stolen crypto. According to an investigation by security firm SlowMist conducted with the OKX security team, the app — FomoPeek — carried two malicious modules capable of exploiting iOS vulnerabilities, gaining elevated privileges and reaching Keychain data and files belonging to other applications. The affected versions were released on Sept. 9 and Sept. 12; version 1.3, released Sept. 17, removed the malicious components. The framework declared support for eight attack methods spanning iOS 12.0 to 18.7.2 and 26.0 to 26.1, and loaded when the app launched. SlowMist stressed that no Safari page or website was involved, and urged affected users to move assets to a new wallet. FlashRescue traced about 579,900 USDT to a hacker address, with funds routed through FixedFloat, KuCoin and other services.

Teaching the Machines to Pay in Satoshis

Payments

Block has joined the x402 Foundation and added Bitcoin Lightning support to the payment protocol, extending its push into payments for artificial-intelligence agents. x402 is an open standard that lets AI agents and web services pay autonomously for API access, data and digital services; Block argues Lightning suits the low-cost, high-volume traffic such commerce will generate. The Last 314 Coins, and the End of a Treasury

Corporate

Sequans Communications has sold its remaining 314 Bitcoin, closing out a treasury strategy that once saw the French semiconductor company hold more than 3,200 BTC. The exit follows the redemption of its convertible debt in May and lets it refocus on its cellular internet-of-things and software-defined radio businesses. Chief executive Georges Karam said the sales eliminated the convertible debt and strengthened the balance sheet, leaving Sequans with no cryptocurrency holdings and no outstanding debt beyond government-financed research and development obligations. The company launched the strategy in June 2025 on the back of a $384 million equity and convertible-debenture sale, when Karam called Bitcoin "a premier asset and a compelling long-term investment." It began unwinding less than six months later, selling 970 BTC in November to redeem half the convertible notes, and by May 2026 said it was "no longer pursuing" the strategy at all. A growing list of digital asset treasury companies has abandoned or scaled back accumulation in 2026.

A Deadline for the Forgotten Zcash Wallets

Protocols

Zcash users holding ZEC in the network's legacy Sprout shielded pool are being urged to move it before the planned NU7 upgrade makes it permanently unspendable. Under Zcash Improvement Proposal-2003, NU7 would stop accepting the older transaction format that allows Sprout funds to be spent. The proposal does not burn those coins and leaves open the possibility of restoring access later, but warns holders may never be able to spend them. A forum user posting as vaspholdings pressed holders on Sunday to check old wallets, which may contain Sprout ZEC, transparent ZEC or both; only Sprout funds face the restriction. Co-founder Zooko Wilcox shared the warning on X. NU7 is scheduled to activate on testnet Oct. 6, with a decision on the targeted Nov. 5 mainnet activation expected Oct. 20. The upgrade would also cut the target block time from 75 seconds to 25, while preserving the halving schedule.

Washington Weighs Exporting the Dollar, Digitally

Policy

The Trump administration is reportedly considering an overseas push for dollar-backed stablecoins as part of an effort to reinforce the dollar's standing as the world's reserve currency. Bloomberg reported that the government could support projects through joint ventures with private firms, involving the Treasury Department, the State Department and the US International Development Finance Corporation. The logic runs in two directions at once: broader international use of dollar-denominated tokens, and the additional demand for US Treasurys those tokens' reserves represent. The timing is competitive — China's digital yuan is among the currencies used in Project mBridge for cross-border CBDC transactions, and the European Central Bank is preparing a twelve-month digital euro pilot expected to begin in the second half of 2027. Cointelegraph sought comment from the Treasury, the DFC and several US stablecoin issuers and received no response before publication.

Wiring the Bank Vault to the Swift Ledger

Infrastructure

IBM has added beta connectivity between its Digital Asset Haven platform and Swift's blockchain-based ledger for tokenized deposits, alongside a beta option for banks to run the platform inside their own data centres. A new messaging adapter lets institutions instruct tokenized deposit transactions using ISO 20022, the global financial messaging standard, so banks can reuse existing payment formats and compliance processes instead of building blockchain-specific workflows. Institutions in Swift's programme have already tested tokenized deposits on the ledger through the platform. Swift built that ledger with more than 40 financial institutions and launched it in July with 17 banks including HSBC, Citi, BNP Paribas, UBS and Standard Chartered; HSBC and Standard Chartered completed its first live cross-border transaction in August. The on-premises variant runs on IBM Z and LinuxONE without public cloud services, keeping key management behind the bank's own walls.

Regulators Fill the Vacuum Congress Left Behind

Policy

The CFTC has updated its guidance on tokenized assets and on the use of blockchain technology for recordkeeping, amending the frequently-asked-questions material for registered crypto-related entities it first published in March. The revision specifies that authorised companies may invest customer funds in tokenized forms, provided "the tokenized form of the asset grants the holder legal and economic rights that are the same or functionally equivalent to the rights received by holders of the asset in its traditional form," and states the regulator "would not object" to blockchain-based recordkeeping under the new rules. Chair Michael Selig called it part of an effort "to provide regulatory clarity for the crypto industry." The timing is the point: the notice lands days after the Senate failed to advance the CLARITY Act, and with the failed cloture vote many now expect no crypto market-structure statute before 2027. The CFTC has already submitted a crypto market regulation plan for the White House to review, and SEC Chair Paul Atkins said before the vote that his agency was "ready, willing, and able" to propose rules in Congress's absence.
