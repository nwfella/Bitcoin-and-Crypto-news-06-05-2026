Bitcoin Closes Above Its 50-Week Average, and the Bears Lose Their Best Argument

The highest weekly close since May puts BTC back over the level that has capped every bear market since 2017 — and by Monday's Wall Street open the tape had printed $86,332, a 33-week high, with nearly $800 million in shorts liquidated in a day.

By The Daily Cryptograph Staff | September 21, 2026

Bitcoin has closed a week above its 50-week moving average for the first time in more than ten months. Per TradingView data, BTC finished the week at $81,159 on Coinbase, clearing a moving average of $78,788; the last weekly close above that line came on Nov. 9, 2025. It is the coin's highest weekly close in four months. By Monday's session the move had extended violently — Bitcoin spiked above $86,000 after the Wall Street open, touching a 33-week high of $86,332 on Bitstamp and trading up more than 5.7% on the day. CoinGlass put cross-crypto short liquidations in the 24-hour window above $600 million, later approaching $800 million as the squeeze deepened.

The technical claim is not mystical. In August, Galaxy Research's head of firmwide research, Alex Thorn, described the 50-week average as the ceiling of a bear market — and the door out of one. "In four of the five completed bear markets, once the 50-week moving average was first broken to the upside, the bear market bottom was definitively 'in,'" Thorn wrote. "Essentially, retaking the 50w MA has previously confirmed the end of a bear market." Ben Simpson, founder of the research firm Collective Shift, had said a week earlier that the close was "the last thing I need to see before I call this a bull market," noting Bitcoin gained between 700% and 900% after breaking the level in 2017, 2020 and 2023.

Analysts on the desk are not yet unanimous. Bitget chief analyst Ryan Lee told Cointelegraph the close "added weight to the case that Bitcoin's recovery was underway," observing that in prior cycles "reclaiming this level has tended to happen after the major low was established and longer-term momentum had started to recover." Against that sits a bearish divergence on the daily RSI, which Rekt Capital flagged last week as higher price highs set against lower indicator highs — "a lack of underlying momentum to support the highs," he warned, raising the risk of a sudden reversal. One desk note put the invalidation plainly: a daily close beneath $77,100 breaks the structure, exposing the True Market Mean at $76,677.

The fuel came from outside crypto. WTI crude fell as low as $91.59 a barrel after signals from Qatar's Foreign Ministry and President Donald Trump that negotiations to end the US–Iran war could resume; JPMorgan told clients that "Middle East oil flows remain surprisingly strong despite the disruption to Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline." The S&P 500 rose 1% and the Nasdaq Composite 1.6%, and The New York Times reported the US plans to extend its trade deal with China by six months ahead of Xi Jinping's visit on Sept. 23–25. Thirteen coins on our tape, twelve of them green: the argument is no longer about whether the low is in, but about what holds when the oil bid fades.

Strategy Breaks Its Two-Week Silence and Buys Again

Treasuries September 21, 2026

Michael Saylor's Strategy returned to the market after a two-week pause, acquiring 950 Bitcoin for $75.7 million at an average price of $79,670 per coin between Monday and Sunday, according to a Form 8-K filed with the SEC on Monday. The purchase lifts the company's holdings to 846,000 BTC, accumulated for roughly $63.8 billion at an average cost of $75,416 including fees. With Bitcoin trading near $84,925 at the time of publication, the treasury was carrying an unrealized gain of about $8.05 billion — a swing that arrived, as these things do, within a single fortnight.

The filing shows the company running two books at once. In the same week Strategy repurchased about 1.77 million shares of its perpetual preferred stock STRC for $174 million, lifting STRC 0.35% to $98.85 in Monday's pre-market — a buyback that keeps the preferred near its $100 par and its effective yield intact. Strategy said $875.1 million remains available under that repurchase program and $1 billion under its MSTR buyback. Its deployable cash, however, fell nearly 20% week over week, which is the cost of the arithmetic. Strive, the fifth-largest corporate Bitcoin holder, bought too: 1,355 BTC last week for a total of 26,355 coins, sending its shares up 6.44% to $32.03. MSTR itself rose 7.4% to $165.2 in pre-market trading.

Zcash Prints Its Highest Price Since 2016

Markets September 21, 2026

Zcash climbed to its highest price since 2016, carrying the privacy coin's market capitalization above $20 billion. ZEC reached $1,249.28 before easing to about $1,195 on Monday, per CoinGecko — a gain of roughly 45% over the week and 138% over 30 days. The rally still sits below the launch-era record of $3,191.93 set on Oct. 28, 2016, when only a sliver of the supply existed. Zcash lets users choose between public and "shielded" transactions, the latter using zero-knowledge proofs to verify a payment without revealing sender, recipient or amount.

The demand thesis has been institutional as much as ideological. Grayscale converted its Zcash Trust into an ETF — ticker ZCSH, trading on NYSE Arca since Aug. 25 — and closed Friday at $83.77 a share on $463.2 million in assets. Grayscale head of research Zach Pandl argued in August that AI could increase demand for financial privacy by making it easier to link public-chain transactions to identities, calling the feature a potential "must have" for privacy-conscious users. The tape has taken some of it back: ZEC is −0.62% on our board tonight at $1,495.33, having given back part of a run that had it at $1,521 on Friday.

"What's the Difference Between $58,000 and $60,000? Nothing."

Analysis September 21, 2026

James Check, founder and lead analyst at Checkonchain, argues Bitcoin may have already set its cycle bottom after two distinct capitulations, and that the four-year calendar everyone is trading against has no mechanical claim on the price. Bitcoin peaked just above $126,000 in October 2025; it traded near $77,400 when he spoke, roughly 39% below that high. Check described February's slide toward $60,000 as a "price-pain capitulation" — top buyers selling at substantial losses — and a second "time-pain capitulation" around $58,000 in June and July, after months of sideways action made holders question whether recovery was coming at all.

"What's the difference between $58,000 and $59,000 or $60,000? Nothing," Check told Cointelegraph. "It's the six months that separated them. That's the actual difference." He cites roughly $300 billion of cost basis clustered between $58,000 and $70,000, about 4 million BTC that moved from unrealized loss into profit during the recovery, and long-term holders controlling near 80% of Bitcoin wealth. His warning to cycle-watchers is blunt: "It's like a broken clock. It's right twice a day. Just assume it's broken and find something better." Look for the evidence, not the calendar.

Samani Predicts the Flippening, and Says Nobody Uses Ethereum

Opinion September 21, 2026

Multicoin Capital co-founder Kyle Samani says more crypto companies will migrate from Ethereum to Solana on ease of use and functionality alone, and predicts the flippening — SOL's market capitalization overtaking Ether's — will happen "this market cycle." The arithmetic is not small: it requires roughly a five-fold increase in SOL's $58 billion market cap to pass Ether's $293 billion. Samani told Cointelegraph's Trade Secrets that "today, no one really uses Ethereum," and that it retains its position mainly through stablecoins and stablecoins borrowed against Ether as collateral — "a $400 billion to $300 billion asset that has questionable value accrual, if any, and it's not growing at all."

The tape complicates the story in both directions. SOL and ETH have moved largely in lockstep during the recent turn — Ether up 30% and Solana 34% over the past month — but Solana's gain comes off a smaller base and a deeper wound, having fallen 59% over the past year against Ether's 45%. Samani's own history is instructive: he said in February he was stepping down as managing partner after ten years, briefly posting and deleting a note that read "I once believed in the web3 vision… I don't anymore." By September he had joined the US board of Backpack. The conviction, apparently, was never really gone.

✦ The Treasury Bid Has Stopped, and the Buyers Are Underwater ✦

Corporate Bitcoin treasuries have become the market's most reliable source of demand in every cycle since 2020. This year they stopped. Glassnode reports that in 2026 listed companies bought roughly 5,900 BTC — less than 7% of what they purchased in July 2025 alone, a single month in which they absorbed 89,000 BTC with BTC/USD above $100,000. The reason is arithmetic: the Corporate Treasury Cost Basis sits at $80.5K, roughly 6% above spot, so the cohort as a whole is under water. "Their average entry... is about 6% above spot, so the group as a whole is under water," Glassnode wrote in the latest Week Onchain. "A buyer that has stopped buying and holds a paper loss is not support."

Two attempts to reclaim that level in 2026 both failed, price declining to hold above it. That puts a ceiling where the market had grown used to a floor: "A reclaim of $80.5K would put the treasuries back in profit and remove one layer of overhead supply; until then their entry is one more ceiling." Strategy, the largest holder with 846,000 BTC at a cost basis of $75,412, bought last at the end of August — 4,603 BTC, its first acquisition in two months — before this Monday's 950 BTC add. Glassnode describes the broader conditions as a "market in waiting" as fresh capital cools. The tell is that the most price-insensitive buyers in the asset class have quietly become the most price-sensitive, and a single reclaim of $80,500 would turn the largest block of overhead supply into the market's largest bid.

The ECB Builds Its Own Rails, Not a Stablecoin

Regulation

The European Central Bank launched Pontes, a system letting financial institutions settle wholesale tokenized asset transactions in central bank money — an explicit alternative to private settlement assets such as stablecoins. Announced Monday as part of the Eurosystem's tokenized-finance strategy, Pontes opens with a core service set and targets full implementation by 2028. It builds on the Eurosystem's 2024 trials settling DLT-based transactions in central bank money, where participants identified access to a risk-free settlement asset as the precondition for adoption at scale. "Pontes brings the stability and trust of central bank money to the European tokenised finance ecosystem," said ECB Executive Board member Piero Cipollone. A companion initiative, Appia, is drawing a blueprint for an integrated DLT financial-services ecosystem, also due by 2028.

Three to Five Days, Compressed Into One

Infrastructure

South Korea's Hana Bank issued a five-year, $100 million digital bond through Euroclear's blockchain-based Digital Financial Market Infrastructure, per Yonhap — the first Korean digital bond issuance to use Euroclear's ledger directly. Issuance, registration and settlement were processed on a distributed ledger network, and the report said the arrangement cut the process from three to five business days down to same-day settlement. Crucially, the instrument also connects to Euroclear's existing global settlement network, so investors trade it through accounts and systems they already run. Euroclear opened the service in October 2023 with a €100 million digital bond for the World Bank's IBRD. Hana Bank and Euroclear did not respond to Cointelegraph by publication.

ZetaChain Votes Its Own Chain Out of Existence

Protocol

ZetaChain tokenholders approved a plan to wind down the project's layer-1 blockchain and migrate ZETA to Solana. Governance proposal 68 passed Sunday with 99.4% support and 58% participation, above the network's 40% quorum. ZETA becomes an SPL token on a 1:1 conversion, same ticker, same total supply. The vote does not trigger shutdown on its own: core contributors must submit a second proposal detailing the withdrawal window for assets linked to other chains, the snapshot block height, the shutdown timetable and the exchange conversion period, with validators continuing to run and staking rewards accruing throughout. ZetaChain, founded in 2021 and backed with $27 million in 2023 from investors including Blockchain.com and Jane Street Capital, said running a Cosmos-SDK chain no longer serves its focus on Anuma, its private AI application. BounceBit and Harmony have announced similar retreats this year.

X Sues a Ring of Bitcoin Accounts for Fake Engagement

Litigation

X has sued the alleged operators of a network of Bitcoin-focused accounts, seeking at least $278,000 in creator payouts it says were obtained by manufacturing engagement. The platform filed in the High Court of England and Wales on Thursday against Vivek Kumar Sen, Zamyang Sherpa and unidentified operators, alleging they fraudulently took at least £207,384 from its revenue-sharing program. X claims the defendants coordinated accounts that reposted and liked one another's content and published identical or substantially similar posts, creating a "false appearance of genuine, human communication." One filing example cites two accounts publishing substantially similar posts 11 seconds apart. The accounts were suspended on Aug. 18.

Canberra Sees Forty Years Ahead and Misses the Rails

Policy

Australia's new Intergenerational Report, published Monday by the Treasury, names artificial intelligence as one of five transitions that will reshape the economy over forty years — describing agentic systems as "significantly" more capable, autonomous and widely used — and does not mention crypto or digital assets once. The other transitions are geopolitical conflict, ageing, clean energy and industrial transformation. Coinbase Australia country director John O'Loghlen called the omission a gap: "While the report focuses heavily on artificial intelligence, it completely misses the financial infrastructure those agents will need." Treasury's separate Financial Innovation Strategy, released Sept. 3, does address the link, noting agentic systems could raise machine-to-machine transactions and the demand for real-time, programmable payment systems. The Digital Finance CRC estimates digital finance could add A$24 billion ($17.1 billion) a year.

A Prediction Market Leaves Base for the Mainnet

Infrastructure

Trueo will migrate from Base to Ethereum, citing broader integration opportunities and plans to build the next generation of its oracle — the system that verifies real-world outcomes to resolve markets. The project launched on Base in March 2025. "As a new app and experiment, an L2 like Base was the right choice for many reasons. At the time, mainnet gas costs were still elevated and some features of our app were experimental," Trueo wrote. "The final form of Trueo is to be a platform that is widely adopted, broadly integrated, fully permissionless, mostly immutable." Co-founder "Lumberg" put it more simply: "Ethereum is the best chain for the most credibly neutral and truthful oracle system." Trueo ranks 14th among onchain prediction markets with $795,687 in TVL, per DefiLlama — a small position, and an unusually explicit statement of why small projects still pay mainnet fees.
