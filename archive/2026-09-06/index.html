Exchanges Santiago Shutdown

Tether-Backed Orionx to Shut Down After Audit Flags $7M Custody Gap

Chilean crypto exchange Orionx — backed by USDt issuer Tether — is permanently closing after a forensic audit found more than $7 million in custodial assets had moved to wallets it did not manage. "Our sole priority now is to return as much of our clients' assets as possible," the exchange said in a statement shared on X, announcing that withdrawals are temporarily suspended. The closure comes just 15 months after Tether exclusively led Orionx's Series A as part of its push into Latin America.

As part of its efforts to comply with Chile's Fintech Law, Orionx reviewed its operations in 2025 and brought in financial professionals, according to Chilean newspaper La Tercera, citing the company's criminal complaint. On Aug. 27, chief operating officer Thomas Mac Millan detected a "significant mismatch" between balances recorded in Orionx's systems and assets actually held in custody. An external forensic audit that compared company records against data verifiable onchain found that recorded balances exceeded custody holdings for Bitcoin, Ether, XRP and Polygon (POL) — with assets allegedly transferred out between 2018 and 2021, including to accounts on other crypto platforms.

Orionx said it filed a criminal complaint against former executives and co-founders Roberto Zibert and Joaquín Díaz, who allegedly had access to the custody systems: an account associated with Díaz reportedly received more than $1.5 million across 14 transfers, while another wallet allegedly received 187 ETH, more than 4.1 million USDT and 200,000 USDC. Both men deny the allegations, saying they never acted against customers' interests and that the cause of the shortfall remains unclear.

Banking OCC Preliminary Approvals

Revolut and OpenReserve Get Preliminary US Bank Approval With Crypto Plans

Revolut and Andreessen Horowitz-backed crypto banking startup OpenReserve have received preliminary conditional approval from the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) to establish national banks — Revolut in Connecticut, OpenReserve in Utah — with both companies planning cryptocurrency and stablecoin-related services. The OCC published the decisions this week; neither bank can open until it meets preopening requirements and receives final approval.

Revolut told the OCC it wants its own US bank to provide services at lower cost and greater efficiency than its current model, which relies on FDIC-insured partner banks. It plans to offer digital asset custody and to let customers use crypto assets, including stablecoins, for cross-border transfers — as well as Revolut-branded stablecoins issued by a third party. Founded in 2025 by MoneyLion founder and former CEO Dee Choubey, OpenReserve is building a blockchain-based bank designed to pair traditional banking with tokenized deposits and digital asset services, plus a subsidiary that would issue US dollar-backed stablecoins — though it has not yet filed an application for that subsidiary.

The approvals are the latest sign of crypto's march into the regulated banking system: a consortium of 21 major institutions, including Bank of America, Goldman Sachs and Citi, is separately planning a stablecoin venture targeting a US dollar coin in the first half of 2027.

✦ Feature: Washington — The CLARITY Act Clears a Law-Enforcement Hurdle ✦

The National Sheriffs' Association has dropped its opposition to the CLARITY Act — shifting to "neutral" — ahead of a key Senate vote this month, removing one of the most prominent sources of law-enforcement resistance to the crypto market-structure bill.

In a letter to Senate leaders, the association cited work by Congress, the Trump administration and other stakeholders to address legal and enforcement concerns, and said it would now "step back" and allow lawmakers to establish a regulatory framework for digital assets. It is a significant turn: the sheriffs' group previously warned that provisions exempting some crypto mixers from registration requirements could make it harder for law enforcement to trace transactions and recover stolen funds. In July, one sheriff said the legislation protected "the crypto industry, not the public."

The House passed the CLARITY Act in July 2025, but the bill has since faced disputes over stablecoin rewards, tokenized equities and potential conflicts involving President Trump's family. Senate Majority Leader John Thune has filed for a cloture vote on Sept. 15 — and the SEC and CFTC have signaled they could move ahead with parts of crypto regulation even if Congress fails to pass the legislation.

Security The Third Wave Moves

Coldcard Hacker Starts Swapping Stolen Bitcoin for Ether via THORChain

The hacker behind the third wave of Coldcard wallet thefts has begun moving stolen funds onchain for the first time, swapping roughly 10% of the haul for Ether through THORChain, Galaxy head of research Alex Thorn reported this week — with 90% still untouched. Thorn said it was the first movement of funds from any of the three waves, and that the attacker appears to be struggling with the mechanics: "They keep getting refunded and he keeps retrying."

Onchain analysts traced the funds through THORChain to a new Ethereum address, which Thorn said he has shared with relevant authorities and crypto companies; it remains unclear whether the attacker will try to further obscure or move the assets via an exchange. The transfers follow the Coldcard exploit that Galaxy Research linked to the theft of at least 1,789 Bitcoin from 8,865 addresses, worth about $114.7 million at the time — while CertiK reported in August that hackers connected to the exploit had sent 64 BTC and 200 ETH to mixers such as Tornado Cash. The activity comes days after Thorn warned the attackers remained active, citing the Aug. 28 sweep of a deliberately weakened researcher wallet.

Markets Prediction-Market Boom

Kalshi's US Traffic Soars 1,500% as Regulatory Pressure Mounts

Kalshi recorded 15.4 million visits from the United States in July — up about 1,520% from under 1 million a year earlier, according to Similarweb traffic estimates — with US traffic now nearly 80% of the platform's total, up from 72.8% in August 2025. Trading volume has grown even faster: roughly $40 billion in monthly notional volume in August, up about 4,500% year over year, with Kalshi accounting for nearly 79% of the prediction-market industry's $50.7 billion monthly total.

The surge comes as Kalshi faces mounting legal challenges over whether its sports contracts fall under federal oversight or state gambling laws, with New Jersey taking the dispute to the US Supreme Court. Sports contracts accounted for 83% of Kalshi's July trading volume — and traffic from restricted jurisdictions is rising too: Canada generated about 450,000 visits in July and the UK 296,000, up from roughly 50,000 and 31,000 respectively, even though both countries' users are barred from direct access under Kalshi's member agreement. The company has partnered with Canadian firm Wealthsimple to offer eligible contracts through a separate app.

LatAm IMF Verification

IMF: El Salvador's Post-Review Bitcoin Came From Private Donations

El Salvador used no public resources to accumulate Bitcoin after the first review of its IMF financing program, the lender said this week — documents supplied by Salvadoran authorities verified the additions came from private donations, with no further accumulation beyond documented donations expected. Majority ownership and operational control of the Chivo wallet has been transferred to a private operator, with the government retaining a minority stake and custodial duties. The explanation addresses how holdings grew after the country said in November 2025 it had acquired 1,090 BTC worth $100 million; the National Bitcoin Office tracker now shows about 7,764 BTC, worth roughly $628 million.

Japan All-In on Bitcoin

Remixpoint Dumps Altcoins, Leaves 1,506 BTC as Sole Crypto Bet

Remixpoint, one of Japan's largest corporate Bitcoin holders, has sold all its Ether, Solana, XRP and Dogecoin for a combined 878.8 million yen ($5.5 million) — booking a 117.8 million yen ($736,000) gain while taking a small loss on DOGE — to concentrate its treasury purely on Bitcoin. The company now holds about 1,506 BTC ($115 million) as its only cryptocurrency position, saying the focus will "clarify investment strategy" and "improve capital efficiency." Remixpoint has also been earning yield on its stack, generating 14.92 BTC from lending between Feb. 24 and Aug. 31, valued at about $1 million.

Networks Reward Overissuance

Core DAO Plans Emergency Hard Fork After Validators Drew Excess Rewards

Core DAO is coordinating an emergency hard fork after a small number of validators accrued CORE rewards "significantly above" the protocol's intended issuance. Core says the incident has been contained, "malicious validators" can no longer draw excess rewards, and user assets remain safe — the fork will be a forward upgrade that neither rolls back the network nor reverses confirmed transactions. Exchanges moved quickly to restrict transfers: Coinbase paused sends and receives on the Core network, while Bithumb, Coinone, Bitget and LBank suspended deposits and withdrawals. Core has not disclosed how much CORE was issued, how long the activity ran, or the underlying vulnerability, though it says a technical postmortem is coming.
