Regulation Washington — The Odds

Galaxy Lowers CLARITY Act Passage Odds to 10%

Galaxy Digital has lowered its estimate of the Digital Asset Market Clarity (CLARITY) Act's chances of passing in 2026 to 10%, warning that multiple political issues remain unresolved and the Senate will have only about two to three weeks to pass it when it reconvenes on Sept. 14. Unless an initial motion to proceed vote occurs immediately upon lawmakers' return, the bill could clear the Senate only if it "dominates basically the entire working session," wrote Galaxy's head of firmwide research, Alex Thorn, in a Friday X post.

Lawmakers still must work through issues including ethics rules for government officials' involvement in crypto and pressure from banks over stablecoin yield provisions. Galaxy's estimate has slid steadily — from 75% on May 22 to 60% on June 6, 50% on June 26 and now 10%. The CLARITY Act cleared the Senate Banking Committee in May, but most Democrats and the banking industry have pushed back, arguing it would let crypto firms offer yields on stablecoins without facing the same requirements as banks.

Morgan Stanley's BlackRock Bitcoin ETF Holdings Rise 23% in Q2

Morgan Stanley reported larger crypto fund positions in the second quarter, led by an increase of more than 3 million shares in BlackRock's Bitcoin ETF. Holdings in the iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) rose 23% to around 16.5 million shares from 13.4 million, per its Q2 13F filing with the SEC — although the position's value fell about 18% to $549 million as Bitcoin declined during the quarter.

The bank also reported 2.57 million shares of its own Morgan Stanley Bitcoin Trust, worth about $43.3 million and launched in April, roughly tripled its iShares Ethereum Trust position to 4.6 million shares and added new Solana exposure. Circle Internet Group — the company behind USDC — jumped from 1.46 million to 8.32 million shares, while Coinbase and CleanSpark positions were trimmed and an roughly 8 million-share Bitfarms stake was fully exited.

Markets The Cycle Call

Bitcoin Could Bottom in October, Altcoins Are 'Basically Dead': Swan CEO

Bitcoin could bottom in October before recovering to around $130,000 ahead of the 2028 halving, according to Swan Bitcoin CEO Cory Klippsten. With BTC's peak above $126,000 in early October 2025, "the market should bottom in October," he told Cointelegraph — noting Bitcoin has historically bottomed about 12 months after each previous bull market peak, while cautioning against extrapolating from only a few cycles.

Klippsten said Bitcoin could fall to $57,000, or even $53,000, before a quick recovery. He called altcoins "basically dead" as competitors to Bitcoin as money, arguing the best outcome for crypto and DeFi is to "become part of TradFi" — pointing to Hyperliquid as a centralized business that will eventually be "sucked up by TradFi and be thought of as an exchange and a bank."

L1s SIMD-0553 — The Fee Overhaul

Solana's Fee Overhaul Increases Burn and Makes Resource Hogs Pay

Solana Improvement Document SIMD-0553 would make the network's most resource-intensive users pay more while cutting costs for simpler transactions — and would increase the amount of SOL burned, one day possibly even helping make the token deflationary. Author Cavey, a researcher at infrastructure firm Temporal, argues fees currently don't reflect real costs: "If I submit a transaction that does nothing versus a transaction that burns 200 million CPU cycles, I'm charged the same amount."

The proposal ties fees to the resources each transaction requests and burns the resource fee rather than paying it to validators — a change not welcomed by all, with contributor bji arguing validator incomes "should not be arbitrarily reduced." Under the proposed model, stablecoin and token transfers could become roughly 20% cheaper, while the daily SOL burn could rise from around 648 SOL to 7,500–9,000 SOL.

✦ Feature: The $1 Million Question ✦

Bitcoin to $1 million by 2030 is "mathematically impossible," says Markus Thielen, head of research at 10x Research. The numbers, he argues, simply don't add up: with a market cap around $1.28 trillion, Bitcoin would need to attract another $15 trillion in capital — roughly 25% of the US stock market's total value — over the next four years. "It takes trillions and trillions of dollars to move the price really materially higher, and that's why we are not as bullish."

Thielen adds a psychological wrinkle: the higher Bitcoin climbs, the weaker retail sentiment becomes, because "people don't want to buy a tenth or a hundredth of a Bitcoin; they want to buy a whole Bitcoin." He warns against expecting a swift return to the $126,000 all-time high — "If we go back to, let's say, $100K, that would already be a big, big achievement" — and dismisses the round-number $1 million forecasts of figures like Brian Armstrong, Jack Dorsey and Cathie Wood as an easy way to generate media attention that ultimately "tend[s] to hurt retail investors." To the question of whether Bitcoin could ever reach $1 million: "I don't want to say never, but a million is really a high number."

AML Dublin — The Strategy

Ireland Plans Industry Standards for Illicit Crypto Use

Ireland published its first National Anti-Money Laundering strategy on Thursday, with final elements introducing new AML obligations for crypto-asset service providers: enhanced checks on transfers involving private crypto wallets and stricter due diligence when dealing with overseas crypto firms. The document also addresses industry standards "relating to the acceptance of crypto-related activities" as a source of funds for gambling, building on the EU's MiCA framework.
