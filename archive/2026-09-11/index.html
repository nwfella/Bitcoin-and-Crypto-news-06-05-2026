★ The Liquid Affair Turns Bitter ★

Blockstream Refuses Ransom as Hackers Still Hold 598 Bitcoin

"Taking assets without authorization and withholding their return is a crime, not responsible disclosure," the Bitcoin infrastructure firm declared — rejecting a demand for a 10% bounty drawn from its own treasury, and warning that Liquid holders would otherwise absorb a 15% loss.

By The Daily Cryptograph Staff | September 11, 2026

The standoff over the Liquid Network continued into the week's end with Blockstream flatly refusing to pay a ransom to recover funds still held by those who raided the Bitcoin sidechain. "Taking assets without authorization and withholding their return is a crime, not responsible disclosure," the company said. "It is not white-hat activity. It is theft." The firm added that it had engaged with the attackers in good faith to recover user funds, but would not accede to their terms.

The demands were delivered onchain and circulated by Jan3 chief executive and former Blockstream strategy chief Samson Mow. The hackers asked Blockstream to pay a 10% bounty out of its own funds, warning that Liquid holders would otherwise face a 15% loss. Blockstream urged the actors to return the remaining Bitcoin voluntarily, and said that failing that it would work with law enforcement, exchanges, service providers and forensic specialists to trace the assets and identify those responsible.

The account of the exploit has hardened as the week wore on. On Sept. 6, Liquid paused operations after self-described white hats withdrew roughly 4,000 Bitcoin — about $320 million at the time — from its federation wallet. The actors later returned 3,400 BTC once Blockstream confirmed the affected bridge nodes had been patched, leaving about 598 BTC outstanding. Liquid resumed block production on Thursday, minting empty blocks after emergency software updates, though transactions and Bitcoin transfers into and out of the network remain suspended. The sidechain's full re-opening now rests on whether the remaining coin comes home — or on the trace.

Consensys to Cleave Itself in Two, Sending MetaMask Forth Alone

Industry September 11, 2026

Consensys Software Inc., the Ethereum firm behind MetaMask, will split into two independent companies, separating its consumer wallet business from its institutional blockchain infrastructure operations. The separation is expected to close by the end of 2026, with Joe Lubin serving as chairman and chief executive of MetaMask and executive chairman of the new Consensys.

The reconstituted Consensys will hold the protocols and institutional infrastructure businesses — Linea, Besu and Teku — under CEO Mike Kriak and President David Cunningham, and will court financial institutions deploying tokenization, stablecoins and other onchain services. MetaMask will remain focused on consumer self-custody while pushing into payments, savings, investing and traditional financial products. The wallet reports more than 100 million downloads across roughly 190 countries. Both businesses, the company said, simply wanted different things.

Cronos Confesses $9.2M Escaped Before Its Rollback Landed

Security September 11, 2026

The Layer-1 blockchain Cronos has published the official accounting of the Tectonic exploit: $9.19 million left the chain before validators halted the network, money that its rollback could not claw back. Manipulated collateral values generated about $120.4 million in borrowing activity, of which the restore-to-pre-exploit-state reversed roughly $111.2 million — leaving 7.6% of affected funds beyond reach.

The disclosure confirms a larger incident than first believed, after earlier estimates put the damage near $75 million. Bitquery's trace found the attacker deposited $5 million and looped TONIC borrows and redeposits 98 times while buying the thinly traded token, driving its price almost 300-fold higher as Tectonic's feed followed. Detection came at 12:49 UTC on Aug. 30; validators stopped the chain at 14:32:47 and resumed block production at 23:49:01.

Bessent Urges the Senate: Pass CLARITY, Or Signal Retreat

Policy September 11, 2026

US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent pressed lawmakers to pass the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act as the Senate returns from its August recess next week. "I strongly urge everyone to remain at the negotiating table, agree to the motion to proceed, and continue the legislative process," Bessent wrote, warning that inaction would send a "troubling signal" about America's leadership in digital assets.

The bill, intended as the first comprehensive US regulatory framework for digital assets, cleared the Senate Banking Committee in May but has stalled amid opposition from most Democrats and the banking lobby, which argues it would let crypto firms offer stablecoin yields without bank-grade requirements. The National Sheriffs' Association moved to neutral on Sept. 3, yet Galaxy's estimate of 2026 passage has collapsed to 10%, down from 75% in May.

ESMA Warns the Crypto River Is Now Wired Into Finance's Reservoir

Markets September 11, 2026

Europe's securities regulator has warned that deepening ties between crypto and traditional finance could let shocks spread through the wider financial system. In its latest risk monitoring report, the European Securities and Markets Authority called for closer monitoring of the "growing linkage between increasingly vulnerable crypto-asset markets and the broader financial system."

ESMA singled out the growing adoption of tokenized equities and recent DeFi exploits as channels that could deepen those links and amplify spillovers. It also flagged prediction markets as an emerging risk, warning that crypto rails can make insider trading and wash trading harder to detect — a pointed note for a sector that has drawn record volumes this year.

✦ Berlin Moves to Tax the Patient Hodler ✦

The German Federal Ministry of Finance has issued a draft proposal to move cryptocurrency trading profits onto the standard 25% flat-rate tax beginning in 2028, ending the country's celebrated one-year exemption. Under current law, gains on crypto held longer than twelve months are entirely tax-free — a provision that has made Germany a favoured domicile for long-term holders. The draft would apply to assets acquired after Jan. 1, 2027, and reportedly carries grandfathering protections so that coin bought before the deadline may still be treated under the old rules. Finance Minister Lars Klingbeil first signalled the overhaul at the end of April, and Berlin expects an additional €2 billion (about $2.3 billion) in revenue from crypto taxation. If enacted, Europe's largest economy would trade its friendliest feature for its most conventional one.

India Issues $107M in Bonds Natively Onchain

Tokenization

India's SEBI and central bank launched a tokenized corporate bond pilot under Demat 2.0, with REC, Larsen & Toubro and IIFL issuing a combined 10.25 billion rupees (about $107 million). Bonds are issued and held as digital tokens on depositories' distributed ledger, settling against the RBI's wholesale CBDC. SEBI says issuers now receive funds on bidding day instead of two to three days later, and calls it the first such combination of native onchain issuance, statutory depositories and CBDC settlement in regulated market infrastructure.

Lords Vote 194–138 to Demand a Digital Asset Strategy

Policy

The UK House of Lords backed Amendment 88 to the Financial Services and Markets Bill by 194 to 138, requiring the Treasury to prepare, publish and consult on a digital asset strategy within 12 months of the bill becoming law. The strategy would cover cryptoassets, stablecoins and tokenized securities alongside innovation, consumer protection and firms' access to banking and settlement. The Labour government opposed the measure, arguing it already has a strategy in execution; the bill must still return to the Commons.
