Galaxy Identifies at Least 15 Attackers
Behind the $100M Coldcard Exploit

New victim reports unmask previously unknown actors — including one that siphoned 12 BTC from 126 addresses — as estimated losses climb toward $130 million across four attack waves and the industry debates whether AI could have stopped it

By Zoltan Vardai & The Daily Cryptograph Staff | August 4, 2026

Continue Reading on Cointelegraph →

☠ Coldcard Attack — Losses Top $100M, Four Waves, 15+ Attackers
  • Galaxy Digital has identified at least 15 attackers behind the Coldcard wallet exploit, with estimated losses now reaching $100 million and potentially climbing to $130 million across four attack waves.
  • Alex Thorn's finding: new victim reports uncovered previously unknown actors — one allegedly siphoned 12 BTC from 126 addresses after a victim reported losing less than 1 BTC. The decentralized nature of the exploit made it harder to trace every actor.
  • The AI debate rages: Dragonfly's Haseeb Qureshi argues "about $2 of AI hardening" could have prevented the exploit, while Tokenomist's Tatsapat Saerejittima cautions the claims lack rigorous testing. Castle Labs' Francesco says AI is rapidly lowering the cost of discovering software bugs.
  • Assume exposure if your seed was generated on a Coldcard (Mk2/Mk3/Mk4/Mk5/Q) after March 2021 — unless you added at least 50 independent, private dice rolls or a strong, unique BIP-39 passphrase.
  • Updating firmware does NOT repair existing seeds. Migrate funds to a freshly generated seed — calmly, with a test transaction first.

Bitcoin Coils at $64K as Hormuz Reopening Hopes Send S&P 500 to Record $70T

Bitcoin built on $64,000 into Tuesday's Wall Street open as hopes of the Strait of Hormuz reopening to oil traffic sent the S&P 500 index to a record $70 trillion market cap — a new high of 7,713. Oil prices dropped 4.6–4.8% to their lowest since July 13 after Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC there was "a chance we may have a deal today or tomorrow to open the Strait," following President Trump's confirmation that the waterway could reopen "as soon as tomorrow."

BTC/USD climbed to $64,176 on Bitstamp, its highest August level, though the pair remains squeezed between its 21-day SMA at $64,388 and the 50-day SMA acting as support. Onchain analytics from CryptoQuant report "strong accumulation" — 0.7% of the BTC supply, roughly 155,000 coins, now belongs to investors with a cost basis between $62,000 and $65,000. "This points to absorption rather than capitulation, as buyers accumulated into weakness."

Markets now see 56.7% odds of a 0.25% rate hike at September's FOMC per CME FedWatch, amid an emerging hawkish split among Fed officials.

Regulation US–UK Working Group

US, UK Reaffirm Support for Stablecoins, Tokenization in Joint Regulation Talks

Washington and London reaffirmed their commitment to closer financial regulatory cooperation at the 13th meeting of the UK-US Financial Regulatory Working Group, held in London on July 8. A joint statement issued Monday said US officials updated their UK counterparts on implementation of the GENIUS Act — the landmark stablecoin law — as well as ongoing work on digital asset market structure, payment modernization and the G20 Cross-border Payments Roadmap.

The UK's renewed emphasis on stablecoins comes as observers argue the country is losing ground to the US. The Bank of England has softened its stance, reviewing whether its proposal requiring at least 40% of reserve assets be held as non-interest-bearing deposits at the central bank is too restrictive. Separately, the FCA said cross-border payments represent one of the "clearest near-term use cases for stablecoins."

The commitment was echoed on July 14, when the Transatlantic Taskforce for Markets of the Future published initial recommendations alongside a joint statement on stablecoins, laying a foundation for continued US-UK leadership in digital assets.

Security AI-Assisted Attacks

Boltz Pauses Service After Wave of AI-Assisted Hacking Attempts

Boltz, the non-custodial Bitcoin swap service, says it is disabling its service until further notice after seeing a steady increase in "automated AI-assisted probing" of its infrastructure this year. In a post to X on Monday, the team said it is being "actively targeted by what appear to be multiple resourceful groups while we race to deploy fixes."

"After reviewing the results of our own recent security scans, we cannot responsibly re-enable Boltz swaps," the team said. "In the past few days alone we saw a drastic acceleration [of attacks] and we do not believe this asymmetry will reverse."

The pause highlights the difficulty smaller development teams face as attackers discover vulnerabilities and adapt exploits faster than defenders can respond.

✦ Analysis: Custody Meets Yield — BNY's Institutional Staking Debut ✦

BNY and Galaxy have partnered to integrate institutional crypto staking into BNY's digital asset custody platform, allowing eligible clients to earn staking rewards without moving assets out of custody. Galaxy will provide the staking infrastructure and serve as a design partner for BNY's broader digital asset platform, with custody, staking, reporting and tax services combined into a single offering.

The move expands BNY's push to integrate digital assets into traditional finance: the bank was among the first major institutions to offer regulated custody for Bitcoin and Ether, and has since broadened its strategy through tokenized fund administration and efforts to move transfer agency records onchain. For institutions still wary of staking risk, keeping assets inside a regulated custody framework could prove the missing bridge — and a meaningful endorsement of proof-of-stake yield as a legitimate institutional product.
